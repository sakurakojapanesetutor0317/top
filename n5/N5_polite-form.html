01 Why Do We Use ます? — Polite Speech

Japanese has different speaking styles depending on your relationship with the listener. ます is the standard polite verb ending used in everyday conversations with people you are not especially close to.

Polite style · 丁寧体ていねいたい

食たべます。
I eat. / I will eat.

A safe default with teachers, customers, coworkers you are not close to, strangers, and people you have just met.

Plain style · 普通体ふつうたい

食たべる。
I eat. / I will eat.

Common with close friends, family, and in casual settings. It is not automatically rude; appropriateness depends on the relationship and situation.

Polite language is not the same as honorific language

ます is 丁寧語ていねいご（ていねいご, polite language）. It makes your sentence polite toward the listener, but it does not by itself show special respect toward the person performing the action.

尊敬語そんけいご（そんけいご, respectful language） elevates the person you are talking about; 謙譲語けんじょうご（けんじょうご, humble language） humbles your own or your group's actions. These are also parts of the broader category 敬語けいご（けいご）. So 丁寧語ていねいご is technically a type of 敬語けいご, but ます alone is not 尊敬語そんけいご or 謙譲語けんじょうご.

先生せんせいが 来きます。 → 先生せんせいが いらっしゃいます。The teacher is coming. (polite) → The teacher is coming. (respectful)

Tip: When you are unsure how casually to speak, starting with です・ます is generally a good choice. You can switch to plain style as your relationship becomes closer.

02 Four Basic Polite Forms

The ending shows whether the action is affirmative or negative and whether it is past or non-past. Learn these four endings once; you can use them with many verbs.

MeaningEndingExample: 食たべる (eat)
Non-past affirmativeます食たべます — eat / will eat
Non-past negativeません食たべません — don't / won't eat
Past affirmativeました食たべました — ate
Past negativeませんでした食たべませんでした — didn't eat
Present or future?

Japanese does not have a separate future ending. ます and ません can refer to routines or future events. Time words and context tell you which.

毎日まいにち 勉強べんきょうします。 / 明日あした 勉強べんきょうします。I study every day. / I will study tomorrow.

03 How to Make the ます Form

Japanese verbs are commonly divided into three groups. The group determines how to change a dictionary-form verb into its ます form. After that, replace ます with the other endings in Section 02.

Group 1 · U-verbs

Change the final u sound to its corresponding i sound + ます.

書かく → 書かきます
飲のむ → 飲のみます
話はなす → 話はなします

Group 2 · Ru-verbs

Remove the final る + ます.

食たべる → 食たべます
見みる → 見みます
起おきる → 起おきます

Group 3 · Irregular

する → します
来くる → 来きます
勉強べんきょうする → 勉強べんきょうします

Important exception

Not every verb ending in る belongs to Group 2.

帰かえる → 帰かえります
入はいる → 入はいります

These are Group 1 verbs.

04 Verbs Commonly Used with に

Some verbs often use に to mark a destination, the person involved, a location, or the result of a change. It is useful to learn the whole pattern, not just the verb.

These are common patterns, not a rule that these verbs always take に. The particle depends on what you mean. For a full explanation of に and で, see the existing particle lesson.

Compare に and other particles

友達ともだちに 会あいます (meet a friend), but 友達ともだちと 話はなします (talk with a friend). 学校がっこうに 行いきます (go to school), but 学校がっこうで 勉強べんきょうします (study at school).

The same verb can appear with different particles when the meaning changes. For example, 先生せんせいに 聞ききます means “ask the teacher,” while 音楽おんがくを 聞ききます means “listen to music.”

05 Polite Questions & Examples

Add か to the end of a polite sentence to make a question. The verb ending stays the same.

毎日まいにち 日本語にほんごを 勉強べんきょうしますか。
Do you study Japanese every day?
昨日きのう 学校がっこうに 行いきましたか。
Did you go to school yesterday?
はい、行いきました。
Yes, I did.
いいえ、行いきませんでした。
No, I didn't.
