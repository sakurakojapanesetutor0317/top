Personal Pronouns · I / My / Me / Mine

Understand how Japanese refers to people through words, particles, names, and context.

N5People · PossessionParticles · Context

01Start Here — Japanese Does Not Change “I” into “Me”

English changes pronoun forms according to their role: I / my / me / mine. Japanese usually keeps the same self-reference, such as わたし, and adds particles to show its role.

English Japanese example Function
I わたしは / わたしが Topic / subject, depending on the sentence
my book わたしの本ほん Owner + の + noun
me わたしを / わたしに Object, recipient, or another role required by the verb
mine わたしの Owner + の; the item is understood
These are examples, not automatic word-for-word rules

I does not always mean わたしは, and me does not always mean わたしを. Choose the particle from the Japanese sentence. The name or pronoun may also be left out when the person is obvious.

02See Each Role in a Sentence

わたしは 学生がくせいです。
I am a student. — は marks the topic.
これは わたしの本ほんです。
This is my book. — の connects the owner to the noun.
先生せんせいは わたしを 呼よびました。
The teacher called me. — を marks the person called.
先生せんせいは わたしに メールを 送おくりました。
The teacher sent me an email. — に marks the recipient.
この本ほんは わたしのです。
This book is mine. — the repeated noun is understood.
A：だれが 行いきますか。
B：わたしが 行いきます。
A: Who is going?
B: I am going. — が identifies the person.
The same principle applies to names

アンさんは / アンさんが / アンさんの / アンさんを / アンさんに

A name can fill these roles too. Japanese does not require a pronoun simply because English would use “he,” “she,” or “you.”

03Who Are We Referring To?

The following words are useful to recognize. They are not a list of forms to repeat in every conversation. Names, titles, and omission are often more natural.

English Japanese starting point Important context
I / me わたし A practical starting point for polite self-reference.
you あなた Not the default in every question. Prefer a known name or title, or omit it.
he / him 彼かれ Refers to a male person, but can also mean a boyfriend in context.
she / her 彼女かのじょ Refers to a female person, but can also mean a girlfriend in context.
that person あの人ひと / その人ひと Useful when a person is identified by the situation or preceding conversation.
we / us わたしたち The speaker and an associated group.
they / them 彼かれら / 彼女かのじょたち / あの人ひとたち Choose wording appropriate to the group; names or a group description may be clearer.
Avoid importing English pronoun habits

English often replaces a repeated name with “he” or “she.” Japanese often repeats the name when needed, or leaves the person unspoken. 彼かれ / 彼女かのじょ are not universal equivalents you must insert.

その人ひと can refer to a person just mentioned, not only someone physically near the listener. あの方かた / その方かた are more respectful expressions than あの人ひと / その人ひと.

04My / Your / Their — Add の

Use person + の + noun for possession. The pronoun itself does not change into a special possessive form.

English Japanese example
my bag わたしのかばん
your book アンさんの本ほん (when speaking to Ann)
his book 彼かれの本ほん
her book 彼女かのじょの本ほん
our teacher わたしたちの先生せんせい
their car あの人ひとたちの車くるま
You can use a name instead of “your”

アンさんの本ほんですか。 — “Is it your book, Ann?”

あなたの本ほんですか is grammatically possible. A known name, an appropriate title, or context is often the more natural choice in conversation.

05Mine / Yours / Hers — Leave Out the Repeated Noun

When the object is clear, person + の can stand for the owned item. This is how Japanese commonly expresses English “mine,” “yours,” “his,” or “hers.”

A：この本ほんは だれのですか。
B：わたしのです。
A: Whose is this book?
B: It is mine.
これは アンさんのですか。
Is this yours, Ann?
これは 彼女かのじょのです。
This is hers.
わたしのかばんは 黒くろいです。アンさんのは 白しろいです。
My bag is black. Ann’s is white. — the second かばん is understood.
Do not delete の

✓ この本ほんは わたしのです。 — This book is mine.
✗ この本ほんは わたしです。 — This identifies the book as me; it does not express ownership.

Only omit the noun when the listener can identify it. Not every noun + の relationship allows the same omission naturally.

06“Me” Depends on the Verb and Particle

English “me” can correspond to several Japanese patterns. Learn the whole expression, not “me = を.”

English phrase Japanese phrase Particle’s role
call me わたしを呼よびます Person directly affected by 呼よぶ
give / send to me わたしに送おくります Recipient
talk with me わたしと話はなします Partner
receive from me わたしからもらいます Source
my book わたしの本ほん Ownership
を and に are not interchangeable

先生せんせいが わたしを呼よびました。 — The teacher called me.
先生せんせいが わたしに本ほんをくれました。 — The teacher gave me a book.

The Japanese verb determines which role needs which particle. Later lessons teach those verb patterns in detail; you do not need to master every verb here.

07How We Say “I” — Identity and Context

Self-reference can express formality, closeness, regional identity, community membership, and personal style. The same person may choose different forms at work and with close friends. These choices can be part of self-expression, but they do not determine someone’s gender or personality.

Form Typical use / impression
わたし Broadly useful in polite speech across genders; also common in casual speech, especially among women.
わたくし More formal; common in business, speeches, and formal introductions.
ぼく Often associated with boys and men; sometimes chosen by other speakers too.
おれ Strongly informal, often associated with men; common for some speakers among close friends.
うち A self-reference used in some regions and communities; not a universal substitute for わたし.
自分じぶん Used as self-reference in some settings; in other constructions it means “oneself.”
Start with わたし; observe before choosing another style

You do not need a special self-reference immediately. わたし is a useful beginner starting point for polite conversation. Listen to real people in comparable situations, rather than copying only a fictional character.

08Plural Groups — たち Is Not an English-Style Plural Ending

たち can indicate a person and an associated group: わたしたち, 子こどもたち, アンさんたち. It is not a general ending to attach to every noun.

わたしたちは 学生がくせいです。
We are students.
アンさんたちは 学校がっこうに います。
Ann and the others are at school. — not several people all named Ann.
English “they” is not always a personal pronoun

English “they” can refer to objects or animals too. Do not automatically use 彼かれら for things. Use the noun, an expression such as それら when appropriate, or leave the topic understood.

Japanese does not require a plural marker every time there is more than one person or thing. 学生がくせいです can describe a person or a group when the context is clear.

09Names, Titles, and Omission in Conversation

Once the person is clear, you often do not need an explicit pronoun. When you do need to identify someone, a name or title may be more natural.

A：学生がくせいですか。
B：はい、学生がくせいです。
A: Are you a student?
B: Yes, I am. — no あなた or わたし is needed.
A：アンさんは どこですか。
B：図書館としょかんです。
A: Where is Ann?
B: At the library. — the answer leaves Ann understood.
Do not replace every English “you” with あなた

Use アンさん for Ann or 先生せんせい when addressing your teacher, and omit the person when obvious. あなた is not forbidden, but it may sound distant or pointed depending on context.

Directly addressing a person as 彼かれ / 彼女かのじょ / あの人ひと is not the same as calling them by their name. Keep the difference between talking to someone and talking about someone clear.
