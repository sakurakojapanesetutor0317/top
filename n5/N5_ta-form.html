Verb た-Form

Plain Past · The た-Form

Learn the plain past form, why it is essential in everyday Japanese, and how it connects to many grammar patterns.

N5Verbsた-form · Plain Past

01 What Is the た-Form?

The た-form is the plain past affirmative form of a verb. It is often called the “past tense,” but it is also the building block for many other grammar patterns.

食たべます → 食たべました (polite past) → 食たべた (plain past)
Why it matters

Japanese speakers use plain forms constantly: in casual conversation, when quoting thoughts, and inside longer sentences. Plain does not mean “rude”; whether it is appropriate as a sentence ending depends on whom you are speaking to.

昨日きのう、映画えいがを見みた。
I watched a movie yesterday. (casual)
昨日きのう、映画えいがを見みました。
I watched a movie yesterday. (polite)

02 How to Make the た-Form

Start with the ます-form to make the た-form. Group 1 changes depend on the sound before ます; Group 2 removes ます and adds た; Group 3 is irregular. The た-form uses the same sound-change patterns as the て-form, but ends in た／だ instead of て／で. Exception: 行いきます → 行いった.

Group 1 (う-verbs)

Start with the ます-form. For Group 1, the sound immediately before ます determines the change: います・ちます・ります → った / みます・びます・にます → んだ / きます → いた / ぎます → いだ / します → した. Remove ます and apply the corresponding change.

買かいます → 買かった
buy → bought
待まちます → 待まった
wait → waited
Group 2 (る-verbs)

Remove ます and add た.

食たべます → 食たべた
eat → ate
見みます → 見みた
see → saw
Group 3 (irregular verbs)
します → した
do → did
来きます → 来きた
come → came

Compare with the て-form lesson to learn both forms together.

03 Plain Form vs Polite Form

The same event can be expressed politely or casually. In a sentence ending, choose the style according to your relationship with the listener. Inside a sentence, however, a plain form is often required even when the overall sentence is polite.

昨日きのう、友達ともだちに会あった。
I met a friend yesterday. (casual)
昨日きのう、友達ともだちに会あいました。
I met a friend yesterday. (polite)
昨日きのう買かった本ほんです。
This is the book I bought yesterday. (plain form modifying a noun + polite ending)
Important

The plain past negative is 〜なかった, not 〜た. For example: 食たべなかった (“did not eat”). See the future ない-form lesson for negative conjugation.

04 Talking About Completed Events

The most familiar use of た is to report a past action or completed event. Context and time expressions clarify when it happened.

朝あさごはんを食たべた。
I ate breakfast.
宿題しゅくだいが終おわった。
I finished my homework.
もう昼ひるごはんを食たべた？
Have you eaten lunch yet? (casual)

05 〜たことがある — Past Experience

Verb た-form + ことがある describes an experience someone has had at least once. It does not simply mean that an action happened yesterday.

Verb た + ことがある / ことがあります
日本にほんに行いったことがあります。
I have been to Japan.
納豆なっとうを食たべたことがありません。
I have never eaten natto.

06 〜たり〜たりする — Giving Examples

Verb た-form + り lists representative activities. It does not mean those actions must be in the past: the final する / します / した determines the tense of the whole sentence.

Verb た + り、Verb た + り + する
週末しゅうまつは映画えいがを見みたり、買かい物ものをしたりします。
On weekends, I do things like watch movies and go shopping.
昨日きのうは掃除そうじをしたり、料理りょうりをしたりしました。
Yesterday I did things like cleaning and cooking.

07 〜た後で — After Doing Something

Verb た-form + 後あとで means “after doing.” Here, the た-form does not mean that the whole sentence must describe the past.

食たべた後あとで、歯はを磨みがきます。
I brush my teeth after eating.
仕事しごとが終おわった後あとで、電話でんわします。
I will call after work finishes.

08 〜たら — If / When

Verb た-form + ら makes the conditional 〜たら. It can describe a condition or an event that must happen before the next action, including future situations.

時間じかんがあったら、映画えいがを見みます。
If I have time, I will watch a movie.
家いえに帰かえったら、連絡れんらくしてください。
Please contact me when you get home.

Related detailed lesson: 〜たら (Coming soon).

09 〜た方がいい — Advice

Verb た-form + 方ほうがいい is commonly used to recommend an action. Even though it uses た, the advice usually concerns the present or future.

早はやく寝ねた方ほうがいいですよ。
You should go to bed early.
病院びょういんに行いった方ほうがいいです。
You should see a doctor.

10 〜たとき / 〜たばかり

〜たとき relates an action to a time when something had already happened or was completed. Compare it with 〜るとき, which often points to a time before the action is completed.

日本にほんに行いくとき、カメラを買かいました。
I bought a camera when I was going to Japan (before going).
日本にほんに行いったとき、カメラを買かいました。
I bought a camera when I went to Japan (during that visit).

〜たばかり means “just did.” The feeling of recency can be subjective.

今いま帰かえったばかりです。
I just got home.
日本語にほんごの勉強べんきょうを始はじめたばかりです。
I have only just started studying Japanese.
