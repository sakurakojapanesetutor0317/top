N4 GRAMMAR · VERB CONJUGATION

Passive Form 受身形うけみけい

Learn how to say that something is done to someone or something.

Form passive verbs from the ます-form, understand who does the action, and distinguish passive from potential meanings.

N4VerbsPassive form · 受身形

01 What Is the Passive Form?

The passive form focuses on the person or thing receiving an action. English often uses “be + past participle,” but Japanese passive sentences can also express that an action affected or inconvenienced someone.

先生せんせいにほめられました。
I was praised by my teacher.
友達ともだちにケーキを食たべられました。
My friend ate my cake (and I was affected by it).

The passive is not automatically negative. It can describe praise, neutral events, or an unwelcome experience.

02 How to Make the Passive — Start with ます

Start with the ます-form, following the same approach as the other conjugation pages.

Group 1 — Change the sound before ます to its あ-row sound + れます

Remove ます, change the final い-row sound to the corresponding あ-row sound, and add れます. For verbs ending in います, use われます (い→わ).

書かきます → 書かかれます
be written
読よみます → 読よまれます
be read

Group 2 — Remove ます and add られます

食たべます → 食たべられます
be eaten
見みます → 見みられます
be seen / watched

Group 3 — Irregular verbs

します → されます
be done
来きます → 来こられます
someone comes (passive); be affected by someone coming

Additional Group 1 example: 買かいます → 買かわれます. Remember the reading change: 来きます → 来こられます.

03 Polite and Plain Passive Forms

The passive verb can be conjugated for politeness, tense, and negation, just like other verbs.

読よまれます。
is read (polite)
読よまれる。
is read (plain)
読よまれません。 / 読よまれない。
is not read (polite / plain)
読よまれました。 / 読よまれた。
was read (polite / plain)

The passive ending is part of the verb. You can attach other grammar to it, for example 〜たら or 〜かもしれません.

04 Who Does the Action? — に

In a passive sentence, は can mark the topic and が can mark the subject (the person or thing receiving the action). に often marks the person who performs the action. Compare the particles in the active and passive sentences below.

先生せんせいが学生がくせいをほめました。
The teacher praised the student. (active)
学生がくせいは先生せんせいにほめられました。
The student was praised by the teacher. (passive)

What changes? The student, previously marked by を, becomes the topic marked by は. The teacher, previously marked by が, becomes the doer marked by に. This is a common pattern, not a rule that every を automatically changes to は or が.

In an indirect passive, the affected person becomes the topic, but the object can still take を:

弟おとうとが私わたしのケーキを食たべました。
My younger brother ate my cake. (active)
私わたしは弟おとうとにケーキを食たべられました。
My younger brother ate my cake, and I was negatively affected. (indirect passive)

Other ways to mark the doer or source: によって is often used for the creator or agent in descriptive or formal passive sentences. から can indicate the source with certain verbs, such as 招待する (to invite). These are not interchangeable with に in every sentence.

この本ほんは有名ゆうめいな作家さっかによって書かかれました。
This book was written by a famous author.
私わたしは友達ともだちからパーティーに招待しょうたいされました。
I was invited to a party by a friend.

05 Direct Passive — An Action Done to Someone or Something

The subject directly receives the action. This is the closest match to many English passive sentences.

この本ほんはたくさんの人ひとに読よまれています。
This book is read by many people.
私わたしは先生せんせいに名前なまえを呼よばれました。
The teacher called my name. (I was the one affected.)
この家いえは100年ねん前まえに建たてられました。
This house was built 100 years ago.

Passive sentences are common in explanations, news, and descriptions of things, but Japanese does not always use passive where English does.

06 Indirect / Affected Passive — Something Happened to Me

Japanese can use the passive when an action involving someone else affects the speaker. The speaker may not be the direct object of the action.

弟おとうとにケーキを食たべられました。
My younger brother ate my cake (to my annoyance).
友達ともだちに日記にっきを読よまれました。
My friend read my diary (and I was upset).
赤あかちゃんに泣なかれて、眠ねむれませんでした。
The baby cried, and I could not sleep.

This is often called the adversative passive. It frequently suggests inconvenience, but the precise feeling depends on context.

07 Passive Form vs. Potential Form

Group 2 verbs and 来る have identical-looking passive and potential forms. Group 1 verbs have different endings. Context and particles help you identify the meaning.

魚さかなが食たべられます。
I can eat fish. (potential; context-dependent)
魚さかなが猫ねこに食たべられました。
The fish was eaten by a cat. (passive)
日本語にほんごが話はなせます。
I can speak Japanese. (Group 1 potential)
日本語にほんごが話はなされます。
Japanese is spoken. (Group 1 passive)

見みられる can mean “can see/watch” or “be seen/watched.” Compare the Potential Form lesson for ability and ら-dropping.

08 Passive vs. 〜てもらう / 〜てくれる

When someone does something helpful for you, Japanese often uses 〜てもらう or 〜てくれる rather than the passive. The passive mainly describes receiving an action, not necessarily receiving a favor.

先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを直なおされました。
My teacher corrected my essay. (passive; may sound affected)
先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを直なおしてもらいました。
I had my teacher correct my essay. (received help)
友達ともだちが手伝てつだってくれました。
My friend helped me. (favor)

Choosing a passive sentence or a giving/receiving expression changes the viewpoint and nuance.

09 Passive in Everyday Descriptions

The passive is useful for describing products, traditions, buildings, and general facts without emphasizing who performs the action.

この車くるまは日本にほんで作つくられています。
This car is made in Japan.
この漢字かんじはよく使つかわれます。
This kanji is often used.
日本にほんではお正月しょうがつにおせちが食たべられます。
In Japan, osechi is eaten at New Year.

In descriptive sentences, 〜られている / 〜われている often expresses an ongoing state or a general practice.

10 Related Patterns with Passive Verbs

Once you know the passive, you can combine it with negative, past, and conditional forms.

先生せんせいに怒おこられたくないです。
I do not want to be scolded by my teacher.
名前なまえを呼よばれたら、返事へんじしてください。
If your name is called, please answer.
この言葉ことばはあまり使つかわれません。
This word is not used very often.

Passive forms also appear in longer grammar patterns. You do not need to memorize every combination at once.
