Verb ない-Form

Plain Negative · The ない-Form

Make negative verbs from the ます-form, and see why the plain negative is essential for everyday grammar.

N5Verbsない-form · Plain Negative

01 What Is the ない-Form?

The ない-form is the plain negative form of a verb. It means that an action does not happen, or that someone does not do something. Compare the polite negative 〜ません with the plain negative 〜ない.

食たべます → 食たべません → 食たべない
Why is the plain negative so common?

Plain forms are common in conversations with friends and family. More importantly, many grammar patterns require the ない-form even when the whole sentence is polite. For example, 〜ないでください and 〜なければなりません are polite expressions built using a plain negative verb.

今日きょうは行いかない。
I'm not going today. (casual)
今日きょうは行いきません。
I'm not going today. (polite)

02 How to Make the ない-Form

Always start with the ます-form. The rules depend on the verb group. The examples below use two verbs per group.

Group 1 — change the sound before ます

Remove ます. Change the final い-row sound to the matching あ-row sound, then add ない: き→か, ぎ→が, し→さ, ち→た, に→な, び→ば, み→ま, り→ら. For verbs ending in います, use わない (not あない).

書かきます → 書かかない
write → do not write (き → か)
買かいます → 買かわない
buy → do not buy (います → わない)
Group 2 — remove ます and add ない

The verb stem stays the same.

食たべます → 食たべない
eat → do not eat
見みます → 見みない
see → do not see
Group 3 — irregular verbs

Memorize these two patterns.

します → しない
do → do not do
来きます → 来こない
come → do not come (the reading changes from き to こ)
Important exception: あります

The plain negative of あります is ない, not あらない.

時間じかんがありません → 時間じかんがない

03 Plain Negative vs. Polite Negative

At the end of a sentence, use 〜ません for polite speech and 〜ない for casual speech. However, using a plain form inside a polite sentence is often grammatically necessary.

明日あしたは働はたらきません。
I won't work tomorrow. (polite)
明日あしたは働はたらかない。
I won't work tomorrow. (casual)
明日あしたは働はたらかないと思おもいます。
I think I won't work tomorrow. (plain form inside a polite sentence)
今日きょうは学校がっこうに行いかない？
Aren't you going to school today? (negative question)
一緒いっしょに映画えいがを見みない？
Want to watch a movie together? (casual invitation)
一緒いっしょに映画えいがを見みませんか。
Would you like to watch a movie together? (polite invitation)
Casual questions and invitations — 〜ない？

In casual speech, the ない-form can be used with rising intonation to ask a negative question or to make a friendly invitation. The same ending can have different meanings depending on the context and tone.

Negative question: “Aren’t you going?” / “Don’t you eat it?” — the speaker is checking whether something is not true or not happening.

Invitation: “Want to ...?” / “Why don’t we ...?” — the speaker is suggesting an activity. This is a very common casual way to invite a friend. Compare the polite invitation 〜ませんか.

Negative past

The plain past negative is 〜なかった: 食たべない → 食たべなかった. The polite equivalent is 食たべませんでした. This is a related form, not the basic ない-form itself.

04 〜ないでください — Please Don’t

Use Verb ない-form + でください to politely ask someone not to do something. Although the verb is in the plain negative form, the whole request is polite.

Verb ない + でください
ここで写真しゃしんを撮とらないでください。
Please don't take photos here.
窓まどを開あけないでください。
Please don't open the window.
Compare

〜てください asks someone to do something; 〜ないでください asks them not to. See the て-form lesson.

05 〜なければなりません / 〜ないといけません — Must

Both patterns express obligation or necessity. In everyday conversation, 〜ないといけません is common; 〜なければなりません often sounds more formal. In casual speech, 〜ないと is frequently used by itself when the meaning is clear.

ない → なければなりません / ない + といけません
明日あした、早はやく起おきなければなりません。
I have to get up early tomorrow.
薬くすりを飲のまないといけません。
I have to take medicine.
Don't confuse the meanings

〜ないといけません means you must do the action, not that you must avoid it. The negative form is part of the obligation pattern.

06 〜なくてもいいです — Don’t Have To

Use 〜なくてもいいです to say an action is not necessary. This is different from saying an action is forbidden.

ない → なくてもいいです
明日あしたは来こなくてもいいです。
You don't have to come tomorrow.
全部ぜんぶ食たべなくてもいいですよ。
You don't have to eat everything.
Compare: not necessary vs prohibited

〜なくてもいい = you do not need to do it. 〜てはいけません = you must not do it. These are not interchangeable. See て-form for the prohibition pattern.

07 〜ないで vs. 〜なくて

Both contain a negative verb, but they connect ideas differently.

〜ないで — without doing / instead of doing

Use this when someone does another action without doing the first action.

朝あさごはんを食たべないで学校がっこうに行いきました。
I went to school without eating breakfast.
〜なくて — reason / situation

Often expresses a reason or cause, especially when the following phrase describes a feeling, difficulty, or result. It is not simply a substitute for 〜ないで.

時間じかんがなくて、困こまっています。
I'm having trouble because I don't have enough time.
友達ともだちに会あえなくて、寂さびしいです。
I'm sad because I can't see my friend.
Key difference

〜ないで focuses on an action that was not done while another action happened. 〜なくて can explain why someone feels a certain way or why a situation occurred. Other uses exist, so context matters.

08 〜ないほうがいい — Advice

Use 〜ないほうがいいです to advise someone not to do something. It is stronger than simply saying you personally would not do it.

Verb ない + ほうがいいです
今日きょうは無理むりしないほうがいいですよ。
You'd better not push yourself today.
夜よる遅おそくコーヒーを飲のまないほうがいいです。
You shouldn't drink coffee late at night.
Related pattern

For positive advice, Japanese commonly uses 〜たほうがいい. See the た-form lesson.

09 〜ないつもりです — Intention

Use 〜ないつもりです to express an intention not to do something. It describes a plan or decision, rather than a rule or prohibition.

Verb ない + つもりです
今年ことしは車くるまを買かわないつもりです。
I don't intend to buy a car this year.
週末しゅうまつは出でかけないつもりです。
I'm planning not to go out this weekend.

10 Other Useful Patterns

Once you know the ない-form, you can recognize many more expressions. Learn the basic meaning here; the detailed grammar can be studied separately.

忘わすれないように、メモします。
I'll make a note so I don't forget. (〜ないように: so that something does not happen)
雨あめが降ふらなかったら、散歩さんぽします。
If it doesn't rain, I'll go for a walk. (〜なかったら: negative condition)
行いかなくちゃ。
I've got to go. (casual contraction of an obligation expression)
Next step

These patterns involve more than conjugation. Understanding what the speaker is trying to express—purpose, condition, or obligation—is just as important as recognizing the form.
