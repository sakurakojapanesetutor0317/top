N5 GRAMMAR · TIME EXPRESSIONS

〜とき

When / At the time of

Learn how to say when something happens with verbs, adjectives, and nouns—and how the verb form changes the timing.

N5Time expressions〜とき
Plain FormDictionary FormNai-formTa-formAdjectiveNoun

01 What Does 〜とき Mean?

Use 〜とき to say when / at the time that something happens. The part before とき describes the situation or time; the main clause tells us what happens then.

朝あさ、起おきるとき、音楽おんがくを聴ききます。
I listen to music when I wake up.
忙いそがしいとき、コーヒーを飲のみます。
I drink coffee when I am busy.

とき is a noun meaning “time.” A verb, adjective, or noun can describe that time. The sentence ending can still be polite (〜ます / 〜です).

02 Verb + とき — Dictionary Form / た-Form

Think of とき as a snapshot of a moment. Ask: Had the action happened yet at that moment? The form before とき shows the timing of that action relative to the snapshot — not whether the whole sentence is past or future.

日本にほんに行いくとき、カメラを買かいました。
I bought a camera before going to Japan. → At the time I bought it, the trip had NOT happened yet.
日本にほんに行いったとき、カメラを買かいました。
I bought a camera when I went to Japan. → At the time I bought it, I had already arrived in Japan.
家いえを出でるとき、電話でんわしました。
I called when I was about to leave home.
家いえを出でたとき、雨あめが降ふっていました。
When I had left the house, it was raining.
ご飯はんを食たべているとき、電話でんわが鳴なりました。
My phone rang while I was eating.
日本にほんに住すんでいるとき、日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうしました。
I studied Japanese while I was living in Japan.

Compare the same main verb: Both sentences end in 買かいました (past tense). The difference is 行いくとき = before going versus 行いったとき = once you have gone / when you were there. The た-form before とき does not automatically mean the entire sentence is about the past.

Picture the moment:

Dictionary form + とき → BEFORE the action is completed (often before it starts): 行いくとき = before going / when about to go.

た-form + とき → AFTER the action has happened or reached the relevant point: 行いったとき = when you have gone / when you are there.

〜ている + とき → DURING the action or continuing state: 食たべているとき = while eating.

03 The Main Sentence Can Be Past or Non-Past

Do not automatically use the た-form before とき just because the sentence ends in 〜ました. The verb before とき and the main verb have different jobs.

日本にほんに行いくとき、切符きっぷを買かいました。
I bought a ticket before going to Japan.
日本にほんに行いくとき、切符きっぷを買かいます。
I buy / will buy a ticket before going to Japan.
日本にほんに行いったとき、友達ともだちに会あいました。
I met a friend when I visited Japan.

Remember: “I bought” (買かいました) is past, but “going” (行いく) can still be dictionary form because the trip had not happened yet when the ticket was bought. A past main verb does not force a た-form before とき.

04 Verb ない-Form + とき

Use the plain negative form to describe a time when you do not do something or when something does not happen.

時間じかんがないとき、朝あさごはんを食たべません。
When I do not have time, I do not eat breakfast.
学校がっこうに行いかないとき、家いえで勉強べんきょうします。
When I do not go to school, I study at home.

05 い-Adjective + とき

Use the plain い-adjective directly before とき. Do not add です between the adjective and とき.

寒さむいとき、コーヒーを飲のみます。
When it is cold, I drink coffee.
忙いそがしいとき、料理りょうりを作つくりません。
When I am busy, I do not cook.
若わかいとき、よく公園こうえんで遊あそびました。
When I was young, I often played in the park.

Common mistake — do NOT add の after an い-adjective. An い-adjective connects directly to とき, just as it does before a noun. Some learners overuse の because nouns use のとき, but the patterns are different.

✓ 寒さむいとき　　✗ 寒さむいのとき
✓ 忙いそがしいとき　　✗ 忙いそがしいのとき
✓ 学生がくせいのとき　　✓ 暇ひまなとき

06 な-Adjective + な + とき

Use な after a な-adjective before とき. This is the same な used before a noun.

暇ひまなとき、本ほんを読よみます。
When I have free time, I read books.
元気げんきなとき、公園こうえんで遊あそびます。
When I feel well, I play in the park.
静しずかなとき、勉強べんきょうします。
I study when it is quiet.

Pattern: 暇ひまなとき ✓ / 暇ひまだとき ✗ / 暇ひまですとき ✗

07 Noun + の + とき

Use の to connect a noun to とき. The noun describes a stage of life, situation, or occasion.

学生がくせいのとき、日本にほん語ごを勉強べんきょうしました。
When I was a student, I studied Japanese.
子供こどものとき、よく公園こうえんで遊あそびました。
When I was a child, I often played in the park.
病気びょうきのとき、薬くすりを飲のみます。
When I am sick, I take medicine.

Pattern: 学生がくせいのとき ✓ / 学生がくせいなとき ✗. Compare: な-adjective + な, noun + の.

08 Compare the Connections

The word before とき determines which connection to use.

食たべるとき、手てを洗あらいます。
When I eat / before eating, I wash my hands.
食たべたとき、おいしいと思おもいました。
When I ate it, I thought it was delicious.
寒さむいとき、家いえにいます。
When it is cold, I stay home.
暇ひまなとき、本ほんを読よみます。
When I am free, I read books.
学生がくせいのとき、日本にほんに住すんでいました。
When I was a student, I lived in Japan.
勉強べんきょうしているとき、音楽おんがくを聴ききません。
I do not listen to music while I am studying.

09 〜とき vs. 〜前まえに / 〜後あとで

〜とき focuses on the time or situation. 〜前まえに explicitly means “before,” and 〜後あとで explicitly means “after.” They are related but not always interchangeable.

寝ねるとき、電気でんきを消けします。
I turn off the light when going to bed.
寝ねる前まえに、電気でんきを消けします。
I turn off the light before going to bed.
ごはんを食たべた後あとで、勉強べんきょうします。
I study after eating.

A dictionary-form verb before とき does not automatically mean exactly the same as 前まえに. Use the surrounding context to understand the timing.

10 Common Mistakes and Meaning Checks

Watch the connection after nouns and な-adjectives, and compare whether the earlier action is completed.

暇ひまなとき、映画えいがを見みます。
When I have free time, I watch movies.
学生がくせいのとき、毎日まいにち勉強べんきょうしました。
When I was a student, I studied every day.
駅えきに着つくとき、電話でんわしてください。
Please call when you are about to arrive at the station.
駅えきに着ついたとき、電話でんわしてください。
Please call when you have arrived at the station.

The last two sentences are both grammatical, but they ask for calls at different points in time.

〜とき vs. 〜たら — time vs. condition / sequence

〜とき identifies a time or situation (“when / at the time”). 〜たら often means “if” or “once / after something happens,” especially when the second action depends on the first. They can overlap, but they are not always interchangeable.

暇ひまなとき、本ほんを読よみます。
When I have free time, I read books. (A time or situation.)
暇ひまだったら、一緒いっしょに映画えいがを見みませんか。
If you are free, would you like to watch a movie? (A condition.)
駅えきに着ついたとき、電話でんわしてください。
Call me when you arrive at the station. (Focus on the time.)
駅えきに着ついたら、電話でんわしてください。
Call me once you arrive at the station. (The call follows the arrival.)

Learn more: 〜たら has other uses, including hypothetical conditions. See 〜たら (Conditional Form) in Related Grammar below.
