N5 GRAMMAR · PARTICLES

と・や・も・の

And · With · Also · Possession

Learn how to connect nouns, talk about doing things with someone, add information, and show possession.

N5ParticlesConnecting · Adding · Possession
Noun

01 と — And (Complete List)

Use と to connect nouns when you are listing the items together. It does not connect full sentences.

Noun A + と + Noun B

Example sentences

パンとたまごを食たべます。

I eat bread and eggs.

月曜日げつようびと水曜日すいようびに勉強べんきょうします。

I study on Monday and Wednesday.

02 と — With Someone

Use と after a person to say you do something with them.

Person + と + Verb

Example sentences

友達ともだちと映画えいがを見みます。

I watch a movie with a friend.

母ははと買かい物ものに行いきます。

I go shopping with my mother.

03 や — And (Some Examples)

Use や to list some examples, not necessarily everything. など means “etc.” and is often added at the end.

Noun A + や + Noun B + (など)

Example sentences

りんごやバナナを買かいました。

I bought apples, bananas, and other things.

本ほんやノートなどがあります。

There are books, notebooks, etc.

と vs や — What is the difference?

と = A and B (a complete list in context). や = A, B, and other things (examples).

04 〜とか — Things Like… (Casual Examples, N4)

とか lists examples, like や, but it sounds more casual and conversational. You can put とか after each item.

Noun A + とか + Noun B + (とか)

週末しゅうまつは映画えいがとか買かい物ものとかを楽たのしみます。

On weekends, I enjoy things like films and shopping.

休やすみの日ひは掃除そうじとか洗濯せんたくとかをします。

On my days off, I do things like cleaning and laundry.

果物くだものなら、りんごとかみかんが好すきです。

As for fruit, I like things like apples and mandarins.

や vs. とか

Both give some examples. や is neutral and common in writing. とか is more casual and is mostly used in conversation.

05 も — Also / Too

Use も when adding something that is also true. It often replaces は, が, or を.

Noun + も + Predicate

Example sentences

わたしも学生がくせいです。

I am a student, too.

コーヒーも飲のみます。

I drink coffee, too.

日本語にほんごも英語えいごも話はなします。

I speak both Japanese and English.

Does も replace another particle?

Yes, with は / が / を, use も instead: コーヒーを飲のみます → コーヒーも飲のみます (I drink coffee too).

With に / で / へ and と meaning “with,” keep that particle before も.

学校がっこうにも行いきます。

I go to school too. (に + も)

学校がっこうでも勉強べんきょうします。

I study at school too. (で + も)

友達ともだちとも話はなします。

I also talk with my friends. (と + も)

東京とうきょうへも行いきます。

I go to Tokyo too. (へ + も)

Compare は (topic / contrast) and も (also / too) in は・が・を. For more on に / で, see に・で.

06 Noun + でも — …or Something (Soft Suggestions, N4)

Add でも to a noun to give it as one example. It makes an invitation or suggestion softer: “some tea, or something like that.”

Noun + でも + 〜ませんか / 〜ましょうか / 〜てください

お茶ちゃでも飲のみませんか。

Would you like some tea or something?

映画えいがでも見みに行いきましょうか。

Shall we go and see a movie or something?

待まっている間あいだ、雑誌ざっしでも読よんでいてください。

Please read a magazine or something while you wait.

Not the same as で + も

In 学校がっこうでも勉強べんきょうします (“I study at school, too”), でも is the particle で + も. In お茶ちゃでも飲のみませんか, でも follows a noun that is the object of the verb and means “…or something.”

07 の — Possession, Description & Noun Replacement

Use の to connect nouns, show possession, or replace a noun when its meaning is clear from context.

Noun A + の + Noun B

Example sentences — Possession & relationships

これはわたしの本ほんです。

This is my book. (possession)

日本語にほんごの先生せんせいです。

(Someone) is a Japanese teacher. (relationship)

日本語にほんごの本ほんです。

It's a Japanese-language book. (type / content)

東京とうきょうのホテルに泊とまります。

I stay at a hotel in Tokyo. (location / relationship)

机つくえの上うえに本ほんがあります。

There is a book on the desk. (position)

Example sentences — Questions with の

これは何なんの本ほんですか。

What kind of book is this?

これはだれの本ほんですか。

Whose book is this?

の can replace a noun

When the noun is already clear, you can leave it out and use の instead.

Adjective + の / Noun + の

Example sentences

A: これはだれの本ほんですか。
B: わたしのです。

A: Whose book is this?
B: It's mine.

赤あかいのをください。

Please give me the red one.

この大おおきいのがほしいです。

I want this big one.
