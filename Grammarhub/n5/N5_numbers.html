How the Numbers Work

Read the place values in order

Build the number from large to small

21 = 20 + 1 → にじゅういち
105 = 100 + 5 → ひゃくご
3,800 = 3,000 + 800 → さんぜんはっぴゃく

Do not say いち before ひゃく or せん in ordinary numbers such as 100 and 1,000. You do say いち in 10,000: いちまん.

Readings to notice

300: さんびゃく · 600: ろっぴゃく · 800: はっぴゃく
3,000: さんぜん · 8,000: はっせん

4 can also be し; 7 can be しち; 9 can be く. This list uses よん・なな・きゅう as the basic number readings. A following counter can require a different reading: 4時じ is よじ, 7時じ is しちじ, and 9時じ is くじ.

Japanese groups large numbers in units of 10,000

10,000 = 1万まん → いちまん
100,000 = 10万まん → じゅうまん
1,000,000 = 100万まん → ひゃくまん
10,000,000 = 1,000万まん → いっせんまん

The 10,000,000 card uses いっせんまん; せんまん is also used. Above this, 100,000,000 = 1億おく and 1,000,000,000,000 = 1兆ちょう. English commas group three digits, but Japanese 万まん・億おく・兆ちょう group four digits.

Numbers change when used with counters

This page teaches numbers by themselves. Prices, clock times, people, and objects can have their own readings. For example, 4 is よん, but 4時じ is よじ. Check the time section in Lesson 4; use the quantity page for object counters.
