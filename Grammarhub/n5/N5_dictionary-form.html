01 What Is the Dictionary Form?

The dictionary form is the form used to look up verbs in a dictionary. It is also the plain non-past affirmative form of a verb. In casual speech, it can be a complete sentence. In longer sentences, it is used before many grammar patterns—even when the sentence ends politely.

食たべる。
I eat. / I will eat. (casual)
明日あした、映画えいがを見みる。
I will watch a movie tomorrow. (casual)

Japanese non-past forms can describe habits, present facts, or future actions. Context and time words tell you which meaning is intended.

02 How to Make the Dictionary Form

Always start with the ます-form. The conversion depends on the verb group.

Group 1 — Change the sound before ます to its う-row sound

Remove ます, then change the final い-row sound to the corresponding う-row sound (き→く, ぎ→ぐ, し→す, ち→つ, に→ぬ, び→ぶ, み→む, り→る, い→う).

書かきます → 書かく
write
話はなします → 話はなす
speak

Group 2 — Remove ます and add る

食たべます → 食たべる
eat
見みます → 見みる
see / watch

Group 3 — Irregular verbs

します → する
do
来きます → 来くる
come

Some verbs that look like Group 2 are actually Group 1, such as 帰かえります → 帰かえる and 入はいります → 入はいる. Check the verb group instead of guessing from the sound alone.

03 Casual Statements and Questions

In casual Japanese, the dictionary form can end a statement. Add a question intonation (often written with ？) to ask a question. You do not need です or ます.

今日きょう、家いえで食たべる。
I’m eating at home today. / I’ll eat at home today.
食たべる？
Are you going to eat? / Want some?
明日あした、行いく？
Are you going tomorrow?

A casual question such as 食たべる？ can also sound like an offer or invitation depending on the situation. Unlike 〜ない？, the dictionary-form question is not specifically a negative invitation.

04 Dictionary Form vs. Plain Form

Plain form is a broader category, not just the dictionary form. The dictionary form is its non-past affirmative member.

食たべる
Plain affirmative / dictionary form
食たべない
Plain negative
食たべた
Plain past affirmative
食たべなかった
Plain past negative

Some grammar patterns require the dictionary form specifically; others allow multiple plain forms. Always check the pattern.

05 〜ことができます — Ability

Dictionary form + ことができます means “can do.” In this fixed ability pattern, use こと, not の.

日本語にほんごを話はなすことができます。
I can speak Japanese.
泳およぐことができます。
I can swim.

You may also express ability with a potential verb, such as 話はなせます. This is a different grammatical structure.

06 〜前まえに / 〜つもりです — Before and Intention

Use the dictionary form before many grammar patterns. The whole sentence can still end with です or ます.

寝ねる前まえに、本ほんを読よみます。
I read a book before going to bed.
来年らいねん、日本にほんに行いくつもりです。
I intend to go to Japan next year.

07 〜ことがあります — Sometimes Do

Dictionary form + ことがあります means that something happens sometimes. This is different from た-form + ことがあります, which describes past experience.

週末しゅうまつは外そとで食たべることがあります。
I sometimes eat out on weekends.
時々ときどき、電車でんしゃで寝ねることがあります。
Sometimes I fall asleep on the train.

Compare: 日本にほんに行いくことがあります = I sometimes go to Japan; 日本にほんに行いったことがあります = I have been to Japan.

08 〜のが好すきです / 〜ことが好すきです — Likes

When describing an activity you like, both の and こと can nominalize the verb. The meaning is often similar, though の can feel more immediate or conversational.

料理りょうりをするのが好すきです。
I like cooking.
料理りょうりをすることが好すきです。
I like cooking.

09 こと vs. の — When Can You Switch?

Both こと and の can turn a verb phrase into a noun-like expression, but they are not always interchangeable. The grammar after the nominalized phrase determines which one you can use.

食たべることができます。
✓ I can eat. (ability pattern)
食たべるのができます。
△ Not the standard ability pattern. Use ことができます.
食たべるのが好すきです。
✓ I like eating.
食たべることが好すきです。
✓ I like eating.
時々ときどき、外そとで食たべることがあります。
✓ I sometimes eat out.
時々ときどき、外そとで食たべるのがあります。
✕ Not a replacement for ことがあります meaning “sometimes.”

Important: のができます or のがあります may appear in other sentence structures or contexts, but they do not replace the fixed patterns dictionary form + ことができます (ability) or dictionary form + ことがあります (occasional action).

10 More Grammar That Uses the Dictionary Form

Once you know the dictionary form, you can build many different sentences. Some of these patterns are introduced at later levels.

早はやく起おきるようにしています。
I try to get up early. (habit / effort)
雨あめが降ふると、道みちがぬれます。
When it rains, the roads get wet.
行いくなら、早はやく予約よやくしたほうがいいです。
If you’re going, you should book early.

These are examples of how widely the dictionary form is used. Learn each pattern separately because its meaning and allowed verb forms may differ.
