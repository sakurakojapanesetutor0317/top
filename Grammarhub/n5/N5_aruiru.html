N5 GRAMMAR · EXISTENCE

あります・います

There is / There are · Have

Learn how Japanese describes existence, location, and possession—and why the same animal can sometimes be described with います or あります.

N5Existence · LocationJapanese Perspective

01 Basic Rule: あります vs. います

Both verbs can mean “there is / there are” or “have.” Japanese chooses the verb according to how the speaker understands the thing being described.

あります — things and plants
います — people and animals

机つくえの上うえに本ほんがあります。

There is a book on the desk.

庭にわにねこがいます。

There is a cat in the garden.

公園こうえんに木きがあります。

There are trees in the park. (Plants normally take あります.)

教室きょうしつに学生がくせいがいます。

There are students in the classroom.

Important: “living” is only a starting point

Plants are alive, but they normally use あります. So the rule is not simply “alive = います / not alive = あります.”

02 Sentence Patterns & Location

Use に for the location where someone or something exists. Use が to introduce what is there.

Place + に + Person / Thing + が + います / あります

部屋へやにテレビがあります。

There is a TV in the room.

駅えきに友達ともだちがいます。

My friend is at the station.

When the person or thing is already the topic

Person / Thing + は + Place + に + います / あります

ねこはソファの下したにいます。

The cat is under the sofa.

本ほんは机つくえの上うえにあります。

The book is on the desk.

For a fuller explanation of は・が and に, see the existing particle lessons.

03 Japanese Perspective: How We See It

In Japanese, the choice is sometimes influenced by whether we treat something as an individual living being or as an object, product, or material. This is a useful way to understand cases that do not fit the beginner rule.

Same fish, different viewpoint

水槽すいそうに魚さかながいます。There are fish in the aquarium. — Fish seen as living animals.

スーパーに魚さかながあります。There is fish at the supermarket. — Fish treated as food or merchandise.

In the second sentence, the fish is understood as something sold at the store, not as an animal swimming around.

Robots, dolls, and fictional characters

A robot is a machine, so あります is a normal choice when referring to it as equipment. But a robot presented as a character or an agent acting like a person can be described with います in some contexts.

倉庫そうこにロボットがあります。There is a robot in the warehouse. — Equipment.

ファミリーレストランに配膳はいぜんロボットがいます。There is a serving robot at the family restaurant. — A robot treated as an active server.

A stuffed animal normally uses あります as an object, but います may be used affectionately or imaginatively when treating it like a companion.

Plants are a key exception to the simple rule

Trees, flowers, and grass are living organisms, yet Japanese ordinarily uses あります for their existence. This shows why “alive vs. not alive” is not a complete explanation.

Does the speaker get to choose freely?

No. These are context-dependent tendencies, not interchangeable verbs. People and ordinary animals normally take います; ordinary objects and plants normally take あります. Special perspectives are useful for understanding particular situations, not for replacing the standard rule everywhere.

04 Having Something / Someone

Both verbs can express possession or the presence of something in your life. English may translate them as “have.”

車くるまがあります。

I have a car.

兄あにがいます。

I have an older brother.

時間じかんがありますか。

Do you have time?

ペットがいますか。

Do you have any pets?

The subject or owner can be understood from context. Add わたしには when you need to make “as for me” explicit.

05 Present, Negative & Past

Both verbs are irregular in the negative: ありません and いません. In the past, use ありました and いました.

Formありますいます
Presentありますいます
Negativeありませんいません
Pastありましたいました
Past negativeありませんでしたいませんでした

Example sentences

今日きょうは時間じかんがありません。

I don't have time today.

昨日きのう、公園こうえんに犬いぬがいました。

There was a dog in the park yesterday.

昔むかし、ここに学校がっこうがありました。

There used to be a school here.

教室きょうしつにだれもいませんでした。

There was nobody in the classroom.

06 Questions: What? Who? Where?

机つくえの上うえに何なにがありますか。

What is on the desk?

部屋へやにだれがいますか。

Who is in the room?

トイレはどこにありますか。

Where is the restroom?

先生せんせいはどこにいますか。

Where is the teacher?

Remember: あります is not です

です identifies or describes something: 学校がっこうです (It is a school). あります says that something exists or is located somewhere: ここに学校がっこうがあります (There is a school here).
