N5 GRAMMAR · NOUN MODIFICATION

名詞修飾めいししゅうしょく

Noun Modification · Describing a Noun

Find the head noun, build its description, and understand how Japanese and English word order differ.

N5 · FoundationsPlain clauses［description］ + noun

01What Is Noun Modification?

Noun modification means adding information to identify or describe a noun: which book, what kind of person, or which place. The noun being described is the head noun.

［明日あした読よむ］本ほん
the book [I will read tomorrow]
［昨日きのう買かった］かばん
the bag [I bought yesterday]
Follow the matching marks

［ ］ / [ ] mark the description. The underlined words are the noun it describes. Japanese puts the description before the noun; English usually puts a relative clause after it. Brackets and underlining are learning aids.

02Basic Word Order — Description First

Japanese: ［description］ + noun
English: noun + [description]

Put a plain verb clause directly before its noun. Japanese does not add a relative pronoun equivalent to who / which / that, and does not insert の between a verb clause and its noun.

［わたしが読よむ］本ほん
the book [that I read]
［日本にほんに住すんでいる］人ひと
the person [who lives in Japan]
Avoid these extra words

✓ ［昨日きのう買かった］本ほん
✗ 昨日きのう買かったの本ほん
✗ 昨日きのう買かいました本ほん

Use plain forms inside the modifier. A polite ending may appear at the end of the whole sentence.

03Build It from a Sentence — Keep a Gap

Start with a sentence about the noun. Remove the noun together with its particle, then put that noun after the remaining clause. The noun’s role is understood from the clause; do not repeat it inside the description.

Object: what I bought

わたしが 本ほんを 買かいました。
→ わたしが ______ 買かった
→ ［わたしが買かった］本ほん

I bought a book. → the book [I bought].
The book was the object of 買かいました. Remove 本ほんを, not just 本ほん.

Subject: who bought it

その人ひとが 本ほんを 買かいました。
→ ______ 本ほんを 買かった
→ ［本ほんを買かった］人ひと

That person bought a book. → the person [who bought a book].
Remove その人ひとが. Keep 本ほんを, because it is still the object inside the clause.

04Particles Inside and Outside the Clause

The head noun can be the subject, object, destination, or place understood inside the description. Keep particles for the other words inside the clause. After the head noun, choose the particle needed by the main sentence.

［友達ともだちと見みた］映画えいが
the movie [I watched with a friend]
［去年きょねん行いった］町まち
the town [I went to last year]
［日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうする］学校がっこう
the school [where I study Japanese]
［母ははが作つくった］料理りょうり
the food [my mother made]
A complete sentence has two layers

［昨日きのう買かった］本ほんを 読よみます。

I will read the book [I bought yesterday].

Inside the brackets: the book is what I bought. Outside the brackets: 本ほんを is the object of 読よみます. The particle after the noun belongs to the main sentence.

05Subjects in Modifiers — Usually が, Not Topic は

When the describing clause has its own subject, が is the basic choice. Do not carry an ordinary topic は from a standalone sentence into a neutral modifying clause.

Compare the sentence and the modifier

わたしは この本ほんを 買かいました。
→ ［わたしが買かった］本ほん

the book [I bought]. The person who bought it is the subject inside the modifier. Contrastive は has other uses; begin with が for this pattern.

Sometimes が can become の

［母ははが作つくった］料理りょうり / ［母ははの作つくった］料理りょうり

Both can mean “the food my mother made.” This の replaces the subject particle が inside the clause. It is not a connector after the verb: 母ははが作つくったの料理りょうり is incorrect for this meaning. This optional subject の is a separate use; using が is a good starting point.

06Choose the Form for the Intended Meaning

Dictionary, negative, past, and 〜ている forms can all modify nouns. Choose the meaning you need. Non-past can describe a future action or a usual action; た-form describes completion / a past action. The surrounding context determines the time relationship.

［明日あした読よむ］本ほん
the book [I will read tomorrow]
［毎日まいにち読よむ］本ほん
the book [I read every day]
［昨日きのう読よんだ］本ほん
the book [I read yesterday]
［まだ読よんでいない］本ほん
the book [I have not read yet]
［肉にくを食たべない］人ひと
a person [who does not eat meat]
［今いま、本ほんを読よんでいる］人ひと
the person [who is reading a book now]
Past negative and past ongoing states also work

［読よまなかった］本ほん = a book I did not read
［使つかっていた］かばん = the bag I was using / used to use

Do not choose a form only from an English translation. Ask whether the action is completed, negative, ongoing, or habitual in the situation.

07Adjectives and Nouns — Three Different Connections

A description does not have to contain a verb. The type of word determines the connection.

Description type Connection Example
い-adjective い-adjective + noun ［高たかい］本ほん
な-adjective · non-past affirmative な-adjective + な + noun ［静しずかな］部屋へや
Noun · direct noun description noun + の + noun ［日本語にほんごの］本ほん
Do not use の as a universal connector

✗ 高たかいの本ほん → ✓ 高たかい本ほん
✗ 静しずかの部屋へや → ✓ 静しずかな部屋へや
✓ 日本語にほんごの本ほん — 日本語にほんご is a noun.

Keep adjective tense and polarity: 高たかかった本ほん / 高たかくない本ほん / 静しずかだった町まち / 静しずかじゃない町まち. Do not keep sentence-ending だ before the noun: ✗ 静しずかだ部屋へや.

Noun predicates inside longer descriptions

［学生がくせいだった］人ひと = a person who was a student
［学生がくせいではない］人ひと = a person who is not a student

For an affirmative non-past identity description, 学生がくせいの人ひと is possible; 学生がくせいである人ひと is a more formal alternative. The simple rule “noun + の” does not mean you must insert の after every noun-predicate clause.

08Use the Whole Noun Phrase in a Sentence

The full unit ［description］ + noun works as one noun phrase. It can be the topic, subject, object, or another part of the main sentence.

［昨日きのう買かった］本ほんは おもしろいです。
The book [I bought yesterday] is interesting. — topic は
［日本にほんに住すんでいる］友達ともだちに 会あいます。
I will meet a friend [who lives in Japan]. — person met に
これは ［母ははが作つくった］料理りょうりです。
This is the food [my mother made]. — noun phrase + です
［駅えきの近ちかくにある］店みせで 買かい物ものをします。
I shop at the store [that is near the station]. — action location で
Polite main sentence, plain modifier

✓ ［昨日きのう買かった］本ほんを読よみます。
✗ ［昨日きのう買かいました］本ほんを読よみます。

The main ending 読よみます is polite. That does not change the modifier 買かった to 買かいました.

09Longer Descriptions — Find the Head Noun First

A modifier can contain time, place, people, and objects. Find the noun after the clause, then read the bracketed words as its description. The brackets may cover several words.

［昨日きのう、友達ともだちと駅えきの近ちかくの店みせで買かった］かばん

the bag [I bought at a store near the station with a friend yesterday]

The head noun is かばん. Everything inside the brackets identifies which bag. The の in 駅えきの近ちかくの店みせ connects nouns within the clause; it does not connect 買かった to かばん.

The noun immediately after a modifier is its target

［日本にほんに住すんでいる］友達ともだちの本ほん

日本にほんに住すんでいる describes 友達ともだち. Then 友達ともだちの本ほん means that friend’s book: “the book belonging to my friend who lives in Japan.” It does not mean the book lives in Japan.

10Longer Sentences — Follow the Whole Noun Phrase

Read each full sentence. The colored brackets show the description; the matching underline identifies the head noun in Japanese and English. The main sentence continues outside the brackets.

Example 01
［母ははが学生がくせいのときに使つかっていた］かばんを、今いま使つかっています。
I am using the bag [my mother used when she was a student] now.

The bracket includes 母ははが学生がくせいのときに and 使つかっていた. All of it describes かばん. Outside the bracket, かばんを is the object of 今いま使つかっています.

Example 02
［友達ともだちが2年前ねんまえに読よんでいた］本ほんを、今度こんど貸かしてもらいます。
I am going to borrow the book [my friend was reading two years ago] soon.

読よんでいた describes the friend’s past reading. 貸かしてもらいます describes my upcoming arrangement: I will have someone lend me the book. The head noun 本ほん is the object of 貸かしてもらいます.

Example 03
［去年きょねん、日本にほんを旅行りょこうしたときに買かった］カップで、毎朝まいあさコーヒーを飲のんでいます。
Every morning, I drink coffee from the cup [I bought when I traveled around Japan last year].

The whole bracket identifies the cup. カップで tells us the item used to drink coffee; コーヒーを is the object of 飲のんでいます. A modified noun does not always take を.

Example 04
［駅えきの近ちかくにある新あたらしい店みせで働はたらいている］姉あねに、今週末こんしゅうまつ会あいに行いきます。
This weekend, I am going to see my older sister [who works at a new store near the station].

The final head noun is 姉あね. Inside its modifier, 駅えきの近ちかくにある describes 店みせ, and 店みせで tells us where she works. Outside the bracket, 姉あねに marks the person I will meet.

11とき Is Also a Noun — Watch the Connection

The same connection differences explain why these common errors occur before とき.

Type Correct Incorrect for this meaning
Verb 食たべるとき / 食たべたとき 食たべるのとき
い-adjective 忙いそがしいとき 忙いそがしいのとき
な-adjective 暇ひまなとき 暇ひまのとき
Noun 学生がくせいのとき 学生がくせいだとき
Do not confuse a connector with nominalization

本ほんを読よむのが好すきです is correct: の turns the activity into a noun phrase, and が marks it before 好すきです.

That is different from describing a noun: ［明日あした読よむ］本ほん. There is no nominalizing の between 読よむ and 本ほん.
