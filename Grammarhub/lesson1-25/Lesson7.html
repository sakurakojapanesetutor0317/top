02

What Do You Use? — で

Tools and means

で: the tool or means used for an action

Put で after the tool or means: ペンで書かきます = write with a pen. This is related to Lesson 5 transport で, but different from Lesson 6 action-location で.

Keep the roles separate: ペンで tells you what you use; 手紙てがみを tells you what you write.

ペンで 手紙てがみを 書かきます。

I write a letter with a pen.

箸はしで ご飯はんを 食たべます。

I eat rice / a meal with chopsticks.

はさみで 紙かみを 切きります。

I cut paper with scissors.

スマホで 写真しゃしんを 撮とります。

I take photos with my smartphone.

One particle, several jobs

電車でんしゃで行いきます: transport.
図書館としょかんで勉強べんきょうします: action location.
ペンで書かきます: tool.

The meaning comes from the noun and the action, not from one fixed English translation of で.

03

In Which Language?

Ask for a word or expression

Language + で

Use で for the language you use to communicate: 日本語にほんごで書かきます = write in Japanese. The language is the means of expression.

英語えいごで何なんですか asks for an English word or expression; 日本語にほんごで何なんですか asks for its Japanese equivalent. In this question, read 何なに as なん.

日本語にほんごで 手紙てがみを 書かきます。

I write a letter in Japanese.

英語えいごで メールを 書かきました。

I wrote an email in English.

A：「Thank you」は 日本語にほんごで 何なんですか。
B：「ありがとう」です。

A: How do you say “Thank you” in Japanese?
B: “Arigatou.”

A：「はさみ」は 英語えいごで 何なんですか。
B：「Scissors」です。

A: What is “hasami” in English?
B: “Scissors.”

The quoted expression is the topic

「Thank you」は日本語にほんごで何なんですか literally asks what the expression “Thank you” is in Japanese. This is useful even when you do not yet know a verb such as “say.”

04

Give — あげます

The giver and the recipient

Giver は + recipient に + thing を + あげます

あげます means “give.” The topic / understood subject is the giver. に marks who receives the thing; を marks what is given.

If you are describing what you give, わたしは can be omitted when it is obvious. Here, あげます describes transferring a thing; the later pattern verb + てあげます describes doing a favour and is not introduced in this lesson.

わたしは アンさんに 本ほんを あげました。

I gave Ann a book.

母ははに 花はなを あげます。

I will give my mother flowers.

A：だれに プレゼントを あげましたか。
B：友達ともだちに あげました。

A: Who did you give a present to?
B: A friend.

05

Receive — もらいます

The receiver and the source

Receiver は + giver に / から + thing を + もらいます

もらいます means “receive.” The topic / understood subject is now the receiver. に or から marks the person the thing comes from; を marks the thing received.

With a person, both 友達ともだちにもらいます and 友達ともだちからもらいます are possible. With an organization as the source, use から: 会社かいしゃからもらいます.

アンさんは わたしに 本ほんを もらいました。

Ann received a book from me.

友達ともだちから プレゼントを もらいました。

I received a present from a friend.

A：だれに その 本ほんを もらいましたか。
B：先生せんせいに もらいました。

A: Who did you receive that book from?
B: My teacher.

Review に — the person involved. With あげます, に marks the recipient; with もらいます, it marks the giver / source. Read it together with the verb.

06

One Transfer, Two Viewpoints

Keep the direction of the transfer clear

The same transfer, two viewpoints

わたし → 本ほん → アンさん

わたしはアンさんに本ほんをあげました。
I gave Ann a book.
Focus on the giver: I.

アンさんはわたしに本ほんをもらいました。
Ann received a book from me.
Focus on the receiver: Ann.

The book travels in the same direction in both sentences. Changing the verb changes whose action you describe. に does not always mean “to”: with あげます it marks the recipient; with もらいます it marks the source.

Giving to me / us: recognize くれます

When someone gives something to the speaker or the speaker’s close group, くれます is commonly used: アンさんはわたしに本ほんをくれました — “Ann gave me a book.” It describes the giver’s action toward the speaker’s side.

For Lesson 7, you can describe this from the receiving side: わたしはアンさんに本ほんをもらいました. Do not use あげます as an automatic translation of every English “give.” くれます is studied later.

あげます is a beginner pattern, but choosing a giving verb also involves relationships. More respectful expressions are used for some real-life situations with teachers, customers, or superiors.

Continue: actions done for someone

〜てくれる・〜てもらう・〜てあげる describes doing an action for someone, rather than giving a thing. These are later patterns; follow the link when you are ready to compare them.

07

Lend / Borrow · Teach / Learn

The person’s role depends on the verb

Provide / give access Receive / learn
貸かします — lend 借かります — borrow
教おしえます — teach / tell 習ならいます — learn from someone

わたしは アンさんに 辞書じしょを 貸かしました。

I lent Ann a dictionary.

アンさんは わたしに 辞書じしょを 借かりました。

Ann borrowed a dictionary from me.

わたしは アンさんに 日本語にほんごを 教おしえます。

I teach Ann Japanese.

アンさんは わたしに 日本語にほんごを 習ならいます。

Ann learns Japanese from me.

The thing and the person have different roles

辞書じしょを = what is lent / borrowed.
日本語にほんごを = what is taught / learned.
アンさんに教おしえます = teach Ann.
先生せんせいに習ならいます = learn from a teacher.

For these receiving / learning patterns, から can also mark the source person: 先生せんせいから借かります・先生せんせいから習ならいます. 習ならいます often describes learning through instruction or practice; it is not a replacement for every English “learn” or “find out.”

08

Have You Done It Yet?

もう〜ましたか and まだです

もう + past form: already / have done

もう昼ひるご飯はんを食たべましたか asks whether lunch has already been eaten. Japanese uses the polite past ました here; it does not need a separate English-style “have + past participle” form.

For “not yet,” the beginner reply is いいえ、まだです. You can then explain what you will do: これから食たべます — “I am going to eat now / after this.”

A：もう 昼ひるご飯はんを 食たべましたか。
B：はい、もう 食たべました。

A: Have you had lunch yet?
B: Yes, I have.

A：もう 宿題しゅくだいを しましたか。
B：いいえ、まだです。これから します。

A: Have you done your homework yet?
B: No, not yet. I am going to do it now.

A：もう その 映画えいがを 見みましたか。
B：はい、見みました。

A: Have you seen that movie yet?
B: Yes, I have.

Not yet is different from a past negative

まだです leaves the action unfinished at the current reference point; it may happen later. 食たべませんでした simply says the eating did not happen in a past situation, so it is not the default answer for “not yet.”

You will later learn the fuller form まだ食たべていません. For this lesson, practise まだです. Also distinguish もう食たべました (“I have already eaten”) from もう食たべません (“I will not eat any more”): the verb form changes the meaning.

Continue: まだ〜ていません

まだ食たべていません means “I have not eaten yet.” It describes an action that has not happened up to now. Review 〜ています and the て-form foundation before learning its negative form 〜ていません. Keep まだです as the short Lesson 7 reply.
