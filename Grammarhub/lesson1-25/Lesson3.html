A：トイレは どこですか。
B：あそこです。

A: Where is the restroom?
B: It is over there.

A：受付うけつけは どちらですか。
B：こちらです。

A: Where is reception?
B: This way / over here.

Location sentences use です

トイレはあそこです identifies the location of the restroom. You do not need to use あります yet to make this basic location sentence.

Compare これは本ほんです (identify a thing) with ここは教室きょうしつです (identify a place).

02

ここ・そこ・あそこ

Look at the place from the speaker’s viewpoint

Look at the Place — A Is Speaking

The pin marks the place. A is the speaker; B is the listener. Choose the word from A’s viewpoint.

ここ

here

A · SpeakerB · Listener

A place near me, the speaker.

そこ

there, near you

A · SpeakerB · Listener

A place near you, the listener.

あそこ

over there

A · SpeakerB · Listener

A place far from both of us.

The speaker’s viewpoint matters

A is standing at a counter and says ここは受付うけつけです. B, standing farther away, points to A’s counter and says そこは受付うけつけですか. The place stays the same; the speaker changes.

When both people are together, ここ is nearby, あそこ is farther away, and そこ can refer to an intermediate place or a place already indicated. Physical distance is the starting point, not the only use of こ・そ・あ words.

ここは 教室きょうしつです。

This place is a classroom. / This is the classroom.

そこは 事務所じむしょですか。

Is that place the office?

食堂しょくどうは あそこです。

The cafeteria is over there.

アンさんは どこですか。
会議室かいぎしつです。

Where is Ann?
In the meeting room.

03

こちら・そちら・あちら

Places, directions, people, and sides

Places and directions — more polite than ここ / そこ / あそこ

こちら: here / this way.
そちら: there / that way, near or associated with the listener.
あちら: over there / that way, away from both.

They can indicate an area or direction, not just one exact point. どちら is often a polite counterpart of どこ in a location question.

A：エレベーターは どちらですか。
B：あちらです。

A: Where is the elevator?
B: Over there.

どうぞ、こちらへ。

This way, please. — the movement verb is understood.

People and groups — my / our side, your side, the other side

The meaning can expand from a place to a person, organization, or side in the conversation. This is especially useful in phone calls and work-related exchanges.

Word Side / person interpretation How to understand it
こちら my side / our side; I / we in context The speaker or the speaker’s group. Not a literal possessive “my” in every sentence.
そちら your side; you / your organization in context The listener, their location, or their group.
あちら the other side / that party; their side in context A third party or a person / group being indicated. It is not a universal substitute for English “they.”

こちら = me / our side

こちらは 田中たなかです。

This is Tanaka speaking. — for example, identifying yourself on the phone.

Speaker’s side

こちらで 確認かくにんします。

We will check on our side. — the speaker’s team or organization.

Listener’s side

そちらの ご都合つごうは いかがですか。

What would be convenient for you / your side?

Third party’s side

あちらの 会社かいしゃは 東京とうきょうに あります。

That company / the other party’s company is in Tokyo. — the third party is clear from context.

A polite introduction can also use こちら

こちらは 田中たなかさんです。 — “This is Mr. / Ms. Tanaka.”

Here こちら introduces a person being indicated. It does not mean “our side” automatically. Compare the phone introduction こちらは田中たなかです, where the speaker identifies themself and does not add さん to their own name.

These are recognition notes beyond the basic Lesson 3 location pattern. You do not need to master the later request and business grammar yet.

04

Country & Company

Ask about origin or affiliation

A：お国くには どちらですか。
B：アメリカです。

A: Which country are you from?
B: The United States.

A：会社かいしゃは どちらですか。
B：さくら電機でんきです。

A: Which company do you work for?
B: Sakura Electric. — a fictional company name.

A：これは どこの 車くるまですか。
B：日本にほんの 車くるまです。

A: What country is this car from?
B: It is a Japanese car.

A：これは どこの コンピューターですか。
B：さくら電機でんきの コンピューターです。

A: Which company makes this computer?
B: Sakura Electric. — company interpretation from context.

What does どちら ask for here?

お国くにはどちらですか asks about someone’s country, not their current physical location. 会社かいしゃはどちらですか can ask the company’s name in a self-introduction; in a different situation it can ask where the company is.

どこの + noun can ask origin, affiliation, or manufacturer. Interpret the question from the topic; どこ does not always require a street address as an answer.

05

Which Floor?

Read the full number + counter

Floors — Number + 階かい

Use 階かい for building floors, and 何階なんがい to ask which floor. The number can change the pronunciation of the counter. In ordinary Japanese floor numbering, the ground-level floor is 1階いっかい; check the local building labels when translating from countries with a separate “ground floor.”

Floor Reading
1階いっかい いっかい
2階にかい にかい
3階さんがい さんがい
4階よんかい よんかい
5階ごかい ごかい
6階ろっかい ろっかい
7階ななかい ななかい
8階はっかい はっかい
9階きゅうかい きゅうかい
10階じゅっかい じゅっかい
何階なんがい なんがい

A：食堂しょくどうは 何階なんがいですか。
B：3階さんがいです。

A: What floor is the cafeteria on?
B: The 3rd floor.

受付うけつけは 1階いっかいです。

Reception is on the 1st floor.

Readings and other counters

3階さんがい is commonly さんがい; さんかい is also used. 10階じゅっかい can be じゅっかい / じっかい. 何階なんがい is normally なんがい. Do not confuse it with 何回なんかい（なんかい）, “how many times.”

06

How Much?

Ask and state prices

Prices — いくらですか

Use いくらですか to ask the price. State the amount with 円えん + です. Japanese 円えん is pronounced えん, without an initial “y” sound.

A：この本ほんは いくらですか。
B：500円ごひゃくえんです。

A: How much is this book?
B: 500 yen.

この かばんは 3,000円さんぜんえんです。

This bag is 3,000 yen.

Number readings also change

300円さんびゃくえん = さんびゃくえん
600円ろっぴゃくえん = ろっぴゃくえん
800円はっぴゃくえん = はっぴゃくえん
3,000円さんぜんえん = さんぜんえん

These are readings of the full amount. Use the quantity / number resource for more practice.
