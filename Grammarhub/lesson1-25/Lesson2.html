Same noun-sentence endings, new ways to refer to things

Keep the basic patterns from Lesson 1: です・じゃありません・ですか. In Lesson 2, you use them to identify objects, ask what they are, and talk about ownership.

This is a book.

02

これ・それ・あれ

Choose from the speaker’s viewpoint

See the Distance — A Is Speaking

Pink marks the speaker A. Purple marks the listener B. Look at where the book is, then choose the word.

これ

この本ほん · this book

A · Speaker (me)B · Listener (you)

The book is near A, the speaker.

これは 本ほんです。

それ

その本ほん · that book near you

A · Speaker (me)B · Listener (you)

The book is near B, the listener.

それは 本ほんです。

あれ

あの本ほん · that book over there

A · Speaker (me)B · Listener (you)

The book is far from both A and B.

あれは 本ほんです。

これ / それ / あれ stand alone. この / その / あの attach to the noun: 本ほん. The distance rule is the same.

Choose according to the object’s location

これ: an object near me, the speaker.
それ: an object near you, the listener.
あれ: an object far from both of us.

These words stand on their own as noun phrases. They do not need a noun immediately after them. When both people are standing together, これ is nearby, あれ is far away, and それ can refer to something at an intermediate distance or something already indicated.

Near the speaker

これは わたしの ペンです。

This is my pen. — I am holding it or it is near me.

Near the listener

それは アンさんの かばんですか。

Is that your bag, Ann? — it is near Ann, the listener.

Far from both

あれは 田中たなかさんの 車くるまです。

That is Tanaka’s car. — it is far from both speakers.

Same Book, Different Speaker

The people and book stay in exactly the same positions. Only the speaker changes.

A is speaking

A：これは 本ほんです。
A: This is a book.

A · near the bookB · away from the book

B is speaking

B：それは 本ほんです。
B: That is a book.

A · near the bookB · away from the book

For A, the book is near me → これ. For B, it is near you → それ. The book does not move. These sentences identify the object without changing its owner.

The word changes when the speaker changes

A is holding a pen and says これはわたしのペンです. B points to the same pen and says それはアンさんのペンですか.

The object did not move. The speaker’s viewpoint changed, so これ became それ. In later lessons, それ can also refer to something just mentioned rather than a physically distant object.

03

この・その・あの

Use a noun after these words

この / その / あの MUST be followed by a noun

これ = this thing.
この本ほん = this book.

この・その・あの attach to a noun and follow the same basic distance pattern as これ・それ・あれ. They can also describe a person: この人ひと. For actually addressing someone, use their name or an appropriate title, not この人ひと.

Location On its own Before a noun
Near me これ この本ほん
Near you それ その本ほん
Far from both あれ あの本ほん

これは わたしの 本ほんです。
この本ほんは わたしのです。

This is my book.
This book is mine. — the topic is phrased differently.

Common mistakes

✗ このは 本ほんです。
✓ これは 本ほんです。
✗ これ本ほんは わたしのです。
✓ この本ほんは わたしのです。

この needs a noun; これ does not attach directly to 本ほん.

04

Questions & Answers

Confirm or choose an alternative

Confirm an identity — そうです / 違ちがいます

For questions that identify an object, はい、そうです means “Yes, that is right.” いいえ、違ちがいます means “No, that is not right.” You can also repeat the noun sentence.

A：それは かぎですか。
B：はい、そうです。

A: Is that a key?
B: Yes, that is right.

A：それは かぎですか。
B：いいえ、違ちがいます。ペンです。

A: Is that a key?
B: No, it is not. It is a pen.

Choose between 2 alternatives

A：これは「1」ですか、「7」ですか。
B：「7」です。

A: Is this a “1” or a “7”?
B: It is a “7.”

A：それは ペンですか、えんぴつですか。
B：えんぴつです。

A: Is that a pen or a pencil?
B: It is a pencil.

Answer the choice, not only yes or no

Aですか、Bですか asks which alternative is correct. Answer with Aです or Bです, not only はい.

そうです confirms the preceding statement. It does not replace every type of “yes,” and it is not an answer naming the object in これは何なんですか.

05

Whose Is It?

Ownership, の, and repeated nouns

の can show ownership or the topic of an item

わたしの本ほん = my book.
日本語にほんごの本ほん = a Japanese-language book / a book about Japanese.

The relationship depends on the nouns. の does not always mean ownership, and it is not a universal connector after verbs or adjectives.

これは アンさんの 本ほんです。

This is Ann’s book.

A：これは だれの 本ほんですか。
B：田中たなかさんの 本ほんです。

A: Whose book is this?
B: It is Tanaka’s book.

A：この本ほんは だれのですか。
B：わたしのです。

A: Whose is this book?
B: It is mine.

A：これは 何なんの 本ほんですか。
B：コンピューターの 本ほんです。

A: What is this book about?
B: It is a book about computers.

You may omit the repeated noun when ownership is clear

わたしの本ほんです → わたしのです

When the item is already clear, の can stand for “my one / mine.” Keep の: ✗ この本ほんはわたしです does not mean “This book is mine.”

Do not use that omission automatically for every relationship. To say the book is about Japanese, use 日本語にほんごの本ほんです; simply 日本語にほんごのです is not the basic pattern for naming its subject matter.

06

Useful Expressions

Short exchanges in daily life

A：あのう、すみません。それは わたしの かばんです。
B：あ、すみません。

A: Um, excuse me. That is my bag.
B: Oh, I am sorry.

A：どうぞ。
B：どうも ありがとうございます。

A: Here you are.
B: Thank you very much.

あのう as a hesitation word

あのう… means something like “um…” when you hesitate or attract attention. It is different from あの本ほん, where あの means “that” before a noun.
