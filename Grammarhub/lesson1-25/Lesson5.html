02

Go, Come & Return

Choose the movement verb from the speaker’s viewpoint

Three different movement verbs

行いきます = go to another place.
来きます = come toward the speaker’s location, or a viewpoint established in context.
帰かえります = return / go home to a place you belong, such as home or your home country.

帰かえります includes the idea of returning. It is not a substitute for every use of English “go back”: returning to a shop, for example, usually uses a different verb.

明日あした、京都きょうとへ 行いきます。

I will go to Kyoto tomorrow.

来週らいしゅう、友達ともだちが 日本にほんへ 来きます。

My friend will come to Japan next week. — the speaker is in Japan.

午後ごご6時じに 家うちへ 帰かえります。

I will go home at 6 p.m.

来月らいげつ、国くにへ 帰かえります。

I will return to my home country next month.

English “I’m coming” does not automatically become 来きます

Imagine you are outside Japan, talking to your Japanese teacher in Japan. English can say “I’m coming to Japan,” taking the listener’s viewpoint. In an ordinary Japanese sentence describing your trip, say 日本にほんへ行いきます.

✓ 来月らいげつ、日本にほんへ行いきます。 — I am going to Japan next month.
Your teacher, who is in Japan, can describe your trip with 来きます: アンさんは来月らいげつ、日本にほんへ来きます。

The same trip has different wording because the speaker’s location changes. Do not choose 来きます simply because the English sentence uses “come.” Japanese can adopt another viewpoint in some contexts, but this basic speaker-based distinction is the useful starting point here.

Polite forms and the dictionary forms

行いきます → 行いく · 来きます → 来くる · 帰かえります → 帰かえる

The lesson uses polite ます forms. For a longer explanation of these verbs and purpose expressions such as “go to buy,” see the related grammar resource below. The purpose pattern itself is introduced later, in Lesson 13.

03

Where? — へ

Destinations and negative answers

へ marks the destination; pronounce it “e”

In 京都きょうとへ行いきます, 京都きょうと is the destination. The particle へ is pronounced e, not “he.” Use the same destination pattern with 来きます and 帰かえります.

For these basic destination sentences, に is also possible: 京都きょうとに行いきます. へ emphasizes the direction toward a place; に can mark the destination / arrival point. At this stage, recognize both and practise the Lesson 5 pattern with へ.

A：どこへ 行いきますか。
B：学校がっこうへ 行いきます。

A: Where are you going?
B: To school.

A：昨日きのう、どこへ 行いきましたか。
B：大阪おおさかへ 行いきました。

A: Where did you go yesterday?
B: To Osaka.

A：明日あした、どこへ 行いきますか。
B：どこへも 行いきません。

A: Where will you go tomorrow?
B: I will not go anywhere.

どこへも + negative

どこへも行いきません means “I do not / will not go anywhere.” For a past event, use どこへも行いきませんでした. The negative verb is essential; も here does not simply mean “also.” You will also hear どこにも行いきません.

に or へ? — Same Trip, Different Focus

Both sentences mean “I go / will go to school.” The arrow and the destination pin describe the same trip. Only the focus changes. に does not mean the person has already arrived, and へ does not mean the person will stop before reaching the school.

With 行いきます・来きます・帰かえります, both particles are natural in basic destination sentences. This does not make them interchangeable in every construction: time expressions and existence sentences, for example, use に, not へ.

Continue with Go · Come · Return — destinations and movement, or review に・で — time, location, and means.

04

How? — で

Transport and going on foot

Transport + で

で marks the means of transport: 電車でんしゃで = by train; バスで = by bus. It tells you how you travel, while へ tells you where you go.

電車でんしゃで 東京とうきょうへ 行いきます。

I will go to Tokyo by train.

バスで 学校がっこうへ 行いきます。

I go to school by bus.

A：何なにで 京都きょうとへ 行いきますか。
B：新幹線しんかんせんで 行いきます。

A: How will you get to Kyoto?
B: By Shinkansen.

歩あるいて 家うちへ 帰かえります。

I will walk home.

Walking is the exception to the transport noun pattern

Say 歩あるいて (on foot), not 歩あるいてで. 歩あるいて is a verb form; you do not need to learn the full て-form system yet to use this expression.

Other transport words: 地下鉄ちかてつ・飛行機ひこうき・自転車じてんしゃ・車くるま・タクシー.

In a transport question, 何なにで can be read なんで or the clearer なにで. Here it asks “by what means?” Context distinguishes it from conversational なんで meaning “why?”

05

With Whom? — と

Companions and travelling alone

Person + と = with someone

Use と after a person or group: 友達ともだちと = with a friend / friends; 家族かぞくと = with family. Ask だれと to find out who goes with someone.

Use 一人ひとりで to say “alone / by myself.” Do not add と after it.

友達ともだちと 京都きょうとへ 行いきます。

I will go to Kyoto with a friend / friends.

家族かぞくと 国くにへ 帰かえります。

I will return to my home country with my family.

A：だれと 行いきますか。
B：一人ひとりで 行いきます。

A: Who will you go with?
B: I will go alone.

来週らいしゅう、友達ともだちと 電車でんしゃで 京都きょうとへ 行いきます。

Next week, I will go to Kyoto by train with a friend / friends.

Build a complete trip sentence

来週らいしゅう (when) + 友達ともだちと (with whom) + 電車でんしゃで (how) + 京都きょうとへ (where) + 行いきます (movement).

These phrases can move around when the meaning stays clear, but each particle must stay attached to its own phrase. End the sentence with the verb. Leave out details that are already understood.

06

When? — いつ

Specific dates and relative time words

いつ = when?

Use いつ to ask generally when an event happens. Do not add に: いつ行いきますか.

A specific date can take に: 7月しちがつ20日はつかに行いきます. Relative words such as 明日あした・来週らいしゅう・来月らいげつ・来年らいねん normally do not take に. Compare the clock-time pattern in Lesson 4.

A：いつ 日本にほんへ 行いきますか。
B：来月らいげつ 行いきます。

A: When will you go to Japan?
B: Next month.

7月しちがつ20日はつかに 日本にほんへ 行いきます。

I will go to Japan on July 20.

A：いつ 日本にほんへ 来きましたか。
B：先月せんげつ 来きました。

A: When did you come to Japan?
B: Last month. — both speakers are now in Japan.

A：誕生日たんじょうびは いつですか。
B：4月しがつ8日ようかです。

A: When is your birthday?
B: April 8. — this is a noun + です sentence, so no に is needed.

07

Months & Dates

A complete reading reference

Months and dates use special readings

4月しがつ = しがつ; 7月しちがつ = しちがつ; 9月くがつ = くがつ. For dates, learn the whole expression: 1日ついたち is ついたち, not いちにち. The reading いちにち refers to a duration of one day.

Ask 何月なんがつ for “which month?” and 何日なんにち for “which date?” Read a full date in month–day order. Notice 14日じゅうよっか・20日はつか・24日にじゅうよっか as well as dates 1–10.

Conversation & Related Grammar

Useful information with よ

よ: give information or draw attention to it

よ can signal information you expect to be useful or new to the listener. It is not a question marker and does not need to be added to every statement. Tone and context matter: it can sound helpful, emphatic, or insistent.

この 電車でんしゃは 京都きょうとへ 行いきますよ。

This train goes to Kyoto. — giving the listener useful information.
