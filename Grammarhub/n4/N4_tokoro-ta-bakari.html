N4 GRAMMAR · ACTION STAGES & RECENT EVENTS

〜ところ・〜たばかり

About to Do · Doing Now · Just Finished · Recently Did

Understand exactly when an action happens with 〜るところ, 〜ているところ, and 〜たところ. Learn why 〜たばかり can mean “recently” even when the event was days or weeks ago.

01 The Big Picture — Before, During, After & Recently

〜ところ tells us exactly which stage an action is at: just before, right in the middle, or just after it. 〜たばかり means something happened recently from the speaker’s point of view.

① About to do

これからごはんを食たべるところです。
I am just about to eat.

② In the middle

今いま、ごはんを食たべているところです。
I am eating right now.

③ Just finished

たった今いま、ごはんを食たべたところです。
I have just finished eating.

④ Recently did

日本にほんに来きたばかりです。
I have only recently arrived in Japan.
The most important difference

〜たところ focuses on the moment immediately after an action. 〜たばかり focuses on how recent an action feels. “Recently” can mean days, weeks, or even longer depending on the situation.

02 How to Form 〜ところ and 〜たばかり

All four patterns attach to a verb, but the verb form changes the meaning. Do not put ます before ところ or ばかり. Keep です at the end for polite speech.

Verb dictionary form + ところです = about to do
Verb て-form + いる + ところです = doing right now
Verb た-form + ところです = just did
Verb た-form + ばかりです = did recently

食たべます — to eat

食たべるところ → 食たべているところ → 食たべたところ
About to eat → eating now → just ate.

書かきます — to write

書かくところ → 書かいているところ → 書かいたところ
About to write → writing now → just wrote.

読よみます — to read

読よむところ → 読よんでいるところ → 読よんだところ
About to read → reading now → just read.

始はじめます — to start

始はじめたばかりです。
I only recently started.

Why ている in the middle?

〜ている expresses an action in progress. Adding ところ emphasizes that the action is happening at this very moment.

03 Verb Dictionary Form + ところ — Just About to Do

Use the dictionary form + ところ when you are just about to start. The action has not started yet. It often appears with これから (from now) or ちょうど (just).

Verb dictionary form + ところです
これから出でかけるところです。
I am just about to go out.
ちょうど映画えいがを見みるところです。
I am just about to watch a movie.
今いまから宿題しゅくだいを始はじめるところです。
I am about to start my homework.
電車でんしゃが出発しゅっぱつするところです。
The train is about to depart.
Before the action

今いまから食たべるところです = I am about to eat (not eating yet). 今いま食たべているところです = I am eating now (already started).

04 Verb ている + ところ — Right in the Middle

Use 〜ているところ when an action is happening right now. This makes the “right now” feeling stronger than ordinary 〜ています.

Verb て-form + いる + ところです
今いま、料理りょうりを作つくっているところです。
I am cooking right now.
先生せんせいと話はなしているところです。
I am in the middle of talking with my teacher.
今いま、会議かいぎをしているところです。
We are in a meeting right now.
今いま、本ほんを読よんでいるところです。
I am reading a book at the moment.
Not every 〜ている describes an ongoing action

東京とうきょうに住すんでいます = I live in Tokyo (a continuing state). You would not normally say 東京とうきょうに住すんでいるところです just to state where you live. Use 〜ているところ for an action actually in progress.

05 Verb た-Form + ところ — Just Finished

Use 〜たところ when something happened immediately before the moment you are talking about. It emphasizes the moment right after the action.

Verb た-form + ところです
たった今いま、家いえに帰かえったところです。
I have just got home.
ちょうど仕事しごとが終おわったところです。
I have just finished work.
さっき駅えきに着ついたところです。
I just arrived at the station.
今いま、宿題しゅくだいを出だしたところです。
I have just handed in my homework.
An immediate moment, not a long period

先月せんげつ、日本にほんに来きたところです sounds unnatural if you mean “I arrived last month and I am still new here.” Say 先月せんげつ、日本にほんに来きたばかりです instead.

06 Verb た-Form + ばかり — Only Recently Did

Use 〜たばかり when an action or change feels recent. It does not have to mean “just a few seconds ago.” The time period depends on the situation and the speaker.

Verb た-form + ばかりです
日本語にほんごの勉強べんきょうを始はじめたばかりです。
I have only recently started studying Japanese.
先月せんげつ、この町まちに引ひっ越こしたばかりです。
I moved to this town only last month.
この会社かいしゃに入はいったばかりなので、まだ仕事しごとに慣なれていません。
I only recently joined this company, so I am not used to the work yet.
先週せんしゅう買かったばかりなのに、もう壊こわれました。
I bought it only last week, but it has already broken.
“Recently” is subjective

If you moved abroad a month ago, 一いっか月げつ前まえに来きたばかりです can sound natural because one month still feels recent. 〜たところ is much more closely connected to the moment right after the action.

07 〜たところ vs. 〜たばかり — The Key Difference

Both can translate as “just did,” but they emphasize different ideas. Choose based on whether you are talking about the immediate moment or how new/recent the experience feels.

〜たところ: immediately after

たった今いま、昼ひるごはんを食たべたところです。
I have just finished lunch (a moment ago).

〜たばかり: feels recent

日本にほんに来きたばかりです。
I have only recently come to Japan (I still feel new here).

〜たところ: exact timing

ちょうど会議かいぎが終おわったところです。
The meeting has just ended.

〜たばかり: recent experience

先月せんげつ、大学だいがくを卒業そつぎょうしたばかりです。
I graduated from university only last month.
Can both be correct?

Yes. たった今いま食たべたところです and たった今いま食たべたばかりです can both be natural. The first emphasizes the moment after finishing; the second emphasizes how little time has passed. Do not treat them as always interchangeable.

08 Time Expressions That Help You Choose

Time words are useful clues, although the meaning depends on context. Think about which stage of the action you want to describe.

これから / 今いまから — BEFORE

これから勉強べんきょうするところです。
I am about to study.

今いま / ちょうど — DURING

今いま、勉強べんきょうしているところです。
I am studying right now.

たった今いま / ちょうど — AFTER

たった今いま、勉強べんきょうが終おわったところです。
I have just finished studying.

先週せんしゅう / 先月せんげつ — RECENT

先週せんしゅう、新あたらしい仕事しごとを始はじめたばかりです。
I started a new job only last week.
Do not use time words mechanically

たった今いま can work with both 〜たところ and 〜たばかり. With 先月せんげつ, 〜たばかり is usually more natural when you want to say that an experience is still new.

09 〜たばかりの NOUN / 〜たばかりなのに

〜たばかり is not limited to the end of a sentence. You can put の + NOUN after it, or use なのに to express surprise that something happened so soon.

Verb た-form + ばかりの + NOUN = a NOUN that was just…
Verb た-form + ばかりなのに + result = even though it was only recently…
買かったばかりの靴くつが汚よごれました。
The shoes I just bought got dirty.
買かったばかりのパソコンが壊こわれました。
The computer I only recently bought broke.
入はいったばかりなのに、もう仕事しごとを辞やめたいです。
Even though I only recently joined, I already want to quit.
食たべたばかりなのに、もうおなかがすきました。
Even though I just ate, I am already hungry.
Why の / なのに?

〜たばかりの + NOUN describes a noun. 〜たばかりなのに connects the recent event to an unexpected result. The 〜た form before ばかり stays the same.

10 Natural Everyday Conversations

These patterns are especially useful when explaining why you cannot do something yet, telling someone what you are doing, or saying you are new to an activity.

A：もう出でかけますか。
B：はい、ちょうど出でかけるところです。
A: Are you leaving already? B: Yes, I am just about to leave.
A：今いま、何なにをしていますか。
B：晩ばんごはんを作つくっているところです。
A: What are you doing now? B: I am making dinner right now.
A：もう昼ひるごはんを食たべましたか。
B：はい、たった今いま食たべたところです。
A: Have you had lunch? B: Yes, I just finished eating.
A：日本にほんには長ながく住すんでいますか。
B：いいえ、先月せんげつ来きたばかりです。
A: Have you lived in Japan long? B: No, I only arrived last month.

11 Similar Grammar — 〜ている / 〜てから / 〜たあとで

Some expressions talk about an ongoing action, and others talk about what happens after an action. The focus is different from 〜ところ and 〜たばかり.

〜ています — simple ongoing action

今いま、本ほんを読よんでいます。
I am reading a book. A neutral description.

〜ているところ — at this moment

今いま、本ほんを読よんでいるところです。
I am right in the middle of reading.

〜てから — sequence

ごはんを食たべてから、勉強べんきょうします。
I will study after eating. Describes order, not how recently I ate.

〜たあとで — sequence

ごはんを食たべたあとで、勉強べんきょうします。
I will study after eating. Describes what comes next.
A different meaning of 〜たところ

You may later see sentences like 聞きいてみたところ、分わかりました (When I tried asking, I found out). This is a different use of 〜たところ, not the “just finished” meaning taught on this page.

12 Common Mistakes

Before — use dictionary form

✕ 今いまから食たべますところです。
✓ 今いまから食たべるところです。
Use the dictionary form, not the ます-form, before ところ.

During — keep いる

✕ 今いま、食たべてところです。
✓ 今いま、食たべているところです。
Use ているところ for an action in progress.

Recently — use the た-form

✕ 先月せんげつ、来くるばかりです。
✓ 先月せんげつ、来きたばかりです。
Use the た-form before ばかり to mean “only recently did.”

Time expression must fit

✕ これから帰かえったところです。
✓ これから帰かえるところです。
これから means “from now,” so the action has not happened yet.
〜ばかり has other meanings

The word ばかり also appears in other patterns (for example, 食たべてばかりいる = doing nothing but eating). 〜たばかり on this page specifically means “only recently did.”
