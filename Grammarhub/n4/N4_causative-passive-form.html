N4 GRAMMAR · VERB CONJUGATION

使役受身形しえきうけみけい

Be made to do something

Learn how to say you were made to do something. Form the verb, mark who made you act with に, and compare causative and passive.

N4Verb Conjugation使役受身形しえきうけみけい

01 What Is the Causative-Passive Form?

The causative-passive form describes someone being made to do something. The subject is the person who has to perform the action, rather than the person who gives the instruction.

私わたしは先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを書かかせられました。
I was made to write an essay by my teacher.
子こどもは母ははに野菜やさいを食たべさせられました。
The child was made to eat vegetables by their mother.
Key idea

The form often suggests that the action was required, inconvenient, or unwanted. However, it does not automatically mean the person was physically forced. Context matters.

02 How to Make It — Start with ます

Start with the ます-form, just as in the N5 verb-conjugation lessons. First make the causative, then change it to the passive. You can also learn the combined ending directly.

Group 1 — Change the sound before ます to its あ-row sound + せられます

Remove ます and change the last い-row sound to its あ-row sound. For verbs ending in います, use わせられます (い → わ).

書かきます → 書かかせられます
be made to write
読よみます → 読よませられます
be made to read
待まちます → 待またせられます
be made to wait
買かいます → 買かわせられます
be made to buy

Group 2 — Remove ます and add させられます

食たべます → 食たべさせられます
be made to eat
見みます → 見みさせられます
be made to watch

Group 3 — Irregular verbs

します → させられます
be made to do
来きます → 来こさせられます
be made to come
Remember

The reading changes in 来きます → 来こさせられます. For Group 2, keep the entire させられます ending.

03 Shortened Group 1 Forms

Many Group 1 verbs have a commonly used shorter causative-passive form. In these verbs, 〜せられる becomes 〜される. Both versions have the same basic meaning.

書かかせられます → 書かかされます
be made to write
読よませられます → 読よまされます
be made to read
待またせられます → 待またされます
be made to wait
走はしらせられます → 走はしらされます
be made to run
Important exception — verbs ending in す

Do not use this shortening with Group 1 verbs ending in す. For example, 話はなします → 話はなさせられます (not 話はなさされます). Group 2 verbs such as 食たべさせられます also do not use this shortening.

04 Polite and Plain Causative-Passive Forms

Once you have the causative-passive verb, you can make polite, plain, negative, and past forms. The shortened Group 1 version is common in everyday speech.

書かかされます。
I am made to write. (polite)
書かかされる。
I am made to write. (plain)
書かかされません。 / 書かかされない。
I am not made to write. (polite / plain)
書かかされました。 / 書かかされた。
I was made to write. (polite / plain)
Full forms are also correct

You can use 書かかせられます / 書かかせられました instead of the shortened forms above.

05 Who Made You Do It? — PERSON に

The most important particle here is に. In a causative-passive sentence, it commonly marks the person who makes the subject do the action.

PERSON made to act は + PERSON who gives the instruction に + OBJECT を + causative-passive verb
私わたしは先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを書かかされました。
I was made to write an essay by my teacher.
先生せんせいに = who made me do it; 作文さくぶんを = what I wrote.
私わたしは上司じょうしに残業ざんぎょうさせられました。
I was made to work overtime by my boss.
上司じょうしに = who made me do it.

What if there is no direct object?

The person who gives the instruction still commonly takes に in the causative-passive sentence. Compare the particle used for the person doing the action in a basic causative sentence.

先生せんせいは学生がくせいを走はしらせました。
The teacher made the student run. (causative: 学生がくせいを)
学生がくせいは先生せんせいに走はしらされました。
The student was made to run by the teacher. (causative-passive: 先生せんせいに)
Keep the roles separate

In the causative-passive, the subject は is the person made to act; に usually identifies the person who made them do it. If the verb takes an object, that object keeps を.

06 Causative vs. Causative-Passive

The causative describes the person making or letting someone act. The causative-passive describes the person being made to act. The event can be the same, but the viewpoint changes.

Transitive verb — write an essay

先生せんせいは学生がくせいに作文さくぶんを書かかせました。
The teacher made the student write an essay. (causative)
学生がくせいは先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを書かかされました。
The student was made to write an essay by the teacher. (causative-passive)

Intransitive verb — run

父ちちは弟おとうとを走はしらせました。
The father made his younger son run. (causative)
弟おとうとは父ちちに走はしらされました。
The younger brother was made to run by his father. (causative-passive)

07 Passive vs. Causative-Passive

Both forms can use に for the person responsible for the action, but they describe different events.

私わたしは先生せんせいに名前なまえを呼よばれました。
My name was called by the teacher. (passive: something was done to me)
私わたしは先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを書かかされました。
I was made to write an essay by the teacher. (causative-passive: I had to do the action)
Do not confuse the endings

食たべられます can be passive or potential, depending on context. 食たべさせられます is causative-passive: “be made to eat.”

08 Everyday Situations

You will often hear the causative-passive when talking about school, work, family rules, and childhood experiences. It can suggest annoyance or lack of choice, depending on the context.

子こどものころ、毎日まいにち野菜やさいを食たべさせられました。
When I was a child, I was made to eat vegetables every day.
昨日きのうは上司じょうしに遅おそくまで残業ざんぎょうさせられました。
Yesterday, my boss made me work overtime until late.
私わたしは先生せんせいに毎日まいにち漢字かんじを書かかされました。
My teacher made me write kanji every day.
会議かいぎで突然とつぜん意見いけんを言いわされました。
I was unexpectedly made to give my opinion at the meeting.
Nuance

The causative-passive often conveys a burden or lack of choice. For neutral statements about being allowed to do something, a causative construction or another expression may be more natural.

09 Common Mistakes

① Missing the person-marking に

✗ 私わたしは先生せんせいを作文さくぶんを書かかされました。
Incorrect for the intended meaning: the teacher made me write an essay.
✓ 私わたしは先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを書かかされました。
Correct: the teacher is marked with に.

② Shortening the wrong verb

✗ 話はなさされました。
Not the standard causative-passive form of 話はなす.
✓ 話はなさせられました。
Correct: Group 1 verbs ending in す do not take the usual shortening.

③ Forgetting the させ in Group 2

✗ 食たべられました。
This is not causative-passive; it can be passive or potential.
✓ 食たべさせられました。
Correct: I was made to eat.
