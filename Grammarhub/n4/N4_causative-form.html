Causative Form

01 What Is the Causative Form?

The causative form expresses making someone do something or letting someone do something. The same form can mean either one; the situation tells you whether it is an instruction, a requirement, or permission.

母ははは子こどもに野菜やさいを食たべさせました。
The mother made her child eat vegetables.
先生せんせいは学生がくせいを休やすませました。
The teacher let the student take a break.

Important: 〜させる does not always mean “force.” It can also mean “allow” or “let.”

02 How to Make the Causative — Start with ます

Start with the ます-form, just like the N5 verb-conjugation lessons. The ending changes according to the verb group.

Group 1 — Change the sound before ます to its あ-row sound + せます

Remove ます and change the final い-row sound to its あ-row sound, then add せます. For verbs ending in います, use わせます (い → わ).

書かきます → 書かかせます
make / let someone write
読よみます → 読よませます
make / let someone read
待まちます → 待またせます
make someone wait
買かいます → 買かわせます
make / let someone buy

Group 2 — Remove ます and add させます

食たべます → 食たべさせます
make / let someone eat
見みます → 見みさせます
make / let someone watch

Group 3 — Irregular verbs

します → させます
make / let someone do
来きます → 来こさせます
make / let someone come

The causative verb also has a plain form: 食たべさせます → 食たべさせる. The polite ます-form and plain dictionary form express the same causative meaning.

03 The Important Part — PERSON + に / を

In a causative sentence, there are usually two people: the person who makes or lets someone act, and the person who actually does the action. Pay attention to the particle after the person who does the action.

Transitive verb (someone does something to an object):

Person who causes the action は + PERSON に + OBJECT を + causative verb

The object already takes を, so the person doing the action is commonly marked with に.

母ははは子こどもに野菜やさいを食たべさせました。
Mother made her child eat vegetables.

PERSON に = 子こどもに · OBJECT を = 野菜やさいを

先生せんせいは学生がくせいに作文さくぶんを書かかせました。
The teacher made the student write an essay.

PERSON に = 学生がくせいに · OBJECT を = 作文さくぶんを

Intransitive verb (no direct object):

Person who causes the action は + PERSON を + causative verb

With many intransitive verbs, the person doing the action is marked with を.

父ちちは弟おとうとを走はしらせました。
My father made my younger brother run.

PERSON を = 弟おとうとを

先生せんせいは学生がくせいを立たたせました。
The teacher made the student stand up.

PERSON を = 学生がくせいを

Remember the difference

PERSON に + THING を + transitive causative

PERSON を + intransitive causative

These are the basic N4 patterns, not an absolute rule for every causative sentence. Other constructions and verb meanings can change particle choice.

04 Compare — Who Does What?

Compare an ordinary sentence with a causative sentence. In the causative, someone else controls or permits the action.

子こどもは野菜やさいを食たべました。
The child ate vegetables. (ordinary sentence)
母ははは子こどもに野菜やさいを食たべさせました。
The mother made the child eat vegetables. (causative)
学生がくせいは休やすみました。
The student took a break. (ordinary sentence)
先生せんせいは学生がくせいを休やすませました。
The teacher let the student take a break. (causative)

05 Asking for Permission — 〜させてください

Use causative て-form + ください to ask someone to let you do something. It is often used for requests at work or school.

帰かえります → 帰かえらせます → 帰かえらせてください
少すこし考かんがえさせてください。
Please let me think for a moment.
今日きょうは早はやく帰かえらせてください。
Please let me go home early today.
一ひとつ質問しつもんさせてください。
Please let me ask one question.

〜させていただけませんか is a more formal way to ask permission, especially in business situations.
