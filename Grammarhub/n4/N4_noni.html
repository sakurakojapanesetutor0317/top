N4 GRAMMAR · CONTRAST & PURPOSE

〜のに

Although / Even though · For doing / To do

One expression, two meanings. Learn the difference between an unexpected contrast and what is useful or necessary for an action, with clear forms and examples.

01 Two Meanings of 〜のに

The same expression, 〜のに, has two important uses in N4 Japanese. The meaning depends on the sentence around it. One expresses an unexpected contrast (“although / even though”). The other expresses what an action requires, is useful for, or takes (“for doing / to do”).

A · Although / Even though

雨あめが降ふっているのに、弟おとうとは出でかけました。
Although it is raining, my younger brother went out.

B · For doing / To do

このアプリは漢字かんじを覚おぼえるのに便利べんりです。
This app is useful for learning kanji.

A · Unexpected result

勉強べんきょうしたのに、試験しけんに落おちました。
I studied, but I failed the exam.

B · Time needed

駅えきまで歩あるくのに15分ふんかかります。
It takes 15 minutes to walk to the station.
How to tell them apart

If the second part goes against what you expected, it is the “although” meaning. If the second part says something is useful, necessary, used, or takes time/money for an action, it is the “for doing” meaning.

02 Although: How to Connect 〜のに

For the although / even though meaning, use the plain form with verbs and い-adjectives. With な-adjectives and NOUNs, add な before のに. Do not use です or だ directly before のに in this pattern.

Verb plain form + のに
行いくのに / 行いったのに / 行いかないのに
い-Adjective plain form + のに
高たかいのに / 高たかくないのに / 高たかかったのに
な-Adjective + な + のに
静しずかなのに / 静しずかじゃないのに
NOUN + な + のに
学生がくせいなのに / 学生がくせいじゃないのに
忙いそがしいのに、手伝てつだってくれました。
Although they were busy, they helped me.
日曜日にちようびなのに、仕事しごとがあります。
Even though it is Sunday, I have work.
静しずかなのに、眠ねむれません。
Although it is quiet, I cannot sleep.
安やすかったのに、買かいませんでした。
Although it was cheap, I did not buy it.

03 Although: Verbs, Past, and Negative Forms

You can use present, past, and negative plain forms before 〜のに. The first part states a fact; the second part shows something surprising or contrary to expectations.

毎日まいにち勉強べんきょうしているのに、まだ話はなせません。
Even though I study every day, I still cannot speak.
早はやく起おきたのに、電車でんしゃに遅おくれました。
Although I woke up early, I missed the train.
何なにも食たべていないのに、おなかがすきません。
Even though I have not eaten anything, I am not hungry.
昨日きのう買かったのに、もう壊こわれました。
I only bought it yesterday, but it has already broken.
Past form does not change the meaning of のに

買かったのに = “although I bought it.” 買かうのに = “although I buy it / am going to buy it” or “for buying it,” depending on the rest of the sentence.

04 Although: Adjectives and NOUNs

For い-adjectives, connect the plain form directly. For な-adjectives and NOUNs, use なのに in the present affirmative. Negative forms such as じゃないのに do not need another な.

い-Adjective

この店みせは安やすいのに、おいしいです。
Although this restaurant is cheap, the food is good.

な-Adjective

簡単かんたんなのに、間違まちがえました。
Even though it was easy, I made a mistake.

NOUN

休やすみなのに、会社かいしゃに行いきました。
Although it was my day off, I went to work.

Negative NOUN

学生がくせいじゃないのに、学生がくせい料金りょうきんで入はいれました。
Although I am not a student, I was allowed in at the student price.
Important: な is not optional

✕ 学生がくせいのに / 便利べんりのに → ✓ 学生がくせいなのに / 便利べんりなのに. For this concessive meaning, the present affirmative NOUN and な-adjective forms need な.

05 Although: Surprise, Disappointment, and Regret

The contrast meaning often carries the speaker’s feeling that something did not turn out as expected. This makes 〜のに especially common when complaining or expressing disappointment.

一生懸命いっしょうけんめい勉強べんきょうしたのに、合格ごうかくできませんでした。
I studied so hard, but I did not pass.
約束やくそくしたのに、来きませんでした。
They promised, but they did not come.
せっかく作つくったのに、誰だれも食たべませんでした。
I went to the trouble of making it, but nobody ate it.
もう夜よるの11時じなのに、まだ仕事しごとをしています。
It is already 11 p.m., but I am still working.
〜のに at the end of a sentence

You may hear “約束やくそくしたのに…” (“But you promised…”). Leaving the rest unsaid can express disappointment or frustration. The listener understands the unexpected result from the context.

06 Compare 〜のに, 〜けど, and 〜ても

All three can connect contrasting ideas, but they are not always interchangeable. 〜けど is a general “but.” 〜のに highlights an unexpected contrast. 〜ても often means “even if,” including hypothetical situations.

〜けど · Neutral contrast

雨あめが降ふっていますけど、出でかけます。
It is raining, but I am going out. A relatively neutral contrast.

〜のに · Unexpected contrast

雨あめが降ふっているのに、弟おとうとは出でかけました。
Although it is raining, my younger brother went out. The speaker highlights the unexpected choice.

〜ても · Even if

雨あめが降ふっても、出でかけます。
Even if it rains, I will go out. This can describe a future possibility.

〜ので · Because

雨あめが降ふっているので、出でかけません。
Because it is raining, I am not going out. This gives a reason, not a contrast.
A useful distinction

雨あめが降ふっているのに = the rain is a fact. 雨あめが降ふっても = rain may happen, or the speaker is speaking generally. In some contexts 〜ても also refers to facts, so focus on the sentence meaning, not just tense.

07 For Doing: 〜のに with Use and Purpose

The second meaning of 〜のに is for doing something. It commonly appears with words such as 便利べんり (convenient), 必要ひつよう (necessary), 使つかう (use), 役やくに立たつ (be useful). Use the verb dictionary form before のに.

Verb dictionary form + のに + 便利べんり / 必要ひつよう / 使つかう / 役やくに立たつ
読よむのに便利べんりです。
勉強べんきょうするのに必要ひつようです。
紙かみを切きるのに使つかいます。
このアプリは漢字かんじを覚おぼえるのに便利べんりです。
This app is convenient for memorizing kanji.
日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうするのに、辞書じしょが必要ひつようです。
A dictionary is necessary for studying Japanese.
このはさみは紙かみを切きるのに使つかいます。
These scissors are used for cutting paper.
この本ほんは漢字かんじを覚おぼえるのに役やくに立たちます。
This book is useful for learning kanji.
The verb form is important

For this use, start with the dictionary form: 読よむのに / 作つくるのに / 話はなすのに. Do not start with ます: ✕ 読よみますのに → ✓ 読よむのに.

08 For Doing: Time and Money Needed

Use Verb dictionary form + のに + 時間じかん / お金かねがかかる to explain how long or how much an action takes. The action may be in the past; the verb before のに still uses the dictionary form.

Verb dictionary form + のに + 時間じかん / お金かねがかかる
駅えきまで歩あるくのに15分ふんかかります。
この本ほんを読よむのに3時間じかんかかりました。
駅えきまで歩あるくのに15分ふんかかります。
It takes 15 minutes to walk to the station.
この本ほんを読よむのに3時間じかんかかりました。
It took three hours to read this book.
パスポートを作つくるのに、いくらかかりますか。
How much does it cost to get a passport?
この仕事しごとを終おわらせるのに、2日ふつかかかりました。
It took two days to finish this work.
Past result, dictionary-form action

読よむのに3時間じかんかかりました is correct. The past tense appears in かかりました, while the action before のに remains 読よむ.

09 〜のに vs. 〜ために — Not Always the Same

Both can be translated as “for” or “in order to,” but their focus is different. 〜ために commonly explains why someone deliberately takes an action. The “for doing” 〜のに commonly explains what is useful, necessary, used, or how much time/money is required for an action.

〜ために · Intentional goal

日本にほんに留学りゅうがくするために、お金かねを貯ためています。
I am saving money in order to study abroad in Japan.

〜のに · What is needed

日本にほんに留学りゅうがくするのに、お金かねが必要ひつようです。
Money is needed to study abroad in Japan.

〜ために · Purpose of an action

日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうするために、このアプリを使つかっています。
I use this app in order to study Japanese.

〜のに · Usefulness of a tool

このアプリは日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうするのに便利べんりです。
This app is useful for studying Japanese.
Not a rigid one-to-one rule

Some sentences allow both 〜ために and 〜のに with a different emphasis. Learn the typical combinations: 〜ために + deliberate action; 〜のに + 便利べんり / 必要ひつよう / かかる.

10 Same 〜のに, Different Meaning — Read the Context

The dictionary form + のに can appear in both meanings. Do not decide the meaning from the verb form alone. Look at the second part of the sentence.

Although

日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうするのに、全然ぜんぜん上達じょうたつしません。
Even though I study Japanese, I am not improving at all.

For studying

日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうするのに、この本ほんは便利べんりです。
This book is useful for studying Japanese.

Although

毎日まいにち歩あるくのに、疲つかれやすいです。
Although I walk every day, I get tired easily.

Time required

学校がっこうまで歩あるくのに20分ぷんかかります。
It takes 20 minutes to walk to school.
Quick test

Ask: “Is the second part a surprising result?” → although. “Does it describe a tool, requirement, time, or cost?” → for doing. This is more reliable than memorizing only the connection form.

11 NOUN + に for Use or Purpose

For the “useful for / needed for” meaning, a NOUN often connects with に, not のに. Compare a NOUN phrase with a verb phrase.

Verb + のに

旅行りょこうするのに便利べんりです。
It is convenient for traveling.

NOUN + に

旅行りょこうに便利べんりです。
It is convenient for travel.

Verb + のに

日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうするのに役やくに立たちます。
It is useful for studying Japanese.

NOUN + に

日本語にほんごの勉強べんきょうに役やくに立たちます。
It is useful for Japanese study.
Do not mix the two patterns

✕ 旅行りょこうのに便利べんり → ✓ 旅行りょこうに便利べんり / 旅行りょこうするのに便利べんり. Here, the noun 旅行りょこう and the verb 旅行りょこうする need different connections.

12 Common Mistakes

NOUN + なのに (although)

✕ 学生がくせいのに
✓ 学生がくせいなのに
Use な after an affirmative NOUN for the “although” meaning.

な-Adjective + なのに (although)

✕ 便利べんりのに
✓ 便利べんりなのに
Use な after an affirmative な-adjective.

Verb dictionary form (for doing)

✕ 読よみますのに便利べんり
✓ 読よむのに便利べんり
Use the dictionary form for the “for doing” pattern.

NOUN + に (usefulness)

✕ 旅行りょこうのに便利べんり
✓ 旅行りょこうに便利べんり
With a NOUN such as travel, use に for usefulness.
One more common confusion

〜ので means “because”; 〜のに often means “although.” 雨あめが降ふっているので、出でかけません (because it is raining). 雨あめが降ふっているのに、弟おとうとは出でかけました (although it is raining). Note: after 〜のに, do not put your own request, invitation, or intention — ✕ 雨あめが降ふっているのに、出でかけましょう.

13 Everyday Conversations

Notice whether the conversation expresses an unexpected result or what is useful or required.

A：昨日きのうの試験しけんはどうでしたか。
B：勉強べんきょうしたのに、難むずかしかったです。
A: How was yesterday’s exam? B: I studied, but it was difficult.
A：このアプリはどうですか。
B：漢字かんじを覚おぼえるのに便利べんりですよ。
A: How is this app? B: It is convenient for learning kanji.
A：駅えきまで歩あるくのに、どのくらいかかりますか。
B：10分ぷんぐらいです。
A: How long does it take to walk to the station? B: About ten minutes.
A：今日きょうは休やすみですか。
B：はい。休やすみなのに、仕事しごとのメールが来きます。
A: Are you off today? B: Yes. Even though it is my day off, I am getting work emails.
