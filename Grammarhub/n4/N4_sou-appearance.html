N4 GRAMMAR · APPEARANCE

〜そうだ (Appearance)

Looks / seems / appears to be · Looks about to happen

Describe an impression based on what you can see, or an event that looks likely to happen soon. Learn how this differs from hearsay 〜そうだ.

N4AppearanceAdjectives & verbs

01 What Does Appearance 〜そうだ Mean?

Use 〜そうだ to say how something looks based on visible signs. With an adjective, it describes an impression. With a verb, it often means that something looks about to happen.

Adjective stem / verb ます-stem + そうだ / そうです

① An impression — looks / seems

このケーキはおいしそうです。
This cake looks delicious.

② Something likely to happen soon

雨あめが降ふりそうです。
It looks like it is going to rain.
Appearance, not a confirmed fact

You are describing what the situation looks like, not necessarily what is true. 〜そうだ is also used for hearsay, but its connection rules are different.

02 Adjectives — Remove い or な

For い-adjectives, remove the final い. For な-adjectives, use the adjective stem without な, then add そうです.

い-adjective: おいしい → おいしそうです
な-adjective: 元気げんき（な） → 元気げんきそうです
高たかい → 高たかそうです
looks expensive
難むずかしい → 難むずかしそうです
looks difficult
元気げんきな → 元気げんきそうです
looks energetic / well
静しずかな → 静しずかそうです
looks quiet
Do not add な before そう

✕ 元気げんきなそうです。
✓ 元気げんきそうです。 — Looks energetic.

03 Verbs — Start with the ます-Form

For verbs, remove ます and add そうです. This usually describes something that seems likely to happen soon, not a general habit.

Verb ます-stem + そうです
降ふります → 降ふりそうです
雨あめが降ふりそうです。
It looks like it is going to rain.
赤あかちゃんが泣なきそうです。
The baby looks about to cry.
このコップは落おちそうです。
This glass looks like it is about to fall.
電車でんしゃがもう出発しゅっぱつしそうです。
The train looks about to depart.
Be careful with the meaning

食たべそうです means “looks about to eat / looks likely to eat.” If you mean “I heard someone will eat,” use 食たべるそうです (hearsay).

04 Two Important Exceptions — いい and ない

The adjective いい and the negative adjective ない do not follow the usual pattern.

いい → よさそう

このホテルはよさそうです。
This hotel looks good.

ない → なさそう

時間じかんがなさそうです。
It looks like there is no time.
Remember

✕ いそうです (for “looks good”) → ✓ よさそうです
✕ ないそうです (for “looks absent”) → ✓ なさそうです

ないそうです can be grammatical as hearsay (“I heard there isn’t any”), but it does not mean “looks absent.”

05 Negative Impressions — Doesn’t Look …

To say something does not look a certain way, use a negative adjective form before そう, or negate そうです. With verbs, 〜そうにない means something does not seem likely to happen.

このケーキはおいしくなさそうです。
This cake doesn’t look tasty.
この店みせは静しずかじゃなさそうです。
This shop doesn’t look quiet. (conversational)
今日きょうは雨あめが降ふりそうにありません。
It doesn’t look like it will rain today.
この料理りょうりはおいしそうではありません。
This dish does not look delicious. (formal)
A useful distinction

おいしくなさそう directly describes an unappetizing appearance. おいしそうじゃない negates the impression “looks delicious.” Both are possible, but the nuance can differ.

06 Before Nouns, with Verbs, and in the Past

〜そうだ can change form. Use 〜そうな before a noun and 〜そうに to describe how an action is done. Use 〜そうだった for a past impression.

おいしそうなケーキ
a delicious-looking cake
彼女かのじょは楽たのしそうに話はなしています。
She is talking happily / looking like she is having fun.
昨日きのう、彼かれは眠ねむたそうでした。
He looked sleepy yesterday.
階段かいだんで転ころびそうになりました。
I almost fell on the stairs.
Almost happened: 〜そうになる

転ころびそうになりました means “I nearly fell,” not that I actually fell. This pattern is useful for near misses.

07 Appearance vs. Hearsay — Same そうだ, Different Rule

Both are written 〜そうだ, but they express different sources of information and use different verb and adjective forms.

Appearance — what you observe

雨あめが降ふりそうです。
It looks like it will rain. (You see dark clouds.)

Hearsay — what you heard

雨あめが降ふるそうです。
I heard it will rain. (Someone said so / a forecast said so.)

Appearance — adjective stem

このケーキはおいしそうです。
This cake looks delicious.

Hearsay — complete adjective

このケーキはおいしいそうです。
I heard this cake is delicious.
Remember the connection

Appearance: 降ふりそう・おいしそう
Hearsay: 降ふるそう・おいしいそう
The form before そう is the key to understanding the meaning.

08 〜そうだ vs. 〜みたいだ / 〜ようだ

These patterns can all express an impression, but they are not always interchangeable. Appearance 〜そうだ is especially common for a visible quality or something that seems about to happen.

〜そうだ — visible impression

この料理りょうりは辛からそうです。
This food looks spicy.

〜みたいだ — seems / looks like

外そとは雨あめみたいです。
It seems to be raining outside. (Inference from signs.)

〜そうだ — likely soon

赤あかちゃんが泣なきそうです。
The baby looks about to cry.

〜ようだ — somewhat formal inference

彼かれは忙いそがしいようです。
He seems to be busy.
For nouns

You cannot attach appearance 〜そう directly to a noun to mean “looks like a …”. Use 〜みたい or 〜のよう instead: 先生せんせいみたいです (looks like a teacher).

09 Common Mistakes

① Keeping い before appearance そう

✕ おいしいそうです。 (if you mean “looks delicious”)
✓ おいしそうです。
おいしいそうです is a hearsay form: “I heard it is delicious.”

② Adding な after a な-adjective

✕ 元気げんきなそうです。
✓ 元気げんきそうです。

③ Using dictionary form for appearance

✕ 雨あめが降ふるそうです。 (if you mean “looks like rain”)
✓ 雨あめが降ふりそうです。
降ふるそうです means “I heard it will rain.”

④ Using いいそう for “looks good”

✕ いいそうです。 (if you mean “looks good”)
✓ よさそうです。
いいそうです can mean “I heard it is good.”
