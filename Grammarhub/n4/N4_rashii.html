N4 GRAMMAR · HEARSAY & CHARACTERISTICS

〜らしい

Apparently / I Hear · Typical of / -like

Learn the two meanings of 〜らしい, how to connect verbs, adjectives, and nouns, and how to tell it apart from 〜そうだ, 〜ようだ, and 〜みたいだ.

01Two Meanings of 〜らしい

〜らしい has two important uses. The first means “apparently / I hear / it seems”. The second means “typical of / characteristic of”. The connection looks the same, so the context tells you which meaning is intended.

① Apparently / I hear

明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるらしい。
Apparently, it is going to rain tomorrow. (Information I heard or inferred.)

② Typical of / -like

今日きょうは春はるらしい天気てんきですね。
It feels like a typical spring day today. (A characteristic of spring.)
One form, two meanings

先生せんせいらしい can mean “apparently a teacher” or “teacher-like”. The surrounding sentence usually makes the meaning clear.

02How to Form 〜らしい

Use the plain form (short form) before 〜らしい, not ます / です. For verbs and い-adjectives, keep their plain forms (including past and negative forms). For nouns and な-adjectives, attach らしい directly to the word: do not add だ, な, or の.

Need a refresher? → N5 · Plain Form (short form). For the different meaning of 〜らしくない, see Section 06.

Verb (plain form) + らしい
い-adjective (plain form) + らしい
な-adjective (base form; no だ / な) + らしい
Noun (no です / だ) + らしい

Verb — present

来くるらしい
Apparently they are coming.

Verb — past

帰かえったらしい
Apparently they went home.

Verb — negative

来こないらしい
Apparently they are not coming.

い-adjective

寒さむいらしい
Apparently it is cold.

な-adjective

元気げんきらしい
Apparently they are doing well.

Noun

学生がくせいらしい
Apparently they are a student. (Context can also mean student-like.)
Negative plain form + らしい

To report negative information, make the word before らしい negative in the plain form: 来こないらしい = apparently not coming; 元気げんきじゃないらしい = apparently not doing well; 学生がくせいじゃないらしい = apparently not a student. This is different from 学生がくせいらしくない (“not behaving like a student”).

Polite form

Use 〜らしいです in polite speech. For example: 田中たなかさんは今日きょう休やすみらしいです。 = Apparently Tanaka is off today.

03〜らしい — Something You Heard

This use is common when sharing news, rumors, or information from other people. You do not claim to have directly confirmed the information.

友達ともだちに聞きいたんだけど、あの店みせは来月らいげつで閉しまるらしい。
A friend told me that the shop is apparently closing next month.
新あたらしい映画えいが、おもしろいらしいよ。
I hear the new movie is good.
天気予報てんきよほうによると、週末しゅうまつは雪ゆきが降ふるらしい。
According to the forecast, it will apparently snow this weekend.
山田やまださん、仕事しごとを辞やめたらしいよ。
Apparently Yamada quit their job.
A very useful conversational ending

〜らしいよ is a natural way to pass on something you heard. You can follow it with そうなの？ (“Really?”).

04〜らしい — A Conclusion from Clues

〜らしい is not limited to hearsay. You can also use it for a conclusion based on information or clues. In such cases, 〜ようだ or 〜みたいだ may also be natural.

電気でんきがついていないし、誰だれもいない。今日きょうは休やすみらしい。
The lights are off and no one is here. Looks like they are closed today.
電話でんわに出でないね。まだ寝ねているらしい。
They are not answering. It seems they are still asleep.
外そとに人ひとが集あつまっている。何なにかあったらしい。
People are gathering outside. Something seems to have happened.
店みせの前まえに長ながい列れつがある。人気にんきがあるらしい。
There is a long line outside. Apparently the shop is popular.
Do not treat this as an absolute rule

You can often express the same inference with 〜ようだ or 〜みたいだ. The difference is about nuance and register, not a strict certainty percentage.

05Noun + らしい — Typical of Something

This is a different use: 〜らしい describes qualities that are characteristic of a person, season, or thing. It can modify a noun or be used at the end of a sentence.

今日きょうは春はるらしい暖あたたかい日ひですね。
It is a warm, springlike day.
彼かれは失敗しっぱいしても笑わらっている。彼かれらしいね。
Even after making a mistake, he is smiling. That is so like him.
子こどもらしい元気げんきな声こえが聞きこえる。
I can hear lively voices typical of children.
今年ことしは秋あきらしい涼すずしい日ひが少すくない。
There have been few cool, autumn-like days this year.
〜らしい vs. 〜みたい

春はるらしい = having the typical qualities of spring. 春はるみたい = like spring (a comparison, possibly in a different season). They are not always interchangeable.

06らしくない / らしく — Typicality in Negative and Adverb Forms

〜らしくない is the negative form of the “typical of / characteristic of” meaning of 〜らしい. It is formed with noun (base form, without です / だ) + らしくない: 彼かれらしくない (“not like him”), 学生がくせいらしくない (“not behaving like a student”). Here, らしい changes like an い-adjective: らしい → らしくない / らしかった / らしく.

Review the N5 Plain Form and N5 Adjectives if you need help with these forms.

〜らしくない — unlike someone

今日きょうは静しずかだね。君きみらしくないよ。
You are quiet today. That is not like you.

〜らしく — in a characteristic way

自分じぶんらしく生いきたい。
I want to live in a way that feels true to myself.

〜らしかった — was characteristic

最後さいごまで笑顔えがおだった。彼女かのじょらしかったね。
She kept smiling until the end. That was just like her.

Negative hearsay — 〜ないらしい

田中たなかさんは今日きょう、来こないらしい。
Apparently Tanaka is not coming today.
Very important: the two negatives are different

Plain negative + らしい: 田中たなかさんは来こないらしい。 = “Apparently Tanaka is not coming.”

Noun + らしくない: 今日きょうの田中たなかさんは田中たなかさんらしくない。 = “Tanaka is not acting like their usual self today.”

〜ないらしい reports something negative; 〜らしくない describes something as uncharacteristic. Do not use らしくない to mean “apparently not.”

07Compare 〜らしい with 〜そうだ / 〜ようだ / 〜みたいだ

All four can relate to uncertainty, but they focus on different things. The best choice depends on where the information comes from and the speaking style.

〜そうだ (hearsay) — reported words

先生せんせいによると、明日あしたは休やすみだそうです。
According to the teacher, tomorrow is a day off. (Relaying a report.)

〜らしい — apparently

明日あしたは休やすみらしいよ。
Apparently tomorrow is a day off. (Source may be less explicit.)

〜ようだ — a careful inference

教室きょうしつに誰だれもいません。今日きょうは休やすみのようです。
No one is in the classroom. It seems today is a day off.

〜みたいだ — conversational inference

今日きょうは休やすみみたい。
Looks like today is a day off. (Casual.)
Another 〜そう is different

おいしそう = “looks delicious” (appearance, adjective stem). おいしいらしい = “apparently it is delicious” (heard/inferred). Compare 雨あめが降ふりそう (looks about to rain) with 雨あめが降ふるらしい (apparently it will rain).

08Everyday Conversations

Notice how 〜らしい often appears in quick, natural exchanges about things someone has heard.

A：新あたらしいカフェ、行いった？
B：まだ。でも、おいしいらしいよ。
A: Have you tried the new café? B: Not yet, but I hear it is good.
A：山田やまださん、今日きょうは？
B：風邪かぜで休やすんでいるらしい。
A: Where is Yamada today? B: Apparently they are out sick.
A：彼かれがそんなことを言いったの？
B：うん。彼かれらしくないよね。
A: He said something like that? B: Yeah. That is not like him, is it?
A：今日きょうは暖あたたかいね。
B：うん、春はるらしい天気てんきだね。
A: It is warm today. B: Yeah, it feels like spring.

09Common Mistakes

No だ after a noun (hearsay)

✕ 学生がくせいだらしい。
✓ 学生がくせいらしい。
For “apparently a student,” attach らしい directly to the noun.

No な after a な-adjective

✕ 元気げんきならしい。
✓ 元気げんきらしい。
Drop な / だ before らしい.

Negative reported information

✕ 来くるらしくない。
✓ 来こないらしい。
To say “apparently not coming,” put ない before らしい.

Typicality ≠ similarity in every case

子こどもらしい笑顔えがお。
子こどもみたいな笑顔えがお。
The first describes a characteristic of children; the second compares a smile to a child’s.

10Practice — Hearsay or Typicality?

Choose the most natural expression for the context. Each answer includes a short explanation.

1. 友達ともだちから聞きいたんだけど、あの店みせは来月らいげつ（閉しまるらしい・閉しまるらしくない）。
You heard that the shop is closing next month.
2. 今日きょうは春はる（らしい・らしくない）暖あたたかい日ひです。
Describe a warm day as typical of spring.
3. 田中たなかさんは今日きょう（来こないらしい・来くるらしくない）。
Apparently Tanaka is not coming.
4. あの人ひとは（先生せんせいらしい・先生せんせいだらしい）。
Apparently that person is a teacher.
5. 彼かれが約束やくそくを忘わすれるなんて、彼かれ（らしい・らしくない）ね。
It is uncharacteristic of him to forget a promise.
6. 天気予報てんきよほうによると、明日あしたは雨あめが（降ふるらしい・降ふりそう）。
Choose the form emphasizing information from the forecast.
7. 彼女かのじょは（元気げんきらしい・元気げんきならしい）。
Apparently she is doing well.
8. 自分じぶん（らしい・らしく）生いきたいです。
I want to live true to myself.
9. 新あたらしい映画えいが、おもしろい（らしいよ・らしくないよ）。
You have not seen it, but heard it is good.
10. 外そとの道みちがぬれている。夜よるに雨あめが（降ふったらしい・降ふったらしくない）。
Infer from the wet road that it rained.
Show answers & explanations

1. 閉しまるらしい — reported information.

2. らしい — characteristic of spring.

3. 来こないらしい — the negative belongs before らしい.

4. 先生せんせいらしい — noun + らしい, without だ.

5. らしくない — not like him.

6. 降ふるらしい — information from a report; 降ふりそう describes an apparent sign of imminent rain.

7. 元気げんきらしい — な-adjective stem + らしい.

8. らしく — the adverb form modifies 生いきる.

9. らしいよ — natural conversational hearsay.

10. 降ふったらしい — an inference from evidence. 降ふったようだ / 降ふったみたいだ are also natural.
