N4 GRAMMAR · HEARSAY

〜そうだ (Hearsay)

I heard that … / Apparently … / According to …

Pass on information you heard or read. Learn the plain-form connection rules, how to mention a source, and how to distinguish hearsay from appearance 〜そうだ.

N4Hearsay / reportingPlain form + そうです

01 What Does Hearsay 〜そうだ Mean?

Use 〜そうです to report information that you heard from another person, the news, a forecast, or another source. You are passing on information, rather than describing something you observed yourself.

Reported information (plain form) + そうです
明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるそうです。
I heard it will rain tomorrow.
田中たなかさんは来週らいしゅう結婚けっこんするそうです。
I heard Tanaka is getting married next week.
Where did the information come from?

Hearsay 〜そうです suggests that the information came from someone or something else. It does not, by itself, say whether the information is definitely true.

02 How to Connect — Plain Form + そうです

Unlike appearance 〜そうです, hearsay attaches to a complete plain-form statement. With な-adjectives and nouns, keep だ before そうです in affirmative nonpast sentences.

Verb — dictionary form

行いく → 行いくそうです
I heard (someone) will go.

い-adjective — keep い

おいしい → おいしいそうです
I heard it is delicious.

な-adjective — add だ

元気げんき → 元気げんきだそうです
I heard (someone) is well.

Noun — add だ

先生せんせい → 先生せんせいだそうです
I heard (someone) is a teacher.
Do not use polite です・ます before hearsay そうです

✕ 行いきますそうです。
✓ 行いくそうです。
The そうです at the end makes the whole sentence polite.

03 Verbs — Present, Negative, and Past

Change the reported verb into the form that matches what happened (or will happen), then add そうです. The reported event can be past even though そうです remains present.

来くるそうです。
I heard (someone) is coming / will come.
来こないそうです。
I heard (someone) isn't coming.
来きたそうです。
I heard (someone) came.
来こなかったそうです。
I heard (someone) didn't come.
Past information ≠ そうでした

For “I heard it rained yesterday,” say 昨日きのうは雨あめが降ふったそうです. The past ending belongs to the reported statement, not to そうです.

04 い-Adjectives — Keep the Complete Form

For hearsay, do not remove い. Negative and past adjective forms also attach directly to そうです.

この店みせは安やすいそうです。
I heard this shop is inexpensive.
この店みせは安やすくないそうです。
I heard this shop isn't inexpensive.
昨日きのうは寒さむかったそうです。
I heard it was cold yesterday.
昨日きのうは寒さむくなかったそうです。
I heard it wasn't cold yesterday.
Special case: いい

いいそうです = I heard it is good.
よさそうです = It looks good.
This is one of the clearest ways to tell hearsay and appearance apart.

05 な-Adjectives and Nouns — Remember だ

For affirmative nonpast hearsay, use だそうです, not なそうです or ですそうです. For negatives and past forms, use the appropriate plain-form ending.

彼かれは元気げんきだそうです。
I heard he is doing well.
彼かれは元気げんきじゃないそうです。
I heard he isn't doing well.
彼女かのじょは学生がくせいだそうです。
I heard she is a student.
彼女かのじょは去年きょねんまで学生がくせいだったそうです。
I heard she was a student until last year.
One key distinction

元気げんきだそうです = I heard (someone) is well.
元気げんきそうです = (Someone) looks well.
The presence or absence of だ changes the meaning.

06 Say Where You Heard It — 〜によると

You can name the source with 〜によると (“according to …”) or 〜の話はなしでは (“according to what … said”).

Source + によると、reported information + そうです
天気予報てんきよほうによると、明日あしたは雨あめが降ふるそうです。
According to the weather forecast, it will rain tomorrow.
ニュースによると、電車でんしゃが止とまったそうです。
According to the news, the trains stopped.
友達ともだちの話はなしでは、あの映画えいがはおもしろいそうです。
According to my friend, that movie is interesting.
先生せんせいによると、来週らいしゅうテストがあるそうです。
According to the teacher, there's a test next week.
When the source is already clear

You do not need to repeat 〜によると every time. A simple sentence ending in そうです is enough when the listener knows you are passing on information.

07 Hearsay vs. Appearance — Don't Mix Them Up

Both use そうです, but their meaning and connection are different. Compare these pairs.

Hearsay — information you heard

雨あめが降ふるそうです。
I heard it will rain. (dictionary form)

Appearance — signs you can see

雨あめが降ふりそうです。
It looks like it will rain. (ます-stem)

Hearsay — complete い-adjective

このケーキはおいしいそうです。
I heard this cake is delicious.

Appearance — adjective stem

このケーキはおいしそうです。
This cake looks delicious.
Quick check

降ふるそう / おいしいそう → reported information.
降ふりそう / おいしそう → an appearance or prediction based on signs.

08 Hearsay 〜そうだ vs. 〜らしい / 〜と言いっていました

These can all relate to information from other people, but they are not identical.

〜そうだ — reporting information

彼かれは引ひっ越こすそうです。
I heard he's moving.

〜らしい — apparently / it seems

彼かれは引ひっ越こすらしいです。
Apparently he's moving. (May reflect inference from information.)

〜と言いっていました — what someone said

彼かれは「引ひっ越こします」と言いっていました。
He said, “I'm moving.” (Explicitly reports his words.)

〜ようだ / 〜みたいだ — inference

彼かれは忙いそがしいみたいです。
He seems busy. (An inference; not necessarily a report.)
Choosing the right expression

Use 〜そうです when the key point is what you heard. Use 〜と言いっていました when you want to make the speaker or their words explicit. 〜らしい often has a more inferential feel.

09 Common Mistakes

① Using the ます-form before hearsay そう

✕ 行いきますそうです。
✓ 行いくそうです。
Use the plain form, not ます.

② Leaving out だ after a noun or な-adjective

✕ 彼女かのじょは先生せんせいそうです。 (if you mean “I heard she's a teacher”)
✓ 彼女かのじょは先生せんせいだそうです。

③ Removing い from an adjective

✕ おいしそうです。 (if you mean “I heard it is delicious”)
✓ おいしいそうです。
おいしそうです is correct Japanese, but it means “looks delicious.”

④ Putting the past tense on そうです

✕ 昨日きのうは雨あめが降ふるそうでした。 (if you simply mean “I heard it rained yesterday”)
✓ 昨日きのうは雨あめが降ふったそうです。
Put the past tense in the reported statement.
