こ・そ・あ（話題わだいの指示しじ）

Referring to Things That Were Talked About

Learn how この・その・あの and これ・それ・あれ point to people, places, and ideas that were mentioned in conversation or writing — not only to things you can see.

N4Conversationその / あの / この
Standalone

01 こ・そ・あ Also Point to Ideas

You already use これ・それ・あれ to point to things you can see. In conversation and writing, こ・そ・あ also point to things that were talked about — a person, a shop, an idea, or a story.

Pointing to something you can see

そのかばん、いいですね。
That bag (near you) is nice.

Pointing to something that was mentioned

A：駅えきの前まえに新あたらしいカフェができました。
B：そのカフェはどうですか。
A: A new café opened in front of the station.
B: How is that café?
The question to ask

When you refer to something that was talked about, ask: Do both of us know it? Or does only one of us know it? The answer tells you whether to use そ or あ.

02 そ — Something Only One Person Knows

Use そ（その・それ・そこ・そういう）for something that the other person just told you about and you do not know, or something you mentioned that the listener does not know.

A：昨日きのう、田中たなかさんという人ひとに会あいました。
B：その人ひとはどんな人ひとですか。
A: Yesterday I met a person called Tanaka.
B: What kind of person is that?
A：新あたらしい店みせに行いきました。
B：その店みせはどこにありますか。
A: I went to a new shop.
B: Where is that shop?
友達ともだちにケーキをもらいました。それはとてもおいしかったです。
A friend gave me a cake. It was very good.
Asking about something new

If you have never seen the person or place, you cannot say あの. ✕ あの人ひとはどんな人ひとですか（you do not know Tanaka）→ ✓ その人ひとはどんな人ひとですか.

03 あ — Something We Both Know

Use あ（あの・あれ・あそこ・ああいう）for something that both the speaker and the listener know, such as a shared memory or a place you both visited.

A：昨日きのうの映画えいが、よかったね。
B：うん、あの映画えいがは本当ほんとうにおもしろかった。
A: Yesterday’s movie was good, wasn’t it?
B: Yeah, that movie was really interesting.
去年きょねん一緒いっしょに行いったあのレストラン、また行いきたいね。
I want to go to that restaurant we went to together last year again.
A：先週せんしゅうの話はなし、どうなった？
B：ああ、あれはもう大丈夫だいじょうぶ。
A: What happened with the thing we talked about last week?
B: Oh, that is all fine now.
A shared memory

あ sounds like “you know the one.” It is common in conversation between friends, family, and colleagues who share the same experience.

04 こ — Something Close to the Speaker’s Story

Use こ（この・これ・こういう・こんな）for information that you are presenting yourself — something you are about to say, or something you just said and want to keep as the main topic.

これは秘密ひみつですが、来月らいげつ結婚けっこんするんです。
This is a secret, but I am getting married next month.
昨日きのう、こんなことがありました。電車でんしゃの中なかで財布さいふを拾ひろったんです。
Something like this happened yesterday: I found a wallet on the train.
日本にほんでは靴くつを脱ぬいで家いえに入はいります。この習慣しゅうかんは大切たいせつです。
In Japan, you take off your shoes before entering a house. This custom is important.
こ or そ after your own sentence?

When you just introduced something, both この and その are possible. この makes it feel closer and more important to your story; その simply refers back to it.

05 Same Topic, Different Word

Look at how the choice changes depending on who knows the topic.

Only you know the shop

駅えきの近ちかくにいい店みせがあるんです。その店みせのパンはおいしいですよ。
There is a good shop near the station. The bread at that shop is delicious.

You both know the shop

駅えきの近ちかくのあの店みせ、閉店へいてんしたそうですよ。
You know that shop near the station? I heard it closed.

You present it as your topic

この店みせは私わたしが一番いちばん好すきな店みせです。
This shop is my favorite. (Talking about it, or showing a photo.)

06 こ・そ in Writing

In emails, essays, and stories, the reader cannot share your memory, so あ is not used to refer back to something in the text. Use そ (or こ) to point to something you already wrote.

先週せんしゅう、京都きょうとへ行いきました。そこで古ふるいお寺てらを見みました。
Last week I went to Kyoto. There I saw an old temple.
山田やまださんから手紙てがみが来きました。その手紙てがみには写真しゃしんが入はいっていました。
A letter came from Mr. Yamada. There was a photo in that letter.
Remember

In writing: そ = the thing I just mentioned. あ is used in writing only for something the writer and reader both know well, such as a shared experience in a personal letter.

07 Common Mistakes

あ for something the listener does not know

昨日きのう、旅行会社りょこうがいしゃの人ひとに会あいました。
✕ あの人ひとはとても親切しんせつでした。
✓ その人ひとはとても親切しんせつでした。
The listener has never met the person, so use そ.

そ for a shared memory

✕ （二人ふたりで行いった店みせ）その店みせ、また行いこうよ。
✓ あの店みせ、また行いこうよ。
When you both know it, あ sounds natural.

あ in writing

✕ 京都きょうとへ行いきました。あそこで…
✓ 京都きょうとへ行いきました。そこで…
Refer back to your own text with そ.

08 Practice — こ・そ・あ in Conversation

Choose the most natural word. Think about who knows the topic.

1. A：昨日きのう、山田やまださんという人ひとに会あいました。
B：（その・あの）人ひとは何なにをしている人ひとですか。
B has never met Mr. Yamada.
2. A：先月せんげつ一緒いっしょに行いった温泉おんせん、よかったね。
B：うん、（その・あの）温泉おんせんにまた行いきたい。
A and B went there together.
3. 先週せんしゅう、北海道ほっかいどうへ行いきました。（そこ・あそこ）で雪ゆきを見みました。
You are writing a diary.
4. （これ・あれ）は秘密ひみつですが、私わたしは来月らいげつ引ひっ越こします。
You are about to say something.
5. A：駅えきの前まえに新あたらしい本屋ほんやができたよ。
B：へえ、（そこ・あそこ）は大おおきい？
B does not know the new bookstore.
6. A：去年きょねんの夏なつ、二人ふたりで見みた花火はなびを覚おぼえてる？
B：もちろん。（あれ・それ）はきれいだったね。
A shared memory.
Show answers

1. その — only A knows the person.

2. あの — both of them know the place.

3. そこ — in writing, refer back with そ.

4. これ — こ introduces what you are about to say.

5. そこ — B is asking about something only A knows.

6. あれ — a memory they share.
