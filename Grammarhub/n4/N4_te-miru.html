N4 GRAMMAR · TRYING & EXPERIENCING

〜てみる

Try Doing · Want to Try · Give It a Try

Learn how to use 〜てみる for new experiences, testing ideas, requests, invitations, and gentle advice. Compare “try doing” with “attempt to do” and “have done before.”

N4Experience & TrialVerb て-form + みる
Te-form

01 What Does 〜てみる Mean?

〜てみる means to try doing something to see what it is like, how it goes, or what happens. You actually give the action a try, often because it is new or you are curious about the result.

Verb て-form + みる = try doing something
Verb て-form + みます = polite form
このケーキを食たべてみます。
I will try this cake (to see what it tastes like).
日本語にほんごで話はなしてみます。
I will try speaking in Japanese.
この服ふくを着きてみます。
I will try on these clothes.
新あたらしいアプリを使つかってみます。
I will try using the new app.
Key idea

You do the action as a trial or experience. 食たべます simply means “I will eat.” 食たべてみます adds the idea “I will try eating it and see what it is like.”

02 How to Form 〜てみる

First, change the verb to its て-form. Then add みる (casual) or みます (polite). Do not attach みる directly to the ます-form or dictionary form.

Verb て-form + みる / みます
食たべます → 食たべて → 食たべてみます
飲のみます → 飲のんで → 飲のんでみます
行いきます → 行いって → 行いってみます
します → して → してみます
来きます → 来きて → 来きてみます
N5 · Verb て-formN5 · Plain Form
友達ともだちに聞きいてみます。
I will try asking my friend.
自分じぶんで作つくってみます。
I will try making it myself.
How to write みる

In this grammar pattern, みる is normally written in hiragana. The verb 見みる (to see/watch) can be written with the kanji 見み, but 〜てみる is a helper expression meaning “give it a try.”

03 Try Something New — Food, Places & Activities

Use 〜てみる when you want to experience something and find out what it is like. It is common with food, travel, clothes, hobbies, and new activities.

日本にほんで温泉おんせんに入はいってみたいです。
I would like to try going to a hot spring in Japan.
京都きょうとに行いってみたいです。
I would like to visit Kyoto and see what it is like.
この靴くつを履はいてみてもいいですか。
May I try these shoes on?
初はじめて納豆なっとうを食たべてみました。
I tried natto for the first time.
Going somewhere to see what it is like

京都きょうとに行いきたいです = I want to go to Kyoto. 京都きょうとに行いってみたいです = I want to try going there / experience visiting Kyoto. Both are natural, but the second emphasizes the experience.

04 Try an Action to Find Out What Happens

〜てみる is also useful when you test an idea, check information, or ask someone to see what answer you get. It does not have to be a completely new experience.

先生せんせいに聞きいてみます。
I will try asking the teacher.
インターネットで調しらべてみます。
I will try looking it up online.
もう一度いちど電話でんわしてみます。
I will try calling again.
このボタンを押おしてみてください。
Please try pressing this button.
Trying is not the same as succeeding

電話でんわしてみました means I tried calling. It does not automatically tell us whether someone answered. 電話でんわしてみましたが、誰だれも出でませんでした = I tried calling, but nobody answered.

05 Present, Past & Negative Forms

After the verb て-form, みる changes like a regular Group 2 verb. You can talk about a future trial, a completed trial, or a decision not to try something.

Present / Future

食たべてみます。
I will try eating it.

Past

食たべてみました。
I tried eating it.

Negative

食たべてみません。
I will not try eating it.

Past Negative

食たべてみませんでした。
I did not try eating it.
Casual forms

食たべてみる (will try), 食たべてみた (tried), 食たべてみない (will not try), 食たべてみなかった (did not try). The first verb stays in the て-form.

06 〜てみたい — Want to Try

Add 〜たい to みます to say you want to try an experience. This is especially common when discussing travel, food, or something you have never done.

Verb て-form + みたいです = want to try doing
Verb て-form + みたかったです = wanted to try doing
一度いちど富士山ふじさんに登のぼってみたいです。
I would like to try climbing Mt. Fuji once.
着物きものを着きてみたいです。
I would like to try wearing a kimono.
子こどものころ、日本にほんに住すんでみたかったです。
When I was a child, I wanted to try living in Japan.
辛からい料理りょうりは食たべてみたくないです。
I do not want to try spicy food.
〜たい vs. 〜てみたい

着物きものを着きたいです = I want to wear a kimono. 着物きものを着きてみたいです = I want to try wearing a kimono. Here, みたい = みる + たい; it is not the separate 〜みたい that means “looks like.”

07 〜てみてください — Ask Someone to Give It a Try

Use 〜てみてください to encourage someone to try an action. This often sounds like a friendly suggestion, rather than simply telling someone what to do.

Verb て-form + みてください = please try doing
この問題もんだいを解といてみてください。
Please try solving this problem.
日本語にほんごで言いってみてください。
Please try saying it in Japanese.
この料理りょうりを食たべてみてください。
Please give this dish a try.
もう一度いちど考かんがえてみてください。
Please try thinking about it once more.
Not the same as 〜てください

食たべてください = Please eat it. 食たべてみてください = Please try eating it. The second highlights the experience or trial.

08 〜てみませんか・〜てみましょう — Invitations

Invite someone to try something together with 〜てみませんか, or suggest doing it together with 〜てみましょう.

一緒いっしょに作つくってみませんか。
Would you like to try making it together?
今度こんど、このレストランに行いってみませんか。
Would you like to try this restaurant sometime?
日本語にほんごで話はなしてみましょう。
Let’s try speaking in Japanese.
次つぎは自分じぶんでやってみましょう。
Next, let’s try doing it ourselves.
Casual invitation

食たべてみない？ can be a casual way to say “Want to try it?” Here, the negative question is an invitation, not necessarily a refusal.

09 〜てみたら・〜てみれば — Giving Advice

When someone is unsure what to do, suggest trying something. 〜てみたらどうですか is a useful and fairly gentle way to give advice.

Verb て-form + みたらどうですか = Why don’t you try…?
Verb て-form + みればどうですか = How about trying…?
先生せんせいに相談そうだんしてみたらどうですか。
Why don’t you try asking the teacher for advice?
少すこし休やすんでみたらどうですか。
Why don’t you try taking a short break?
別べつの方法ほうほうを試ためしてみたらどうですか。
Why don’t you try another method?
自分じぶんで調しらべてみればどうですか。
How about trying to look it up yourself?
A small nuance

〜てみたらどうですか is often a gentle suggestion, but the tone and context still matter. 〜てみればどうですか can sometimes sound a little more direct. In friendly conversation, 〜てみたら？ is very common.

10 〜てみる vs. 〜ようとする vs. 〜たことがある

These patterns can all involve actions and experiences, but they answer different questions: “Did you give it a try?”, “Did you attempt it?”, or “Have you ever done it?”

〜てみる — give it a try

日本語にほんごで話はなしてみました。
I tried speaking in Japanese to see how it would go.

〜ようとする — attempt to do

日本語にほんごで話はなそうとしました。
I attempted to speak in Japanese. It does not say whether I managed to speak.

〜たことがある — past experience

日本語にほんごで話はなしたことがあります。
I have spoken in Japanese before. It states past experience.

〜てみたい — desire to try

日本語にほんごで話はなしてみたいです。
I want to try speaking in Japanese.
Trying can lead to success or failure

作つくってみたら、おいしくできました = I tried making it, and it turned out delicious. 作つくってみましたが、うまくできませんでした = I tried making it, but it did not turn out well.

11 Natural Everyday Conversations

〜てみる is very common when you are shopping, studying, making plans, or solving a problem.

A：この服ふく、どうですか。
B：かわいいですね。着きてみてもいいですか。
A: How about these clothes? B: They’re cute. May I try them on?
A：この漢字かんじが分わかりません。
B：辞書じしょで調しらべてみてください。
A: I don’t know this kanji. B: Please try looking it up in a dictionary.
A：週末しゅうまつ、何なにをしますか。
B：新あたらしいカフェに行いってみようと思おもいます。
A: What will you do this weekend? B: I think I’ll try going to a new café.
A：この料理りょうり、食たべたことがありますか。
B：いいえ。でも、食たべてみたいです。
A: Have you ever eaten this dish? B: No, but I’d like to try it.

12 Common Mistakes

Use the て-form first

✕ 食たべますみます。
✓ 食たべてみます。
Use the て-form before みます.

Do not omit み

✕ 着きてたいです。
✓ 着きてみたいです。
Use てみたい to mean “want to try doing.”

みる is not literal seeing

✕ この料理りょうりを食たべて見みます。
✓ この料理りょうりを食たべてみます。
Write the helper expression みる in hiragana in ordinary usage.

Use an action you can try

✕ 雨あめが降ふってみます。
✓ 外そとに出でてみます。
〜てみる normally describes an intentional action someone can choose to try. Rain falling is not such an action.
Do not confuse two negatives

食たべてみません = I will not try eating it. 食たべないでみます = I will try not eating it (to see what happens). The position of the negative changes the meaning.

Show answers

1. 食たべて — The verb must be in the て-form before みる.

2. みたい — 〜てみたい expresses a desire to try something.

3. みました — 〜てみました is the polite past form.

4. みてもいいですか — 〜てみてもいいですか asks permission to try something.

5. みてください — 〜てみてください asks someone to give it a try.

6. みませんか — 〜てみませんか is a polite invitation.

7. みます — 〜てみます is the correct polite form.

8. みました — The sentence says “yesterday,” so use past tense.

9. みたい — 〜てみたいです means “want to try.”

10. みます — The speaker intends to try it now or in the future.

11. みたらどうですか — This gives advice: “Why don’t you try…?”

12. I have spoken Japanese before. — 〜たことがある describes a past experience, not a desire.

13. Yes, they tried calling; no one answered. — 〜てみました tells us the action was tried; the second clause gives the result.

14. I will not try eating it. — The negative applies to みる: the speaker will not try.

15. I will try not eating it. — 〜ないでみる means trying the experiment of not doing the action.

16. みましょう — 〜てみましょう means “Let’s try doing…”.
