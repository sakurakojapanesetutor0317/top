Do it! / Don’t do it! · Direct Commands and Prohibitions

Learn how to form direct commands and prohibitions with Group 1, 2, and 3 verbs, and when to use them instead of polite requests or 〜なさい.

01 Imperative & Prohibitive Forms — Do It! / Don’t Do It!

The imperative form gives a direct command: “Do it!” The prohibitive form tells someone not to do something: “Don’t do it!” These forms can sound strong, so the situation and relationship matter.

Imperative: 行いく → 行いけ！ = Go!
Prohibitive: 行いく → 行いくな！ = Don’t go!
急いそげ！
Hurry up! (Direct command.)
止とまれ！
Stop! (Direct command.)
ここに入はいるな！
Do not enter here!
心配しんぱいするな。
Don’t worry. (Direct, informal reassurance.)
Important: strong does not always mean angry

You may hear these forms in emergencies, sports, cheering, signs, movies, or forceful conversations. For everyday polite requests, 〜てください / 〜ないでください is usually more suitable.

02 Formation Overview — Three Verb Groups

The imperative form changes depending on the verb group. The prohibitive form is simpler: use the dictionary form + な for all three groups.

Group 1 — final sound → e-row

書かく → 書かけ！
書かく → 書かくな！
Write! / Don’t write!

Group 2 — remove る, add ろ

食たべる → 食たべろ！
食たべる → 食たべるな！
Eat! / Don’t eat!

Group 3 — irregular する

する → しろ！
する → するな！
Do it! / Don’t do it!

Group 3 — irregular 来くる

来くる → 来こい！
来くる → 来くるな！
Come! / Don’t come!
You need the dictionary form first

These patterns do not start with the ます-form. For example, 書かきます → dictionary form 書かく → imperative 書かけ or prohibitive 書かくな.

03 Group 1 — Change the Final u-Sound to an e-Sound

For Group 1 verbs, change the last kana from the u-row to the matching e-row. The rest of the verb stays the same.

う → え ／ く → け ／ ぐ → げ ／ す → せ ／ つ → て
ぬ → ね ／ ぶ → べ ／ む → め ／ る → れ

う → え

買かう → 買かえ！
Buy it!

く → け

書かく → 書かけ！
Write!

ぐ → げ

急いそぐ → 急いそげ！
Hurry!

す → せ

話はなす → 話はなせ！
Speak!

つ → て

待まつ → 待まて！
Wait!

ぶ → べ

遊あそぶ → 遊あそべ！
Play!

む → め

読よむ → 読よめ！
Read!

る → れ

走はしる → 走はしれ！
Run!
Not every る-verb is Group 2

走はしる and 帰かえる are Group 1, so the imperatives are 走はしれ and 帰かえれ — not 走はしろ or 帰かえろ. Check the verb group, not just the last kana.

04 Group 2 — Remove る and Add ろ

For most Group 2 verbs, remove the dictionary-form ending る and add ろ.

食たべる → 食たべ + ろ → 食たべろ
見みる → 見み + ろ → 見みろ
早はやく起おきろ！
Get up early!
こっちを見みろ！
Look this way!
野菜やさいも食たべろ！
Eat your vegetables, too!
もう寝ねろ！
Go to sleep already!
A small exception and a written alternative

The verb くれる has the imperative くれ, not くれろ (e.g., 水みずをくれ). In formal writing or older/literary styles, some Group 2 imperatives end in 〜よ, such as 見みよ; 〜ろ is the form to learn first for everyday spoken commands.

05 Group 3 — する and 来くる Are Irregular

Memorize the two main irregular imperatives: する → しろ and 来くる → 来こい. For a する-verb, keep the NOUN and change する to しろ.

する → しろ
勉強べんきょうする → 勉強べんきょうしろ
来くる → 来こい (read: こい)
早はやくしろ！
Hurry up! / Do it quickly!
静しずかにしろ！
Be quiet! (Forceful.)
明日あしたも練習れんしゅうしろ！
Practice again tomorrow!
こっちに来こい！
Come here! (Forceful.)
Pay attention to the reading of 来くる

来くる (くる) → 来こい (こい), but 来くるな (くるな). The imperative and the prohibitive use different readings. A written/literary alternative for する is せよ; しろ is the common direct spoken form.

06 Prohibitive Form — Dictionary Form + な

To give a direct negative command, use dictionary form + な. This rule works for Group 1, Group 2, and Group 3 verbs. You do not need to make a ない-form.

Dictionary form + な = Don’t do…!
ここで走はしるな！
Don’t run here!
このボタンを押おすな！
Don’t press this button!
まだ食たべるな！
Don’t eat yet!
嘘うそをつくな！
Don’t lie!
無理むりをするな。
Don’t overdo it.
こっちに来くるな！
Don’t come here!
Prohibition is not the same as a negative statement

行いかない = “(I) do not go” (negative statement). 行いくな = “Don’t go!” (direct prohibition). The ending な is not a shortened ない here.

07 Compare Positive Commands and Prohibitions

For the same verb, the imperative changes its ending, while the prohibitive keeps the dictionary form and adds な. Learn the two forms as a pair.

行いく — go

行いけ！
行いくな！
Go! / Don’t go!

入はいる — enter

入はいれ！
入はいるな！
Enter! / Don’t enter!

食たべる — eat

食たべろ！
食たべるな！
Eat! / Don’t eat!

見みる — look

見みろ！
見みるな！
Look! / Don’t look!

する — do

しろ！
するな！
Do it! / Don’t do it!

来くる — come

来こい！
来くるな！
Come! / Don’t come!
Watch the pair carefully

入はいれ and 入はいるな are both based on Group 1 入はいる. Do not use 入はいろ. In contrast, Group 2 見みる becomes 見みろ.

08 Imperative Form vs. 〜なさい

Both expressions tell someone to do something, but 〜なさい is especially common for instructions from a parent or teacher. The imperative form is more direct and can sound harsher, though it is also used in cheering or emergencies.

〜なさい — parent/teacher-style instruction

早はやく行いきなさい。
Go now. (Authoritative instruction.)

Imperative — direct command

早はやく行いけ！
Go now! (Forceful or urgent.)

〜なさい — parent/teacher-style instruction

野菜やさいを食たべなさい。
Eat your vegetables.

Imperative — direct command

野菜やさいを食たべろ！
Eat your vegetables! (Blunt.)
The formation is completely different

〜なさい uses the ます-stem: 行いきます → 行いきなさい. The imperative uses the dictionary form: 行いく → 行いけ. Neither is a normal polite request to a stranger.

09 Direct Commands vs. Polite Requests

In everyday conversations with strangers, colleagues, teachers, or customers, choose 〜てください (“please do…”) or 〜ないでください (“please don’t…”). Imperative and prohibitive forms can sound rude if the situation does not justify them.

Direct command

ここに名前なまえを書かけ！
Write your name here! (Very direct.)

Polite request

ここに名前なまえを書かいてください。
Please write your name here.

Direct prohibition

ここで写真しゃしんを撮とるな！
Don’t take photos here! (Strong.)

Polite negative request

ここで写真しゃしんを撮とらないでください。
Please do not take photos here.
N5 · Verb て-FormN5 · Verb ない-Form
A rule can also use 〜てはいけません

ここで写真しゃしんを撮とってはいけません = “Taking photos here is not allowed.” This expresses a rule, rather than shouting a command at one person.

10 When Are Imperatives and Prohibitions Used?

Context changes how a direct form sounds. You may hear it as an urgent warning, an order, encouragement, or a line in a movie. Do not assume every imperative is hostile.

危あぶない！逃にげろ！
Danger! Run away! (Emergency.)
止とまれ！
Stop! (Traffic or safety instruction.)
頑張がんばれ！
Do your best! / You can do it! (Cheering.)
負まけるな！
Don’t give up / Don’t lose! (Encouragement.)
今いますぐ出でて行いけ！
Get out right now! (Angry/forceful.)
人ひとの物ものを勝手かってに触さわるな！
Don’t touch other people’s things without permission!
Different feelings, same grammar

頑張がんばれ can be supportive, while 出でて行いけ can sound hostile. The form alone does not tell you the speaker’s emotion; the words, tone, and situation do.

11 Signs, Sports, and Short Instructions

Short imperative and prohibitive forms appear in road signs, warnings, cheering, and sports. Some signs use other expressions such as NOUN + 禁止きんし (“prohibited”).

Traffic instruction

止とまれ
STOP (command form of 止とまる).

Direct warning

入はいるな！
Do not enter!

Short warning sign

立入禁止たちいりきんし
No entry / Keep out. (A NOUN expression, not a verb command.)

Cheering

頑張がんばれ！
You can do it! / Keep going!
A useful detail

止とまれ comes from Group 1 止とまる. It is different from Group 2 止とめる → 止とめろ (“Stop something!”).

12 Reading Signs — 〜と書かいてあります・〜と読よみます・〜という意味いみです

When you see a sign or a word you do not know, three questions are useful (Minna no Nihongo Lesson 33): What does it say? How is it read? What does it mean?

What does it say? — 〜と書かいてあります

「何なんと書かいてありますか。」「『止とまれ』と書かいてあります。」
“What does it say?” — “It says ‘Tomare.’”

How is it read? — 〜と読よみます

「これは何なんと読よみますか。」「『とまれ』と読よみます。」
“How do you read this?” — “It is read ‘tomare.’”

What does it mean? — 〜という意味いみです

「止とまれ」は、ここで止とまらなければならないという意味いみです。
“Tomare” means that you must stop here.
Word / sign + は + plain form + という意味いみです
「禁煙きんえん」は、たばこを吸すってはいけないという意味いみです。
“Kin’en” means that you must not smoke.
「無料むりょう」は、お金かねがいらないという意味いみです。
“Muryō” means that you do not need to pay.
「入はいるな」は、入はいってはいけないという意味いみです。
“Hairu na” means that you must not enter.
Use the plain form before という

Put the explanation in the plain form: ✕ 入はいってはいけませんという意味いみです → ✓ 入はいってはいけないという意味いみです. To ask, say どういう意味いみですか (“What does it mean?”).

〜と書かいてあります uses 〜てある: the words are already written there. Compare it with “someone is writing” (〜ています).

13 Mini Conversations — Choose the Right Tone

Compare natural situations. A direct command may fit an emergency or a close, forceful exchange, while a polite request fits ordinary interactions.

A：車くるまが来くる！止とまれ！
B：あっ、ありがとう！
A: A car is coming! Stop! B: Oh, thanks!
A：試合しあい、頑張がんばれ！
B：ありがとう！
A: Good luck in the match! B: Thanks!
A：ここで電話でんわをしないでください。
B：すみません。
A: Please do not make phone calls here. B: Sorry. (Polite request.)
母はは：早はやく寝ねなさい。
子こ：はーい。
Mother: Go to bed early. Child: Okay. (Authoritative instruction.)
Do not copy commands into every conversation

For a friend, a casual “〜て” request may also be natural, but a beginner can safely use 〜てください when speaking politely. The direct imperative is not a general replacement for 〜てください.

14 Common Mistakes — Verb Groups and Endings

Group 1 — use the e-row

✕ 書かきろ！
✓ 書かけ！
書かく is Group 1: く → け.

Group 2 — remove る + ろ

✕ 食たべれ！
✓ 食たべろ！
食たべる is Group 2: remove る and add ろ.

来くる — irregular imperative

✕ 来きろ！
✓ 来こい！
来くる → 来こい (こい), not きろ.

Prohibition — use dictionary form

✕ 行いかないな！
✓ 行いくな！
Dictionary form + な expresses “Don’t go!”

Do not confuse 〜なさい and 〜な

行いきなさい。
行いくな！
The first means “Go!”; the second means “Don’t go!”

Don’t confuse a negative statement

行いかない。
行いくな！
“I don’t go” vs. “Don’t go!”
Form and tone are both important

Even if your conjugation is correct, using a direct command with a customer or a stranger may be inappropriate. Grammar accuracy and social context go together.
