N4 GRAMMAR · EMBEDDED QUESTIONS

〜か・〜かどうか

Who / Where / When…? · Whether or Not

Learn to put questions inside sentences: ask who, where, when, or what with 〜か, and express whether something is true with 〜かどうか. Includes plain-form rules, examples, and practice.

01 Two Types of Questions Inside a Sentence

Japanese can put a question inside a longer sentence. Use question word + か when you need information such as who, where, or when. Use 〜かどうか when the answer is yes or no.

Question word + か

誰だれが来くるか分わかりません。
I do not know who is coming.

〜かどうか

田中たなかさんが来くるかどうか分わかりません。
I do not know whether Tanaka is coming.
Ask yourself: What kind of answer do I need?

Who / where / when / what / how? → question word + か.
Yes or no? → かどうか (or sometimes just か).

02 How to Form Them — Use the Plain Form

Before か or かどうか, use the plain form (short form). This works with dictionary form, negative form, and past form. For a present positive NOUN or な-adjective, do not add だ before か.

Question word + sentence (plain form) + か + 分わかりません / 知しっていますか / 教おしえてください
Sentence (plain form) + かどうか + 分わかりません / 確認かくにんします / 決きめます

Verb — dictionary form

明日あした、雨あめが降ふるかどうか分わかりません。
I do not know whether it will rain tomorrow.

Verb — past form

昨日きのう、彼かれが来きたかどうか知しっていますか。
Do you know whether he came yesterday?

NOUN — no だ

あの人ひとが先生せんせいかどうか知しりません。
I do not know whether that person is a teacher.

な-adjective — no だ

この店みせが有名ゆうめいかどうか調しらべます。
I will check whether this shop is famous.

Where does the polite ending go?

Use the plain form inside the question, then put ます / ません on the main verb: 来くるかどうか分わかりません. Not 来きますかどうか分わかりません.

03 Question Word + か — Who, Where, When, What, How

Keep the question word inside the sentence and put か after the question clause. Then add a verb such as 分わかる, 知しる, 聞きく, or 教おしえる.

誰だれが / どこに / いつ / 何なにを / どうやって + plain-form clause + か + main verb
誰だれが来くるか知しっていますか。
Do you know who is coming?
駅えきがどこにあるか分わかりません。
I do not know where the station is.
会議かいぎがいつ始はじまるか教おしえてください。
Please tell me when the meeting starts.
何なにを買かうかまだ決きめていません。
I have not decided what to buy yet.
どうやって使つかうか分わかりません。
I do not know how to use it.
何時なんじに帰かえるか聞きいてみます。
I will ask what time they are going home.
Keep the question word

If you need a specific piece of information, keep the question word: 誰だれが来くるか (who will come), いつ来くるか (when they will come). Do not replace these with かどうか.

04 〜かどうか — Whether / Whether or Not

Use 〜かどうか when you want to know whether something is true or will happen. You are not asking for a specific person, time, or place; you want to know yes or no.

Plain-form statement + かどうか + 分わかる / 知しる / 確認かくにんする / 決きめる
明日あした、雨あめが降ふるかどうか分わかりません。
I do not know whether it will rain tomorrow.
電車でんしゃが時間じかんどおりに来くるかどうか確認かくにんします。
I will check whether the train will arrive on time.
このカードが使つかえるかどうか聞きいてみます。
I will ask whether this card can be used.
来年らいねん、留学りゅうがくするかどうかまだ決きめていません。
I have not decided whether to study abroad next year.
かどうか is not the same as どうか alone

Treat かどうか as one useful expression meaning “whether (or not).” You do not need to add a second negative clause after it.

05 〜か vs. 〜かどうか — Important Difference

The most important point: question word + か asks for specific information. 〜かどうか makes a yes/no question explicit. However, 〜か alone can also express “whether” in a yes/no embedded question.

WHO? — question word + か

誰だれが来くるか分わかりません。
I do not know WHO will come.

YES / NO? — かどうか

彼かれが来くるかどうか分わかりません。
I do not know WHETHER he will come.

YES / NO? — か alone also works

彼かれが来くるか分わかりません。
I do not know whether he will come. (Also natural.)

WHERE? — question word + か

彼かれがどこに行いくか分わかりません。
I do not know WHERE he is going.
Do not overgeneralize

It is not true that か is only for question words. Both 来くるか分わからない and 来くるかどうか分わからない can mean “I do not know whether they will come.” The longer form clearly emphasizes the yes/no choice.

06 NOUN & な-Adjective Connections

For present positive NOUNs and な-adjectives, attach か / かどうか directly. Do not put だ or な before か. Past forms can use だった.

NOUN + か / かどうか (not だかどうか)
な-adjective + か / かどうか
NOUN / な-adjective + だった + かどうか (past)

NOUN — present

あの人ひとが学生がくせいかどうか知しりません。
I do not know whether that person is a student.

な-adjective — present

この町まちが便利べんりかどうか調しらべます。
I will check whether this town is convenient.

NOUN — past

彼かれが去年きょねん、学生がくせいだったかどうか覚おぼえていません。
I do not remember whether he was a student last year.

な-adjective — past

この店みせが昔むかし有名ゆうめいだったかどうか知しりません。
I do not know whether this shop used to be famous.
About 〜なのか

〜なのか also exists in Japanese, but it is a related construction using の. For the basic N4 pattern on this page, remember 学生がくせいかどうか and 便利べんりかどうか.

07 Past, Negative & い-Adjectives

The clause before か or かどうか can be past or negative. い-adjectives also connect using their plain forms. Think of the question clause as a small sentence inside the bigger sentence.

Past verb

昨日きのう、彼かれが来きたかどうか知しりません。
I do not know whether he came yesterday.

Negative verb

彼かれが今日きょう来こないかどうか確認かくにんします。
I will check whether he is not coming today.

い-adjective — present

この問題もんだいが難むずかしいかどうか分わかりません。
I do not know whether this question is difficult.

い-adjective — past

昨日きのう、忙いそがしかったかどうか聞きいてみます。
I will ask whether they were busy yesterday.
Natural choice for negative meaning

If your meaning is simply “whether he will come or not,” 来くるかどうか is usually the clearest form. 来こないかどうか specifically asks whether “not coming” is true, so the nuance is different.

08 Useful Verbs After 〜か / 〜かどうか

These patterns often appear with verbs about knowing, checking, asking, deciding, and remembering. The main verb tells you what the speaker does with the information.

何なにが必要ひつようか教おしえてください。
Please tell me what is needed.
予約よやくできるかどうか確認かくにんします。
I will check whether I can make a reservation.
どの本ほんを読よむか決きめました。
I decided which book to read.
試験しけんに合格ごうかくしたかどうかまだ分わかりません。
I still do not know whether I passed the exam.
何時なんじに出発しゅっぱつするか聞きいてください。
Please ask what time they will depart.
この店みせが開あいているかどうか調しらべます。
I will look up whether this shop is open.
A decision can use either pattern

何なにを買かうか決きめる = decide what to buy.
買かうかどうか決きめる = decide whether to buy it. The decision is different.

09 Direct Questions vs. Questions Inside Sentences

A direct question can end with か. But when you add another verb such as 分わかりません or 知しっていますか, the question becomes part of a longer sentence.

Direct question

誰だれが来きますか。
Who is coming?

Question inside a sentence

誰だれが来くるか知しっていますか。
Do you know who is coming?

Direct yes/no question

明日あした、雨あめが降ふりますか。
Will it rain tomorrow?

Yes/no question inside a sentence

明日あした、雨あめが降ふるかどうか分わかりません。
I do not know whether it will rain tomorrow.
There can be two different か sounds

誰だれが来くるか知しっていますか。 The first か ends the question inside the sentence (“who is coming”); the last か makes the whole sentence a question (“do you know?”).

10 Word Order — Keep the Whole Question Together

Put か / かどうか after the entire question clause, not immediately after the question word. The question clause comes before the main verb.

Correct — question word stays inside

誰だれが来くるか分わかりません。
I do not know who is coming.

Incorrect word order

✕ 誰だれかが来くる分わかりません。
Do not put か directly after the question word in this construction.

Correct — yes/no question

彼かれが来くるかどうか確認かくにんします。
I will check whether he is coming.

Incorrect — polite form inside

✕ 彼かれが来きますかどうか確認かくにんします。
Use the plain form before かどうか.
Be careful with dareka (someone)

誰だれか by itself means “someone” in many sentences: 誰だれかが来きました。 = Someone came. That is not the same as 誰だれが来きたか分わかりません。 = I do not know who came.

11 Everyday Conversations

These expressions are useful when making plans, checking schedules, and asking for information in daily life.

A：会議かいぎは何時なんじに始はじまりますか。
B：まだ何時なんじに始はじまるか分わかりません。
A: What time does the meeting start? B: I do not know what time it starts yet.
A：明日あした、彼かれは来きますか。
B：来くるかどうかまだ分わかりません。
A: Is he coming tomorrow? B: I do not know whether he is coming yet.
A：どの電車でんしゃに乗のりますか。
B：どの電車でんしゃに乗のるか調しらべています。
A: Which train will you take? B: I am checking which train to take.
A：この店みせでカードは使つかえますか。
B：使つかえるかどうか聞きいてみます。
A: Can I use a card at this shop? B: I will ask whether cards are accepted.

12 Common Mistakes

WHO? — do not use かどうか

✕ 誰だれが来くるかどうか分わかりません。
✓ 誰だれが来くるか分わかりません。
To ask WHO will come, use the question word + か.

NOUN — no だ in this pattern

✕ 先生せんせいだかどうか知しりません。
✓ 先生せんせいかどうか知しりません。
Attach かどうか directly to a present positive NOUN.

Verb — use plain form

✕ 雨あめが降ふりますかどうか分わかりません。
✓ 雨あめが降ふるかどうか分わかりません。
Use the plain form before かどうか.

WHAT? vs. WHETHER?

何なにを食たべるか決きめます。
食たべるかどうか決きめます。
Decide WHAT to eat vs. decide WHETHER to eat.
One more important distinction

誰だれか = someone; 誰だれが来くるか = who is coming. The position of か changes the meaning.

13 Practice — Choose か or かどうか

Choose the most natural expression. For items asking for an explicit “whether or not” form, choose かどうか even though か alone can sometimes also work. Then open the answers to check your reasoning.

Show answers

1. か — The question word asks WHO.

2. かどうか — The sentence asks whether he will come. Just か is also possible, but かどうか makes the yes/no meaning explicit.

3. か — The question word いつ asks WHEN.

4. かどうか — The sentence asks whether it will rain.

5. かどうか — Present positive NOUN + かどうか; do not add だ.

6. かどうか — Present positive な-adjective + かどうか; do not add な.

7. か — The question word asks WHICH book.

8. かどうか — The decision is whether to study abroad at all.

9. かどうか — A past plain-form verb can come before かどうか.

10. か — どうやって asks HOW.

11. はい — Yes. A yes/no embedded question may use か alone.

12. Someone came. — Dareka means someone, not an embedded who-question.

13. か — What to buy: a specific item is being chosen.

14. かどうか — Whether to buy: the yes/no choice is being made.
