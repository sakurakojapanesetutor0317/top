N4 GRAMMAR · COMPLETION & REGRET

Completion & Regret 〜てしまう・〜ちゃう

Finish completely / End up doing something

Use 〜てしまう for a completed action, or to express regret or an unwanted result. In conversation, it often becomes 〜ちゃう / 〜じゃう.

N4Completion / RegretVerb て-form + しまう

01 What Does 〜てしまう Mean?

Verb て-form + しまう has two common meanings. It can emphasize that something is completely finished, or show the speaker's feelings about something that happened, such as regret, disappointment, or surprise.

Verb て-form + しまう / しまいます

① Completion — completely finished

宿題しゅくだいを全部ぜんぶやってしまいました。
I finished all my homework.

② Regret — an unwanted result

財布さいふを忘わすれてしまいました。
Oh no, I forgot my wallet.
Important

〜てしまう does not automatically mean “by accident.” The context, verb, and speaker's feelings tell you which meaning is intended. A completed action can be intentional, too.

02 How to Make It — Start with the て-Form

First change the verb to its て-form. Then add しまう (plain) or しまいます (polite). Keep で for verbs whose て-form ends in で.

食たべます → 食たべてしまいます
eat it all / end up eating
読よみます → 読よんでしまいます
finish reading / end up reading
買かいます → 買かってしまいます
buy it all / end up buying
忘わすれます → 忘わすれてしまいます
unfortunately forget
て vs. で

読よんでしまう is correct; 読よんてしまう is not. The て-form itself does not change when you add しまう.

03 Meaning 1 — Finishing Something Completely

Use 〜てしまう to emphasize that an action is finished or done all the way. Words like もう (already), 全部ぜんぶ (all), and 最後さいごまで (to the end) often make this meaning clear.

この本ほんはもう読よんでしまいました。
I've already finished reading this book.
今日きょう中じゅうにレポートを書かいてしまいます。
I'll finish writing the report today.
ケーキを全部ぜんぶ食たべてしまいました。
I ate the whole cake.
休やすみの間あいだに宿題しゅくだいをやってしまおう。
Let's get the homework finished during the break.
Not necessarily negative

When the speaker is simply emphasizing completion, 〜てしまう can be neutral or even positive. “I finished my homework” does not have to express regret.

04 Meaning 2 — Regret, Mistakes, and Unwanted Results

Use 〜てしまう when something happens and the speaker feels unhappy, disappointed, surprised, or sorry about it. English translations may use “unfortunately,” “oops,” or “ended up,” but the Japanese sentence does not need those words.

電車でんしゃで寝ねてしまいました。
I accidentally fell asleep on the train.
大切たいせつな写真しゃしんを消けしてしまいました。
I unfortunately deleted an important photo.
約束やくそくの時間じかんに遅おくれてしまいました。
I'm sorry — I was late for our appointment.
雨あめが降ふってきて、服ふくが濡ぬれてしまいました。
It started raining, and my clothes got wet.
Regret ≠ accidental action

食たべてしまった can mean “I ate it all” or “I shouldn't have eaten it.” The action may have been intentional; the speaker can regret it afterward.

05 Same Form, Different Feeling

The same sentence can express completion or regret. Listen for context, tone, and the surrounding conversation.

Completion

全部ぜんぶ食たべてしまった！
I finished it all! (The speaker may be satisfied.)

Regret

ダイエット中ちゅうなのに、全部ぜんぶ食たべてしまった…。
Even though I'm on a diet, I ended up eating it all…
How to decide

Ask yourself: Is the speaker focusing on finishing the action, or reacting to its result? Both meanings can overlap.

06 Polite, Plain, and Past Forms

Only しまう changes for politeness and tense. The main verb stays in its て-form. The past form is especially common when talking about mistakes that already happened.

FormExampleMeaning
Polite present / future食たべてしまいますwill finish eating / will end up eating
Plain present / future食たべてしまうfinish / end up eating
Polite past食たべてしまいましたfinished / ended up eating
Plain past食たべてしまったfinished / ended up eating (casual)
Polite negative食たべてしまいませんdo not finish / will not end up eating
Volitional食たべてしまおうlet's finish eating it
Past form in conversation

For an event that has already happened, you will often hear 〜てしまいました (polite) or 〜てしまった (plain).

07 Casual Speech — 〜ちゃう / 〜じゃう

In everyday conversation, 〜てしまう often becomes 〜ちゃう, and 〜でしまう becomes 〜じゃう. These contractions are common in speech and casual writing.

〜てしまう → 〜ちゃう　／　〜でしまう → 〜じゃう

あっ、鍵かぎを忘わすれちゃった！
Oh no, I forgot my keys!
その本ほん、もう読よんじゃった。
I've already finished that book. (casual)
Politeness

Use 〜てしまいました in polite speech. You may also hear 〜ちゃいました / 〜じゃいました: they have a polite ending but still sound conversational.

08 〜てしまう vs. 〜ておく vs. 〜ている

All three use the verb's て-form, but they express different ideas.

〜てしまう — completion or regret

宿題しゅくだいをやってしまいました。
I finished the homework. (completion)

〜ておく — preparation

明日あしたのために宿題しゅくだいをやっておきました。
I did my homework in advance for tomorrow.

〜ている — action in progress

今いま、宿題しゅくだいをやっています。
I'm doing my homework now.

〜てある — resulting prepared state

机つくえの上うえに資料しりょうが置おいてあります。
The materials have been placed on the desk.
Remember the focus

〜てしまう = finishing or reacting to a result. 〜ておく = doing something beforehand. 〜ている = an ongoing action or state. 〜てある = a resulting state from an intentional action.

09 Common Mistakes

① Forgetting the て-form

✕ 食たべますしまいました。
✓ 食たべてしまいました。 — I finished eating / ended up eating.

② Mixing up ちゃう and じゃう

✓ 食たべてしまう → 食たべちゃう
✓ 読よんでしまう → 読よんじゃう

③ Assuming it always means a mistake

仕事しごとを終おわらせてしまいました can simply mean “I got the work finished.” Look at the context before choosing a negative English translation.
