N4 GRAMMAR · GIVING & RECEIVING

〜てあげる・〜てくれる・〜てもらう

Doing something for someone / Receiving help

Learn how to describe favors from different viewpoints, mark the people involved with に, and make polite requests.

N4て-FormGiving & Receiving

01 Three Ways to Describe a Favor

Use verb て-form + あげる / くれる / もらう to describe an action that benefits someone. The same event can be described differently depending on whose viewpoint you choose.

〜てあげる — I / someone does something for another person.
〜てくれる — Someone does something for me or someone close to me.
〜てもらう — I / someone receives help from another person.
私わたしは友達ともだちに日本語にほんごを教おしえてあげました。
I taught my friend Japanese as a favor.
友達ともだちが私わたしに日本語にほんごを教おしえてくれました。
My friend taught me Japanese as a favor.
私わたしは友達ともだちに日本語にほんごを教おしえてもらいました。
I received Japanese lessons from my friend.
The main difference

These expressions do not simply mean “give” and “receive” objects. They describe who does an action for whom and how the speaker views that help.

02 How to Make It — Start with the て-Form

First change the verb to its て-form. Then attach あげます, くれます, or もらいます. The verb in front describes the action being done.

書かきます → 書かいてあげます
write for someone
読よみます → 読よんでくれます
read for me / us
手伝てつだいます → 手伝てつだってもらいます
get help
教おしえます → 教おしえてくれます
teach me / us
The ending changes, not the て-form

Past: 〜てあげました / 〜てくれました / 〜てもらいました. Negative: 〜てくれませんでした / 〜てもらいませんでした.

03 〜てあげる — Doing Something for Someone

Use 〜てあげる when the subject performs an action for the benefit of another person. The beneficiary is commonly marked with に.

PERSON doing the favor は + PERSON receiving help に + OBJECT を + VERB て-form + あげます
私わたしは妹いもうとに宿題しゅくだいを手伝てつだってあげました。
I helped my younger sister with her homework.
私わたしは弟おとうとに本ほんを読よんであげました。
I read a book to my younger brother.
Be careful with the nuance

Saying 〜てあげる about your own favor can sound as though you are emphasizing how kind you were, especially when speaking directly to the person you helped. In polite situations, simply offering help (e.g. お手伝てつだいしましょうか) may be more natural.

04 〜てくれる — Someone Does Something for Me

Use 〜てくれる when another person does something that benefits you or someone you identify closely with. The person doing the favor is the subject, often marked by が. If the recipient is stated, use に.

PERSON doing the favor が + ME / MY IN-GROUP に + OBJECT を + VERB て-form + くれます
友達ともだちが私わたしに日本語にほんごを教おしえてくれました。
My friend taught me Japanese.
兄あにが私わたしに傘かさを貸かしてくれました。
My older brother lent me an umbrella.
母ははが晩ばんご飯はんを作つくってくれました。
My mother cooked dinner for me.
You can omit 私に

When it is already clear that the action was done for you, the recipient is often left out: 友達ともだちが手伝てつだってくれました。 — My friend helped me.

05 〜てもらう — Receiving Help from Someone

Use 〜てもらう when the person receiving the favor is the subject. The person who performs the action is commonly marked by に.

PERSON receiving help は + PERSON doing the favor に + OBJECT を + VERB て-form + もらいます
私わたしは友達ともだちに写真しゃしんを撮とってもらいました。
I had my friend take a photo for me.
私わたしは先生せんせいに作文さくぶんを直なおしてもらいました。
I had my teacher correct my essay.
私わたしは母ははに駅えきまで送おくってもらいました。
My mother gave me a ride to the station.
Does もらう mean “force someone”?

No. 〜てもらう expresses receiving the benefit of an action. It does not, by itself, mean that you forced the other person to do it.

06 Compare the Viewpoints — Same Event, Different Subject

These two sentences can describe the same favor. Look at who is marked by が / は and who is marked by に.

友達ともだちが私わたしに写真しゃしんを撮とってくれました。
My friend took a photo for me. (Focus: the friend who helped)
私わたしは友達ともだちに写真しゃしんを撮とってもらいました。
I had my friend take a photo. (Focus: me receiving the help)
〜てくれる: Helper が → Recipient に
〜てもらう: Recipient は → Helper に
The person marked by に changes role

With 〜てくれる, に marks the recipient. With 〜てもらう, に marks the helper. This is why translating only the particle is not enough.

07 Particles — Who Does the Action, and Who Benefits?

Use the sentence pattern to decide what に means. The object of the action, when there is one, is marked by を.

私わたしは妹いもうとに本ほんを読よんであげました。
に = person receiving the favor; を = book being read.
妹いもうとが私わたしに本ほんを読よんでくれました。
に = person receiving the favor; を = book being read.
私わたしは妹いもうとに本ほんを読よんでもらいました。
に = person doing the favor; を = book being read.
Important: に is not always the same person

In the first two examples, に marks who benefits. In the third example, に marks who performs the action. Check the ending: あげる / くれる / もらう.

08 Asking Someone for a Favor — Polite Requests

For requests, use a question form instead of directly stating that someone does you a favor. The following patterns become progressively more polite.

写真しゃしんを撮とってくれますか。
Will you take a photo for me?
写真しゃしんを撮とってもらえますか。
Could you take a photo for me?
写真しゃしんを撮とってもらえませんか。
Would you mind taking a photo for me?
写真しゃしんを撮とっていただけませんか。
Could you please take a photo for me? (more formal)
Extra polite forms

〜ていただく is a humble alternative to 〜てもらう. 〜てくださる is an honorific alternative to 〜てくれる. You can learn these after you are comfortable with the basic three patterns.

09 Common Mistakes and Natural Usage

① Using くれる when the benefit is for someone unrelated to the speaker

私わたしは友達ともだちに宿題しゅくだいを手伝てつだってあげました。
I helped my friend with their homework. (I did the favor.)
友達ともだちが私わたしに宿題しゅくだいを手伝てつだってくれました。
My friend helped me with my homework. (I received the favor.)

② Confusing 〜てもらう and the passive

私わたしは友達ともだちに写真しゃしんを撮とってもらいました。
My friend took a photo for me. (A favor)
私わたしは友達ともだちに写真しゃしんを撮とられました。
My friend took my photo. (Passive; does not necessarily mean it was a favor)
Focus on the benefit

The passive describes something being done to the subject. 〜てもらう / 〜てくれる present the action as beneficial. The two forms are not interchangeable in every situation.
