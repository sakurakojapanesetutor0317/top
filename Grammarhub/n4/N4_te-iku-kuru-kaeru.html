〜ていく・〜てくる・〜て帰かえる

Go and come back · Take / bring · Say / ask · Changes over time

You already know the basic movement patterns from N5. Now learn how Japanese speakers use them to report trips, bring things, pass on messages, and describe changes in everyday conversation.

Te-form

01 Start Here — From N5 to Real Conversation

At N5, you learned 行いく・来くる・帰かえる, the purpose pattern ます-stem + に行いく, and basic combinations such as 持もっていく. This page focuses on what these expressions communicate in a real conversation, rather than repeating the basic conjugation rules.

Verb て-form + いく / くる / 帰かえる
Movement, viewpoint, and what happens before or after a trip

Going somewhere

今いまからコンビニに行いく。
I’m going to the convenience store. (Focus: going)

Going and coming back

ちょっとコンビニに行いってくる。
I’ll pop over to the convenience store and come back.

Reporting a trip

昨日きのう、友達ともだちと映画えいがを見みに行いってきた。
I went to see a movie with a friend (and I’m back).

Doing something before going home

駅えきで晩ばんごはんを食たべて帰かえった。
I had dinner at the station before going home.
Review the N5 foundation

If you need the basic difference between 行いく・来くる・帰かえる, or 食たべに行いく versus 食たべてくる, see the N5 movement page →. Here we build on those patterns.

02 行いく vs. 行いってくる vs. 行いってきた

行いく simply says that you go somewhere. 行いってくる adds the expectation of returning. 行いってきた reports a trip after you have returned. The trip does not have to be recent.

今日きょうは図書館としょかんに行いく。
I’m going to the library today.
ちょっと郵便局ゆうびんきょくに行いってくるね。
I’m going to the post office for a bit. I’ll be back.
週末しゅうまつ、京都きょうとに行いってきたよ。
I went to Kyoto over the weekend.
昼休ひるやすみに銀行ぎんこうに行いってきました。
I went to the bank during my lunch break (and returned).
Natural conversation

A：週末しゅうまつ、何なにしてた？
B：大阪おおさかに行いってきたよ。友達ともだちに会あってきた。
A: What did you do over the weekend? / B: I went to Osaka. I met up with a friend.
Both 行いった and 行いってきた are grammatical. The second naturally presents the trip as an experience you are reporting after returning.

03 〜に行いってくる vs. 〜てくる — Purpose or Completed Action?

These patterns are related, but 〜に行いく expresses the purpose of going, while 〜てくる can describe doing the action and coming back. The difference matters especially in the past tense.

Purpose: went to do something

映画えいがを見みに行いってきた。
I went to see a movie and came back. Focus: why I went.

Action: did it and returned

映画えいがを見みてきた。
I watched a movie and came back. The watching happened.

Purpose: went to buy it

新あたらしい靴くつを買かいに行いってきた。
I went to buy new shoes. This does not by itself confirm I bought them.

Action: bought it and returned

新あたらしい靴くつを買かってきた。
I bought new shoes and came back with them.
Not always interchangeable

“I went to buy it” does not guarantee that the purchase happened. “I bought it and came back” does. In natural conversation, the surrounding context can make the distinction less important, but the basic focus is different.

04 持もっていく・持もってくる・持もって帰かえる — What Is the Viewpoint?

You studied these combinations at N5. At N4, practice choosing the right one in real situations. English bring does not always correspond to Japanese 持もってくる: the speaker’s viewpoint matters.

明日あしたのパーティー、何なにか持もっていったほうがいい？
Should I bring anything to tomorrow’s party? (I’ll go there.)
じゃあ、私わたしが飲のみ物ものを持もっていくね。
Okay, I’ll bring the drinks. (I’ll take them there.)
明日あした、うちに来くるとき、写真しゃしんを持もってきてね。
When you come over tomorrow, bring the photos.
このケーキ、余あまったから持もって帰かえっていいよ。
There’s leftover cake, so you can take it home.
Imagine the direction

持もっていく = take something away from the speaker’s viewpoint toward a destination.
持もってくる = bring something toward the speaker’s viewpoint.
持もって帰かえる = take something home / back. The viewpoint can also be the location or situation being discussed.

05 言いってくる — “Go Tell Someone” or “Someone Says It to Me”

言いってくる has two very useful conversational readings. You can often tell which one is intended from who is speaking and who receives the message.

A. Go and tell someone

ちょっと店長てんちょうに言いってくる。
I’ll go tell the manager (and come back).

B. Someone says something to me

同僚どうりょうが毎回まいかい『早はやくして』って言いってくる。
My coworker keeps telling me to hurry up.
先生せんせいに事情じじょうを言いってきた。
I went and explained the situation to the teacher.
友達ともだちが『手伝てつだおうか？』って言いってきた。
My friend offered to help me.
母ははが毎日まいにち『早はやく寝ねなさい』って言いってくる。
My mother tells me to go to bed early every day.
知しらない人ひとが急きゅうに話はなしかけてきた。
A stranger suddenly started talking to me.
Does 言いってくる sound annoyed?

Not necessarily. It can be neutral, friendly, surprising, or annoying, depending on context and words such as 毎回まいかい (every time) or しつこく (persistently). In the second use, the message is directed toward the speaker; the other person does not have to physically travel.

06 聞きいてくる・連絡れんらくしてくる — Questions and Messages Coming Toward You

The same idea works with other communication verbs. 聞きいてくる can mean “go ask and return” or “someone asks me.” 連絡れんらくしてくる means someone contacts you.

受付うけつけの人ひとに空あいている時間じかんを聞きいてくるね。
I’ll go ask the receptionist what times are available.
友達ともだちが急きゅうに『明日あした、暇ひま？』って聞きいてきた。
My friend suddenly asked me if I was free tomorrow.
昨日きのう、昔むかしの同級生どうきゅうせいが連絡れんらくしてきた。
An old classmate contacted me yesterday.
困こまったら、いつでも連絡れんらくしてきてね。
If you have any trouble, feel free to contact me.
Watch the particles

私わたしが先生せんせいに聞きいてきた。 = I went and asked the teacher.
先生せんせいが私わたしに聞きいてきた。 = The teacher asked me. が marks who asks; に marks the person being asked.

07 〜て帰かえる vs. 〜てくる vs. 帰かえってくる

〜て帰かえる describes doing something and then going home or back to one’s base. 〜てくる describes doing something and returning toward the viewpoint. 帰かえってくる simply says someone comes back home or returns here.

Do something, then go home

仕事しごとのあとで買かい物ものをして帰かえる。
I’ll go shopping after work, then go home.

Do something, then come back

ちょっと買かい物ものをしてくる。
I’ll go shopping and come back.

Return home / here

父ちちは夜よる10時じごろ帰かえってくる。
My father comes home around 10 p.m.

Do something and go home

映画えいがを見みてから帰かえった。
I went home after watching the movie.
Why not always use 帰かえってくる?

帰かえる focuses on returning to a home or base. 帰かえってくる additionally presents that return as movement toward the speaker’s viewpoint. 〜て帰かえる focuses on what happens before going home.

08 〜てくる・〜ていく — Changes and Continuation Over Time

These uses go beyond physical movement. 〜てくる can describe a change that has developed up to now or a new feeling that is emerging. 〜ていく often describes a change or continued action from now onward.

最近さいきん、日本語にほんごが少すこしずつ聞きき取とれるようになってきた。
Recently, I’ve gradually become better at understanding spoken Japanese.
なんだか眠ねむくなってきた。
I’m starting to feel sleepy.
これからもっと寒さむくなっていくね。
It’s going to keep getting colder from now on.
これからも毎日まいにち、日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうしていきたい。
I want to keep studying Japanese every day from now on.
A useful starting point, not an absolute rule

〜てきた often looks back at developments leading to the present; 〜ていく often looks ahead to what will continue or develop. Their meanings also depend on the verb and the viewpoint, so do not translate every occurrence as “come” or “go.”

09 Common Mistakes & Nuance Checks

① Going somewhere is not the same as reporting your return

京都きょうとに行いった。 is perfectly natural. 京都きょうとに行いってきた。 is also natural, but presents it as a trip you made and returned from. The first is not wrong.

② 〜に行いってきた does not guarantee success

靴くつを買かいに行いってきた。 = I went to buy shoes (perhaps the store had none).
靴くつを買かってきた。 = I bought shoes and returned.

③ 言いってきた can point in two directions

私わたしが店長てんちょうに言いってきた。 = I went and told the manager.
店長てんちょうが私わたしに言いってきた。 = The manager said it to me. Look at the subject and recipient.

④ 〜て帰かえる is not automatically “come back here”

外そとでごはんを食たべて帰かえる。 = I’ll eat out and go home. It does not mean that I will return to the listener’s location.

Choose the most natural expression in this context.
10. 友達ともだちがLINEで「来週らいしゅう、会あえる？」って聞きいて（きた・いった）。
Choose the most natural expression in this context.
Show answers & explanations

1. 行いってくる — the speaker plans to return shortly.

2. Both are grammatical. 行いってきた is a natural choice when reporting a trip after returning.

3. 買かいに行いってきた — the speaker went shopping but did not buy shoes.

4. 持もっていく — from the speaker’s current viewpoint, the drinks are going to the party.

5. 言いってきた — the boss said it to me.

6. 聞きいてくる — I will go ask the teacher and return.

7. 帰かえる — the speaker will go home after eating.

8. なってきた — the change has developed up to now.

9. いきたい — the speaker wants to continue from now onward.

10. きた — the friend asked the speaker.
