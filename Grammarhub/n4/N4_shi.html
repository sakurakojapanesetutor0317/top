N4 GRAMMAR · GIVING REASONS

〜し

And / Also / Because … (listing reasons)

Use 〜し to give more than one reason, list supporting points, or suggest that there are other reasons too. Learn how it connects to verbs, adjectives, and nouns.

01 What Does 〜し Mean?

〜し connects reasons or supporting points. It often means “and,” “also,” or “because … and …” depending on the context.

Reason A + し、Reason B + し、result / opinion
この店みせは安やすいし、おいしいし、よく来きます。
This restaurant is inexpensive and the food is good, so I come here often.
今日きょうは雨あめだし、寒さむいし、家いえにいます。
It's rainy and cold today, so I'm staying home.
Reasons, not just a list

〜し often gives reasons for a choice or opinion. It can also simply add information, but the speaker usually has a point they want to support.

02 How to Connect 〜し

For the basic pattern, attach し to the plain form. Pay special attention to だ after nouns and な-adjectives.

Verb — plain form + し

時間じかんもあるし、行いきましょう。
We have time, so let's go.

い-adjective + し

安やすいし、おいしいです。
It's inexpensive and delicious.

な-adjective + だし

静しずかだし、広ひろいです。
It's quiet and spacious.

Noun + だし

明日あしたは休やすみだし、ゆっくりできます。
Tomorrow is a day off, so I can relax.

Negative / past + し

高たかくないし、前まえにも使つかったし、これにします。
It isn't expensive, and I've used it before, so I'll choose this one.

Verb — past + し

予約よやくしたし、準備じゅんびもできました。
I've made the reservation, and the preparations are done.
Important: な-adjectives and nouns need だ

✕ 便利べんりし → ✓ 便利べんりだし
✕ 学生がくせいし → ✓ 学生がくせいだし

Polite forms such as 便利べんりですし and 行いきますし are also possible in polite speech. Start with the plain-form patterns above.

03 Listing Two or More Reasons

Use 〜し several times to explain why you like something, choose something, or decide to do something.

このアパートは駅えきから近ちかいし、部屋へやも広ひろいし、ここに住すみたいです。
The apartment is close to the station and spacious, so I'd like to live here.
田中たなかさんは親切しんせつだし、日本語にほんごも上手じょうずだし、いい先生せんせいです。
Tanaka is kind and good at Japanese, so they're a good teacher.
仕事しごとが忙いそがしいし、疲つかれているし、今日きょうは早はやく寝ねます。
I'm busy with work and tired, so I'll go to bed early today.
Adding も can sound natural

Notice 部屋へやも and 日本語にほんごも. The particle も often helps emphasize that you are adding another reason, but it is not required every time.

04 Using Just One 〜し

You do not need two 〜し clauses. One 〜し can suggest “that's one reason, and there may be others.”

明日あしたは休やすみだし、映画えいがを見みに行いこう。
Tomorrow's a day off, so let's go see a movie. (That's one reason.)
もう遅おそいし、帰かえりましょう。
It's getting late, so let's head home.
この店みせはおいしいし、おすすめですよ。
The food here is good, so I'd recommend it.
Natural in conversation

A sentence with a single 〜し can sound softer or more open-ended than giving one firm reason with 〜から. It often leaves room for other reasons.

05 Negative and Past Forms

The form before し can be negative or past. Use the same plain-form rules you already know.

Verb — negative

時間じかんがないし、今日きょうは行いけません。
I don't have time, so I can't go today.

Verb — past

宿題しゅくだいも終おわったし、休やすみましょう。
We've finished the homework, so let's take a break.

い-adjective — negative

高たかくないし、買かいやすいです。
It isn't expensive, and it's easy to buy.

な-adjective — negative

静しずかじゃないし、ここでは勉強べんきょうできません。
It isn't quiet, so I can't study here.

06 〜し vs. 〜から / 〜ので

All three can explain reasons, but they are not interchangeable in every situation.

〜し — adding reasons

安やすいし、便利べんりだし、ここにします。
It's inexpensive and convenient, so I'll choose this one. (Multiple points)

〜から — stating a reason

安やすいから、ここにします。
I'll choose this one because it's inexpensive. (A direct reason)

〜ので — giving a reason gently

用事ようじがあるので、先さきに帰かえります。
I have something to do, so I'll leave first. (A relatively gentle explanation)
Choose based on your purpose

Use 〜し to add supporting points, 〜から to state a reason directly, and 〜ので for a reason that often sounds softer or more explanatory. The difference depends on context, not a fixed politeness ranking.

07 それで — That Is Why / And So

それで connects two sentences. The first sentence gives a reason or situation, and the next sentence gives the result. It often comes after sentences with 〜し, but it can follow any sentence.

Reason / situation。 それで、result。
この店みせは駅えきに近ちかいし、安やすいです。それで、よく利用りようします。
This shop is near the station and inexpensive. That is why I use it often.
昨日きのうは熱ねつがありました。それで、学校がっこうを休やすみました。
I had a fever yesterday. So I stayed home from school.
A：来月らいげつ、日本にほんで働はたらくんです。
B：それで、日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうしているんですね。
A: I am going to work in Japan next month.
B: Oh, so that is why you are studying Japanese.

それで — a result that is a fact

道みちが混こんでいました。それで、遅おくれました。
The roads were crowded. That is why I was late.

だから — also before requests and suggestions

もうすぐ雨あめが降ふります。だから、傘かさを持もって行いってください。
It will rain soon, so please take an umbrella.
それで is not used before requests or invitations

After それで, describe a result or a fact. For a request, a suggestion, an invitation, or your own decision, use だから or ですから: ✕ それで、傘かさを持もって行いってください。 ✓ だから、傘かさを持もって行いってください。

In conversation, それで？ on its own means “And then?” — it asks the speaker to continue.

08 Common Mistakes

Remember だ after な-adjectives

✕ 便利べんりし
✓ 便利べんりだし
Use だし after a な-adjective in the plain form.

Remember だ after nouns

✕ 休やすみし
✓ 休やすみだし
Use だし after a noun in the plain form.

Don't add だ to い-adjectives

✕ 安やすいだし
✓ 安やすいし
い-adjectives connect directly to し.

Don't confuse し with から

安やすいし、近ちかいし、ここがいいです。
〜し naturally lists multiple reasons or supporting points.
