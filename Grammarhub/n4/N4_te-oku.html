N4 GRAMMAR · PREPARATION & MAINTAINING A STATE

〜ておく

Do something in advance / Leave something as it is

Use 〜ておく to prepare for a future situation, or to intentionally keep something in a certain state.

N4Preparation / StateVerb て-form + おく

01 What Does 〜ておく Mean?

Verb て-form + おく has two important uses. Both involve doing something intentionally because it will be useful later, or because you want to keep the resulting situation.

Verb て-form + おく / おきます

① Preparation — do it beforehand

旅行りょこうの前まえにホテルを予約よやくしておきます。
I’ll book a hotel before the trip.

② Maintaining a state — leave it that way

窓まどは開あけておいてください。
Please leave the window open.
The key idea

〜ておく is not simply “do something.” It adds the idea of preparation or deliberately leaving something in a particular state.

02 How to Make It — Start with the て-Form

Change the verb to its て-form, then add おく (plain) or おきます (polite). The main verb tells us what action is done.

買かいます → 買かっておきます
buy in advance
読よみます → 読よんでおきます
read beforehand
調しらべます → 調しらべておきます
check / look up in advance
準備じゅんびします → 準備じゅんびしておきます
prepare beforehand
Remember

With a verb ending in 〜で (e.g. 読よんで), keep the で: 読よんでおく, not 読よんておく.

03 Meaning 1 — Preparing for Something Later

Use 〜ておく for an action completed before a future event, so you are ready when the time comes.

明日あしたの授業じゅぎょうのために予習よしゅうしておきます。
I’ll prepare for tomorrow’s lesson in advance.
旅行りょこうの前まえに切符きっぷを買かっておきました。
I bought the tickets before the trip.
会議かいぎの前まえに資料しりょうを読よんでおいてください。
Please read the materials before the meeting.
日本にほんへ行いく前まえに、少すこし日本語にほんごを勉強べんきょうしておこうと思おもいます。
I think I’ll study some Japanese before going to Japan.
Common time expressions

〜の前まえに (before), 〜までに (by), 〜のために (for), and 〜ときのために (in case / for when) often appear with preparation.

04 Meaning 2 — Leaving Something in a Certain State

Use 〜ておく when you intentionally keep the result of an action. The focus is on what someone chooses to do or leave unchanged.

暑あついので、窓まどを開あけておきます。
It’s hot, so I’ll leave the window open.
電気でんきはつけておいてください。
Please leave the light on.
その本ほんは机つくえの上うえに置おいておいてください。
Please leave that book on the desk.
まだ使つかうので、このままにしておきましょう。
We’ll use it again, so let’s leave it as it is.
Why are there two おいて in 置いておいて?

The first 置おいて is the て-form of 置おく (“to put”). The second おいて comes from the grammar pattern 〜ておく. Together they mean “put it somewhere and leave it there.”

05 Polite, Plain, Past, and Requests

Only おく changes to show tense, politeness, or a request. The first verb stays in its て-form.

FormExampleMeaning
Polite present / future買かっておきますI’ll buy it in advance.
Plain present / future買かっておくI’ll buy it beforehand. (casual)
Polite past買かっておきましたI bought it in advance.
Plain past買かっておいたI bought it in advance. (casual)
Request買かっておいてくださいPlease buy it beforehand.
Volitional買かっておこうI’ll buy it beforehand / Let’s buy it.
Not always a completed action

買かっておきます can describe an intention to prepare in the future. 買かっておきました tells us the preparation has already been done.

06 Casual Speech — 〜とく / 〜どく

In everyday conversation, 〜ておく is often shortened to 〜とく, while 〜でおく becomes 〜どく. These are casual forms, not standard polite forms.

StandardCasualMeaning
買かっておく買かっとくbuy in advance
買かっておいた買かっといたbought in advance
買かっておいて買かっといてbuy beforehand / leave it bought
読よんでおく読よんどくread in advance
読よんでおいた読よんどいたread in advance (past)
明日あしたまでに調しらべとくね。
I’ll look it up by tomorrow. (casual)
そのメール、もう読よんどいたよ。
I already read that email. (casual, with preparation implied)
For learners

Use 〜ておきます in polite conversation. Recognizing 〜とく / 〜どく will help you understand natural speech.

07 〜ないでおく — Decide Not to Do Something

You can also use verb ない-form + でおく when you intentionally choose not to do something, often for the time being.

Verb ない-form + でおく / でおきます
今日きょうはお酒さけを飲のまないでおきます。
I’ll avoid drinking alcohol today.
まだ決きめないでおきましょう。
Let’s hold off on deciding for now.
〜ないでおく vs. 〜ておかない

買かわないでおく = choose not to buy it (for now). 買かっておかない = not buy it in advance. The difference is where the negative is placed.

08 〜ておく vs. 〜てある vs. 〜ている

These expressions can describe similar situations, but they focus on different things.

〜ておく — an intentional action

机つくえの上うえに資料しりょうを置おいておきました。
I put the materials on the desk in preparation.

〜てある — the resulting prepared state

机つくえの上うえに資料しりょうが置おいてあります。
The materials have been placed on the desk (and are there).

〜ている — an ongoing action or state

今いま、資料しりょうを読よんでいます。
I’m reading the materials now.

〜てしまう — completion or regret

資料しりょうを全部ぜんぶ読よんでしまいました。
I finished reading all the materials. (completion)
Focus matters

〜ておく emphasizes what someone does in preparation or deliberately maintains. 〜てある emphasizes the state that exists as a result of someone's intentional action. 〜ている can describe an ongoing action or state, depending on the verb.

09 Common Mistakes

① Forgetting the て-form

✕ 予約よやくしますおきます。
✓ 予約よやくしておきます。 — I’ll make the reservation in advance.

② Confusing preparation with simple completion

宿題しゅくだいをしました = I did my homework.
宿題しゅくだいをしておきました = I did my homework beforehand (so it would be done when needed).

③ Using the casual contraction in a formal context

With a teacher or client, prefer 確認かくにんしておきます over 確認かくにんしとく.
