SAKURA JAPANESE · N4

Lesson 31

Suggest an action casually, explain your intentions and plans, and say what you have not done yet.

01

Volitional Form — Let’s / I’ll

A casual suggestion or a decision to act.

The casual counterpart of 〜ましょう

The volitional form can suggest doing something together (“let’s…”) or express your own decision (“I’ll…”). Context tells you which: 一緒いっしょに帰かえろう invites someone to go home together; そろそろ帰かえろう can express your own decision to go home.

Start from the ます-form. Group 1: remove ます, change the final sound to the お-row, and add う. Group 2: replace ます with よう. Group 3: します → しよう and 来きます → 来こよう.

Group 1

書かきます → 書かこう
飲のみます → 飲のもう

Group 1: change the final sound before ます to its お-row sound, then add う.

Group 2

食たべます → 食たべよう
見みます → 見みよう

Group 2: replace ます with よう.

Invite someone

一緒いっしょに映画えいがを見みよう。

Let’s watch a film together.

Decide what to do

今日きょうは早はやく寝ねよう。

I’ll go to bed early tonight.

02

An Intention — 〜ようと思おもっています

Something you have been thinking of doing.

Volitional form + と思おもっています

Use this pattern for an intention or plan you have been considering. It does not mean you are doing the planned action now.

〜ようと思おもいます often presents your intention or decision at the time of speaking. 〜ようと思おもっています presents an intention you already hold. This is a useful difference, not a fixed rule about how long you have had the idea.

来年らいねん、日本にほんへ留学りゅうがくしようと思おもっています。

I am thinking of studying abroad in Japan next year.

田中たなかさんは会社かいしゃを辞やめようと思おもっています。

Tanaka is thinking of leaving the company.

03

Not Yet — まだ〜ていません

An expected action has not happened or been completed.

まだ + て-form + いません

This pattern means “have not… yet.” It tells the listener that an action is still incomplete or has not happened by now, often with the expectation that it will happen later. It is different from saying you do not intend to do it.

ホテルはもう予約よやくしましたか。
いいえ、まだ予約よやくしていません。

Have you booked a hotel yet? — No, I have not booked one yet.

旅行りょこうの準備じゅんびはまだ終おわっていません。

I have not finished preparing for the trip yet.

04

A Plan or Intention — 〜つもりです

State what you intend to do or not to do.

Dictionary form / ない-form + つもりです

Use the dictionary form + つもりです for an intended action. Use the ない-form + つもりです for an intention not to do something. This often sounds more decided than “I’m thinking of…”, but it does not guarantee that the plan will happen.

週末しゅうまつは家いえで休やすむつもりです。

I intend to rest at home this weekend.

今年ことしは車くるまを買かわないつもりです。

I intend not to buy a car this year.

05

A Scheduled Plan — 〜予定よていです

A planned or scheduled event.

Dictionary form + 予定よていです / noun + の予定よていです

Use 〜予定よていです for a plan or schedule. A verb connects in the dictionary form; a noun connects with の. It can describe your plans, someone else’s plans, or an organized event. A schedule can still change.

来月らいげつ、大阪おおさかへ出張しゅっちょうする予定よていです。

I am scheduled to go on a business trip to Osaka next month.

会議かいぎは金曜日きんようびの予定よていです。

The meeting is scheduled for Friday.

06

Lesson Focus

Match the form to the kind of plan.

Keep the connections separate

行いこうと思おもっています — “I am thinking of going.” Use the volitional form.

行いくつもりです / 行いかないつもりです — “I intend to go / not to go.” Use the dictionary form or ない-form.

行いく予定よていです / 金曜日きんようびの予定よていです — “I am scheduled to go / it is scheduled for Friday.” Use a dictionary-form verb or a noun + の.

まだ決きめていません — “I have not decided yet.” This leaves the decision open; it does not mean “I have decided not to.”
