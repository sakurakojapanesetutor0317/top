SAKURA JAPANESE · N4

Lesson 40

Put questions inside sentences, ask whether something is true, and try an action.

01

What Kind of Answer Do You Need?

A missing detail, or a yes/no answer?

Start with the original question

An embedded question is a question used as part of a larger sentence: “I do not know when she will come” or “Please check whether she will come.” You are talking about an unanswered question, rather than necessarily asking it directly.

A missing detail: “When? Where? Who? What? How many?” → keep the question word and use 〜か. The answer supplies a time, place, person, thing, or amount.

Yes or no: “Will she come?” → use 〜かどうか (“whether or not”). The issue is whether the statement is true, not which time or place fills a gap.

A time is missing — when?

田中たなかさんはいつ来きますか。
→ 田中たなかさんがいつ来くるか、分わかりません。

“When will Tanaka come?” → “I do not know when Tanaka will come.” A possible answer is “At three.” We are asking for the time, not whether Tanaka will come.

The visit itself is uncertain — yes or no?

田中たなかさんは来きますか。
→ 田中たなかさんが来くるかどうか、分わかりません。

“Will Tanaka come?” → “I do not know whether Tanaka will come.” The alternatives are “will come” and “will not come.”

What does どうか add?

In 〜かどうか, think of どうか as the “or not” part: 来くるかどうか = whether they will come or not. It is not asking “how?” here. Adding it after いつ来くる changes the structure; to ask when someone will come, use いつ来くるか, not いつ来くるかどうか.

Important: a yes/no embedded question can also use か alone: 来くるか分わかりません is also possible. かどうか makes the two alternatives explicit. So か is not restricted to question-word questions; the question-word / かどうか contrast is a useful starting pattern, not a ban on shorter か.

02

A Question Inside a Sentence

Say what you do not know or want to check.

Question word + plain clause + か + main expression

Keep the question word inside the embedded question: いつ来くるか (“when they will come”), どこにあるか (“where it is”), だれが来くるか (“who will come”). Then add 分わかりません, 教おしえてください, 確認かくにんします, or another suitable expression.

Use a plain form inside the question: 始はじまりますか → 始はじまるか; 撮とりましたか → 撮とったか. Keep the tense and negative meaning needed. Do not retain ます just because the whole sentence ends politely.

For a present affirmative noun or な-adjective, omit だ: だれですか → だれか; どこが静しずかですか → どこが静しずかか. A complete clause such as だれが来くるか is often clearer than an isolated だれか, which can also mean “someone.”

会議かいぎが何時なんじに始はじまるか、教おしえてください。

Please tell me what time the meeting starts.

この写真しゃしんをどこで撮とったか、覚おぼえていません。

I do not remember where I took this photo.

03

Whether or Not — 〜かどうか

Embed a yes/no question.

Plain clause + かどうか + main expression

Use this for an embedded yes/no question. Compare 行いけますか (“Can you go?”) → 行いけるかどうか (“whether I/you can go”). No question word is needed to supply a time, place, or person.

An い-adjective keeps its final い: 高たかいかどうか. A present affirmative noun or な-adjective connects without だ: 学生がくせいかどうか / 必要ひつようかどうか. Past and negative forms stay intact: 学生がくせいだったかどうか / 必要ひつようではないかどうか.

You can often omit どうか in a yes/no embedded question. Learn かどうか first because it makes “whether or not” especially easy to recognize.

明日あした行いけるかどうか、まだ分わかりません。

I do not know yet whether I can go tomorrow.

この書類しょるいが必要ひつようかどうか、確認かくにんします。

I will check whether this document is necessary.

One more comparison: where, or whether?

この薬くすりをどこで買かえるか、調しらべます。
“I will find out where I can buy this medicine.” → The missing answer is a place.

この薬くすりをここで買かえるかどうか、調しらべます。
“I will find out whether I can buy this medicine here.” → The place is already specified; the question is whether buying it there is possible.

04

Try an Action — 〜てみます

Do something to find out what it is like.

Verb て-form + みます

You actually perform or attempt the action to see the result. This differs from 〜ようとします, which focuses on trying or being about to do something. Write the auxiliary みる in hiragana.

この靴くつを履はいてみてもいいですか。

May I try these shoes on?

分わからなかったので、先生せんせいに聞きいてみました。

I did not understand, so I tried asking the teacher.

05

Lesson Focus

Keep the meaning, connection, and viewpoint clear.

Choose the meaning before choosing the ending

Missing detail: いつ / どこ / だれ / 何なに… + plain clause + か.
Yes/no: plain clause + かどうか; shorter か is also possible.

The final expression tells you what happens to the question: 分わかりません = “I do not know”; 教おしえてください = “please tell me”; 確認かくにんします = “I will check.”

The embedded question normally uses plain forms even when the whole sentence is polite. Omit present affirmative だ with nouns and な-adjectives before か / かどうか.
