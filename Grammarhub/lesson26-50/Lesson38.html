SAKURA JAPANESE · N4

Lesson 38

Turn actions and clauses into noun phrases and use them as parts of a larger sentence.

01

First: の Works Like こと

Turn “do something” into “doing something” — an activity or fact you can talk about.

Verb + の ≈ verb + こと: making an action into a noun phrase

You have already met dictionary form + こと, for example in Lesson 18. In the activity patterns below, の does the same basic job as こと: it makes the preceding action into a noun phrase. Think of 泳およぐ = “swim,” but 泳およぐこと / 泳およぐの = “swimming / the activity of swimming.”

This の is not possession (“of”). It does not add a new action. It lets you describe an action, like it, or use it as the object of another verb. This grammatical job is called nominalization.

日本語にほんごで日記にっきを書かくことは楽たのしいです。
＝ 日本語にほんごで日記にっきを書かくのは楽たのしいです。

Both: Writing a diary in Japanese is enjoyable. — こと and の both turn the action into the topic being described here.

私わたしは料理りょうりを作つくることが好すきです。
＝ 私わたしは料理りょうりを作つくるのが好すきです。

Both: I like cooking. — The object of your liking is the whole activity, not a particular dish.

の makes the noun phrase; は・が・を give it a role

Separate the parts as [action + の] + particle:
[泳およぐの] は — as for swimming…
[泳およぐの] が好すきです — I like swimming.
[薬くすりを飲のむの] を忘わすれました — I forgot to take my medicine.

のは, のが, and のを are not three different verb conjugations. The same noun-making の is followed by the particle needed in the larger sentence. Put the particle after の; do not leave the action as a bare verb before は・が・を in these patterns.

Same basic job does not mean interchangeable everywhere

Use の ≈ こと as the starting idea for nominalizing an action, not as a rule for replacing every の in Japanese. Both work in the two paired examples above, but some constructions select one of them.

Ability: 日本語にほんごを話はなすことができます is the pattern learned in Lesson 18; do not replace it with のができます to mean “can speak Japanese.”
Direct perception: 子供こどもが走はしっているのを見みました means “I saw a child running.” With directly seeing or hearing an event, の is the usual choice.

Also keep の in the 〜のは…です focus pattern later on this page: 日本にほんへ来きたのは3年前ねんまえです. Replacing it mechanically with こと does not preserve that “It was three years ago that…” construction.

02

An Action as the Topic — 〜のは

Say what an activity is like.

Dictionary form + のは + adjective

の turns the action into something you can talk about; は marks it as the topic. Compare “I run” with “Running is enjoyable.” Use the dictionary form here for an activity in general.

日本語にほんごで日記にっきを書かくのは楽たのしいです。

Writing a diary in Japanese is enjoyable.

毎朝まいあさ早はやく起おきるのは大変たいへんです。

Getting up early every morning is hard.

03

Likes & Skills — 〜のが

Say which activity someone likes or does well.

Dictionary form + のが + 好すき・上手じょうず・下手へた, etc.

The whole action phrase comes before のが. The particle is determined by the following expression: 好すき and 上手じょうず commonly take が. This pattern describes liking or skill, not a request to do the action.

私わたしは料理りょうりを作つくるのが好すきです。

I like cooking.

姉あねは人ひとの名前なまえを覚おぼえるのが上手じょうずです。

My older sister is good at remembering people’s names.

04

An Action as the Object — 〜のを

Forget to do something or know a fact.

Dictionary form + のを忘わすれる; plain clause + のを知しっていますか

の makes the action or fact into the object, and を marks that object. In the forgetting pattern, the dictionary form commonly describes something you failed to do. With “know,” retain the tense of the fact: 帰かえったのを知しっていますか.

薬くすりを飲のむのを忘わすれました。

I forgot to take my medicine.

田中たなかさんが引ひっ越こしたのを知しっていますか。

Did you know that Tanaka has moved?

05

Identify the Missing Information — 〜のは…です

Specify who, when, where, or what.

Plain clause + のは + noun + です; noun / な-adjective: な + のは

This construction highlights the information that follows は. It can identify a time, place, person, or reason. It is more focused than simply stating the same facts in a basic sentence.

初はじめて日本にほんへ来きたのは、3年前ねんまえです。

It was three years ago that I first came to Japan.

この店みせで有名ゆうめいなのは、チーズケーキです。

What this shop is famous for is its cheesecake.

06

Lesson Focus

Keep the meaning, connection, and viewpoint clear.

Points to remember

Start with の ≈ こと: 泳およぐの / 泳およぐこと both name the activity “swimming.” の makes the noun phrase; the following は・が・を marks its role. Use this equivalence for the activity patterns explained here, not as a universal replacement rule.

The の in this lesson makes an action or clause function as a noun phrase. It does not mean “of” in every case. Before this の, a present affirmative noun or な-adjective needs な; a verb or い-adjective does not.
