SAKURA JAPANESE · N4

Lesson 36

Describe goals, changes in ability and habits, and efforts to do something regularly.

01

A Goal — 〜ように

Act so that a desired result becomes possible.

Dictionary form / ない-form + ように、action

The first clause often uses a potential verb or a verb describing a result rather than a deliberate action: 話はなせる, 分わかる, 忘わすれない. The second clause says what you do to achieve or avoid that result. Compare deliberate purpose 〜ために in Lesson 42.

日本語にほんごで話はなせるように、毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうしています。

I practise every day so that I can speak Japanese.

忘わすれないように、メモしてください。

Please make a note so that you will not forget.

02

A Change — 〜ようになりました / 〜なくなりました

Start doing / become able to do — stop doing / become unable to do.

Affirmative change: dictionary form + ようになりました

Use an ordinary dictionary-form verb for a new habit or behaviour: 食たべるようになりました = “started eating regularly.” Use the dictionary form of a potential verb for a new ability: 読よめるようになりました = “became able to read.” Both describe a change from before, rather than simply a present fact.

漢字かんじが少すこし読よめるようになりました。

I have become able to read some kanji.

最近さいきん、朝あさご飯はんを食たべるようになりました。

Recently, I have started eating breakfast regularly.

Negative change: ない → なくなりました

For “no longer do” or “can no longer do,” start with the appropriate ない-form and replace the final い with くなりました: ない → なくなりました. This describes a change from doing something to not doing it, or from being able to being unable to do it.

Change in habit: 食たべる → 食たべない → 食たべなくなりました (“no longer eat”).
Loss of ability: 食たべられる → 食たべられない → 食たべられなくなりました (“can no longer eat”). Use the negative potential form when the meaning is about ability.

Other examples: 行いかない → 行いかなくなりました (“no longer go”); 行いけない → 行いけなくなりました (“can no longer go”); できない → できなくなりました (“can no longer do”).

No longer do — a changed habit

以前いぜんは毎日まいにちテレビを見みていましたが、最近さいきんは見みなくなりました。

I used to watch television every day, but recently I have stopped watching it.

Can no longer do — a lost ability

目めが悪わるくなって、眼鏡めがねがないと小ちいさい字じが読よめなくなりました。

My eyesight has got worse, and I can no longer read small print without glasses.

“No longer…” is different from “did not become…”

読よめなくなりました means “I used to be able to read it, but now I cannot.” 読よめるようになりませんでした means “I did not become able to read it.” Negating なりました does not express the same change.

食たべなくなりました describes the resulting change in habit. 食たべないようにしています describes an ongoing effort not to eat it. These can describe related situations, but their focus is different.

03

Make an Effort — 〜ようにしています

Try to maintain a habit.

Dictionary form / ない-form + ようにしています

This expresses an ongoing effort to do or avoid something. 〜ようにしてください asks someone to make a point of doing or avoiding it. It often concerns a habit or precaution, rather than a request for one immediate action.

毎日まいにち、野菜やさいを食たべるようにしています。

I make a point of eating vegetables every day.

夜よる遅おそくコーヒーを飲のまないようにしてください。

Please try not to drink coffee late at night.

04

Lesson Focus

Keep the meaning, connection, and viewpoint clear.

Points to remember

Purpose 〜ように often focuses on an ability or a result you want to make possible. 〜ようになりました describes a change; 〜ようにしています describes a continuing effort. Keep these different from appearance 〜ようです in Lesson 47.

Positive change: 読よめるようになりました / 食たべるようになりました.
Negative change: 読よめなくなりました / 食たべなくなりました. Match the ordinary or potential verb to whether you mean a habit or an ability.
