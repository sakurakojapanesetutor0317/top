SAKURA JAPANESE · N4

Lesson 33

Understand commands and signs, explain meanings, and pass on messages.

01

Imperative Form — Commands

Understand both the form and how strong it sounds.

An instruction that leaves little room for choice

The imperative tells someone strongly to act: “Stop!” / “Hurry up!” It can sound forceful or rough in ordinary conversation. You may hear it in emergencies, military-style instructions, sports coaching or cheering, arguments, and quoted speech. It is not a neutral replacement for 〜てください.

For everyday requests, choose 〜てください or a suitable question such as 〜ていただけませんか. A sign or an emergency can use a short imperative because the instruction needs to be immediate.

How to form it

Group 1: change the final dictionary-form う-row sound to the corresponding え-row sound. No extra ending is added. Group 2: replace final る with ろ. Group 3: learn しろ and 来こい separately.

A verb ending in る is not automatically Group 2: 帰かえる is Group 1, so its imperative is 帰かえれ; 食たべる is Group 2, so its imperative is 食たべろ.

GroupDictionary formImperative
1 — う → え買かう買かえ
1 — く → け / ぐ → げ書かく / 泳およぐ書かけ / 泳およげ
1 — す → せ / つ → て話はなす / 待まつ話はなせ / 待まて
1 — ぬ → ね / ぶ → べ死しぬ / 遊あそぶ死しね / 遊あそべ
1 — む → め / る → れ読よむ / 帰かえる読よめ / 帰かえれ
2 — る → ろ食たべる / 見みる食たべろ / 見みろ
3 — irregularする / 来くるしろ / 来こい

A formation example is not a recommendation

The table includes 死しぬ → 死しね to show the ぬ → ね change. Directed at a person, 死しね means “Die!” and is extremely abusive; do not use it as an instruction to someone.

危あぶない！ 止とまれ！

Watch out! Stop! — An urgent warning.

がんばれ！ あと少すこし！

Come on! Just a little more! — Cheering someone on.

02

Prohibition — Dictionary Form + な

A strong “Do not…!” uses the same pattern for every group.

Add な to the complete dictionary form

Do not change the verb into its ない-form or its imperative. Add な directly to the dictionary form: 入はいる → 入はいるな; 食たべる → 食たべるな; する → するな; 来くる → 来くるな.

This prohibition is strong, like an imperative. Signs, urgent warnings, and quoted commands are common contexts. Use 〜ないでください or another suitable request in ordinary conversation.

MeaningStrong command / prohibitionPolite request
Stop.止とまれ。止とまってください。
Do not enter.入はいるな。入はいらないでください。
Do not touch.触さわるな。触さわらないでください。

Watch the form before な

食たべるな means “Do not eat.” 食たべなさい means “Eat,” as an instruction often given by a parent or teacher. In informal speech, shortened 食たべな can also mean “Go ahead and eat.” The presence of な alone does not make every expression a prohibition.

ここに車くるまを止とめるな。

Do not park here. — Strong wording, for example on a sign.

その箱はこに触さわるな！ 危あぶない！

Do not touch that box! It is dangerous! — An urgent warning.

03

Signs — Writing, Reading & Meaning

These are three different questions.

What does it say? How is it read? What does it mean?

何なんと書かいてありますか asks what words are written. 何なんと読よみますか asks how written characters are read. どういう意味いみですか asks for the meaning. A pronunciation alone does not explain the meaning.

と marks the quoted wording in 「…」と書かいてあります and 「…」と読よみます. 書かいてあります describes an existing written result, such as the text on a sign, not someone writing right now.

「何なんと書かいてありますか。」「『入口いりぐち』と書かいてあります。」

“What does it say?” — “It says ‘entrance.’”

「これは何なんと読よみますか。」「『いりぐち』と読よみます。」

“How do you read this?” — “It is read ‘iriguchi.’”

Explain a meaning with 〜という意味いみです

Use word / expression は + explanation + という意味いみです. A verb explanation uses the plain form: 止とまれ → 止とまれという意味いみです; 入はいってはいけません → 入はいってはいけないという意味いみです. Here the command is the content being explained; you are not personally ordering the listener.

For a noun or な-adjective explanation, retain the plain だ: 無料むりょうだという意味いみです. Do not put the polite ending です or ます inside an ordinary paraphrased explanation. Exact quotations of wording can keep their original polite form.

「立入禁止たちいりきんし」は、ここに入はいってはいけないという意味いみです。

“No entry” means that you must not enter here.

「使用中しようちゅう」は、だれかが今いま使つかっているという意味いみです。

“In use” means that someone is using it now.

04

Report Speech — と言いっていました

Tell someone what another person said earlier.

Speaker は + message in plain form + と言いっていました

と marks the message, and 言いっていました reports an earlier statement. In this reporting use, the English is often simply “said”; it does not necessarily mean that the person kept repeating it.

Convert the message to a plain form when paraphrasing: 行いきます → 行いく; 行いきません → 行いかない; 行いきました → 行いった; 忙いそがしいです → 忙いそがしい; 元気げんきです → 元気げんきだ; 学生がくせいです → 学生がくせいだ.

Do not automatically change the message to past tense just because 言いっていました is past. Preserve the time relationship intended in the message. 来くると言いっていました means “said they would come”; 来きたと言いっていました means “said they had come.”

田中たなかさん：「明日あしたは休やすみます。」
→ 田中たなかさんは、明日あしたは休やすむと言いっていました。

Tanaka: “I will take tomorrow off.” → Tanaka said they would take tomorrow off. — Reported on the same day.

田中たなかさん：「週末しゅうまつは暇ひまです。」
→ 田中たなかさんは、週末しゅうまつは暇ひまだと言いっていました。

Tanaka: “I am free this weekend.” → Tanaka said they were free this weekend.

Whose “I,” “here,” and “tomorrow”?

When paraphrasing, update references so the listener understands them. If you report “tomorrow” on the following day, it may now be 今日きょう. Omit an obvious subject or identify the original speaker; blindly repeating 私わたし can make the message sound as though it is about you.

Japanese 行いく / 来くる must fit the reporting viewpoint too. Do not translate English “come” mechanically. For example, a person abroad telling someone in Japan about their own trip normally says 日本にほんに行いきます.

と言いいました vs. と言いっていました

Both can report a statement. と言いいました presents the act of saying it; と言いっていました is especially common when passing on something you heard someone say. This is a useful reporting tendency, not an absolute division. An exact quote inside 「…」 can retain the original です / ます wording.

05

Ask Someone to Relay a Message

The listener passes your message to a third person.

Recipient に + plain message + と伝つたえていただけませんか

This means “Could you please tell [recipient] that…?” The person you are speaking to is the messenger; the person marked with に is the recipient. The content before と is the message.

For example, you speak to a receptionist and ask them to tell the teacher you will be late. If you are speaking directly to the teacher, there is no need to ask the teacher to relay the same message to themselves.

いただけませんか makes the request polite. You can say it as a complete chunk here: 伝つたえて + いただけませんか. It does not make the quoted content polite automatically; a paraphrased message still uses plain forms.

すみません。先生せんせいに、今日きょうは10分ぷんほど遅おくれると伝つたえていただけませんか。

Excuse me. Could you tell the teacher that I will be about ten minutes late today? — Said to someone who can pass on the message.

田中たなかさんに、会議かいぎは3時じからだと伝つたえていただけませんか。

Could you tell Tanaka that the meeting starts at three? — Retain だ after the noun phrase.

A complete exchange

Caller: 田中たなかさんはいらっしゃいますか。
Receptionist: 田中たなかはただ今いま、席せきを外はずしております。
Caller: では、あとでまた電話でんわすると伝つたえていただけませんか。
Receptionist: はい、伝つたえます。

“Is Tanaka available?” — “Tanaka is away from their desk at the moment.” — “Then could you tell them I will call again later?” — “Yes, I will pass that on.” The caller’s message is I will call again later. The receptionist is being asked to relay it, not to make the call.

06

Lesson Focus

Keep the meaning, connection, and viewpoint clear.

Separate the form from the function

Command: 行いけ。 / Prohibition: 行いくな。 / Polite negative request: 行いかないでください。

Reading: how the characters sound. / Meaning: what the expression communicates. / Quotation: the words or message marked by と.

For ordinary reported content, use plain forms and keep だ with nouns and な-adjectives. Reporting an order does not mean you are ordering the current listener to do it.
