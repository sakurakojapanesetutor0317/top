SAKURA JAPANESE · N4

Lesson 29

Describe the state left by a change, say that something is completely finished, and talk about unwanted events.

01

A Resulting State — 〜ています

Describe how something is now, after a change.

Noun + が + intransitive て-form + います

With a verb that describes a change, 〜ています can describe the state that remains after that change. It does not always describe an action happening now.

窓まどが開あきました means “The window opened.” 窓まどが開あいています means “The window is open.” The opening has already happened; the open state remains.

Use が when reporting what you notice. When the thing is already the topic, は is also natural: この窓まどは壊こわれています — “This window is broken.”

窓まどが開あいています。

The window is open.

電気でんきがついています。

The light is on.

この椅子いすは壊こわれています。

This chair is broken.

袋ふくろが破やぶれています。

The bag is torn.

02

Intransitive & Transitive Verbs

Choose whether to focus on the thing or on someone acting on it.

The verb and particle work together

An intransitive verb describes what happens to something: 窓まどが開あきます — “The window opens.” A transitive verb describes someone acting on an object: 窓まどを開あけます — “Someone opens the window.”

For the resulting state in this lesson, use the intransitive verb: 窓まどが開あいています. Changing が to を is not enough; the verb also changes. Learn pairs such as 開あきます / 開あけます、閉しまります / 閉しめます、壊こわれます / 壊こわします together.

A person doing an action

田中たなかさんが窓まどを開あけています。

Tanaka is opening the window.

The state of the thing

窓まどが開あいています。

The window is open.

03

Completely Finished — 〜てしまいます

Emphasize that an action will be or has been completed.

て-form + しまいます / しまいました

〜てしまいます can mean doing something completely or finishing it off. Use 〜てしまいました for something already completed. This use is not necessarily negative: finishing a book or some work can be intentional and welcome.

この本ほんは全部ぜんぶ読よんでしまいました。

I have finished reading this whole book.

この仕事しごとは今日中きょうじゅうにやってしまいます。

I will finish off this work by the end of today.

04

An Unwanted Event — 〜てしまいました

Express regret, disappointment, or an unfortunate result.

The same form can add the speaker’s feelings

〜てしまいました also describes an unwanted event or result. Depending on the context, English may use “unfortunately,” “accidentally,” or “ended up…”. It does not always mean that the action was accidental.

The form alone does not tell you which meaning is intended. Look at the event and the surrounding situation: finishing a book emphasizes completion, while losing a wallet normally suggests regret.

財布さいふを落おとしてしまいました。

Unfortunately, I lost my wallet.

電車でんしゃの中なかに傘かさを忘わすれてしまいました。

I left my umbrella on the train, unfortunately.

05

Lesson Focus

A present state and a completed event are different.

Choose what you want to report

窓まどが開あいています。 — “The window is open.” You are reporting the state now.

窓まどが開あきました。 — “The window opened.” You are reporting the change.

窓まどを開あけてしまいました。 — “I went and opened the window.” In a situation where it should have stayed shut, しまいました can convey regret. In another context, it can emphasize completing the action.

In conversation, 〜てしまう → 〜ちゃう and 〜でしまう → 〜じゃう: 忘わすれちゃった・飲のんじゃった. Recognize these casual forms, but practice the full polite form first.
