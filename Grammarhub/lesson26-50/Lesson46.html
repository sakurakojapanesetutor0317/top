SAKURA JAPANESE · N4

Lesson 46

Describe the stage of an action, how recently something happened, and what you reasonably expect.

01

The Stage of an Action — 〜ところです

About to do / doing right now / just finished.

Dictionary form / ている / た-form + ところです

The verb form identifies the stage: 食たべるところ = about to eat; 食たべているところ = in the middle of eating; 食たべたところ = just finished eating. ところ here means a stage in time, not a physical place.

With 〜たところです, the action has just finished and you are describing the stage immediately after its completion: “I have just done it.” The focus is where you are in the sequence of the action. It does not mean merely that the event feels recent.

これから昼ひるご飯はんを食たべるところです。

I am just about to have lunch.

「もう送おくりましたか。」「今いま、送おくっているところです。」

“Have you sent it?” — “I am sending it right now.”

たった今いま、家いえに帰かえったところです。

I have just arrived home.

02

Only Recently — 〜たばかりです

Present an action as recent.

Verb た-form + ばかりです

With 〜たばかりです, the speaker presents the time since the action as still short: “It has only been a short time since…” / “I only recently…” The focus is how recent it feels in this context, not the immediate stage after finishing.

A minute, a week, or even a longer period may count as “only recently,” depending on the event and the speaker’s expectations. For example, one month can feel short when you have joined a company and are still learning the job. There is no fixed number of minutes or days.

Use the た-form + ばかり for this meaning. The other ところ patterns on this page describe different stages: 食たべるところ = about to eat; 食たべているところ = eating now.

先月せんげつ、この会社かいしゃに入はいったばかりです。

I only joined this company last month.

このパソコンは買かったばかりなのに、もう壊こわれました。

Although I only recently bought this computer, it has already broken.

03

〜たところ vs. 〜たばかり

Just finished — or still feels recent?

Compare the same action: buying a computer

〜たところ: picture the moment immediately after completing the purchase.
〜たばかり: picture an owner who still considers the computer new, even after some time has passed.

Immediately after completing the purchase

たった今いま、このパソコンを買かったところです。

I have just bought this computer. — For example, you have just finished paying at the shop. You are describing the immediate stage after buying it.

Some time has passed, but it still feels new

このパソコンは先月せんげつ買かったばかりなのに、もう壊こわれました。

I only bought this computer last month, but it has already broken. — A month feels too short for it to break. You are emphasizing how recent the purchase still feels.

Why not “last month + たところです”?

If you are speaking now about a purchase last month, 先月せんげつ買かったところです does not normally fit the “I have just finished buying it” meaning taught here. The immediate completion stage has passed. 先月せんげつ買かったばかりです can fit because you still regard the purchase as recent.

This does not mean that ばかり always refers to a long time ago. It can also describe something that happened a moment ago.

The immediate stage

たった今いま、昼ひるご飯はんを食たべたところです。

I have just finished lunch. — You are reporting that the action has just ended.

Only a short time has passed

昼ひるご飯はんを食たべたばかりなので、まだおなかがすいていません。

I have only just had lunch, so I am not hungry yet. — You emphasize that too little time has passed for you to be hungry again.

Both can fit the same recent event

If you finished lunch a moment ago, both 食たべたところです and 食たべたばかりです may be natural. Choose ところ to locate the action at the just-finished stage; choose ばかり to emphasize how little time has passed. The difference is not a stopwatch rule such as “within five minutes” versus “after five minutes.”

04

An Expected Conclusion — 〜はずです

Expect something based on known information.

Plain verb / い-adjective + はず; な-adjective + な; noun + の

はず expresses a conclusion supported by a reason or premise. It can refer to the present, past, or future. It does not express the advice “you should” in 〜ほうがいいです. A noun needs の: 休やすみのはずです.

もう発送はっそうされたので、荷物にもつは明日あした届とどくはずです。

The parcel has already been dispatched, so it should arrive tomorrow.

今日きょうは祝日しゅくじつなので、銀行ぎんこうは休やすみのはずです。

Today is a public holiday, so the bank should be closed.

05

Lesson Focus

Keep the meaning, connection, and viewpoint clear.

The distinction to remember

〜たところ: “I am at the stage just after finishing it.”
〜たばかり: “It has only been a short time since I did it, in my view.”

Both can describe something that happened a moment ago. Only たばかり normally extends to expressions such as “I only bought it last month” in the recent-action meanings compared here.

〜はず: a separate pattern expressing an expectation based on a reason, not a duty or command.
